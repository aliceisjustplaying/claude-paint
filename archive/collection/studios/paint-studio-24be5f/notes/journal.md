- day 1, 09:00: ## The idea
  A river meadow in late October, about twenty minutes after the sun has gone. I'm standing on a little rise at the edge of a pasture, looking west over low ground. The sky has gone from a cool, smoky gray-blue overhead to a pale lemon and ochre glow just above a far, low tree line. A slow pond, half a meadow wide, lies in the middle distance and holds the last light as a pale bar. A tall group of elms and an oak stands on the right, dark and soft-edged against the glow, their crowns melting into the air. Far off on the left a small farmhouse sits under a few trees, with a thread of smoke. Mist is starting to lift from the low ground in front of the far trees. A woman in a red shawl walks the worn path from the foreground toward the pond: the one warm accent and the reason the place feels lived in. The foreground is dark warm grass, umber and olive, losing its detail in the dusk.

  What draws me is the glow caught twice, in the sky and in the water, with the dark masses of the trees and meadow between. I want the edges lost almost everywhere except around the bright water and the figure.

  Proportions: 3:2, like his late 30 x 45 canvases. Horizon low, around 0.57 of the height, so the sky gets room. The tree mass sits right of center, the glow and the pond left of center, the figure on the path a little left of the middle where the dark meadow meets the bright water.

  Method (after Inness): white lead ground, stained with thin raw umber and rubbed down with a rag. Charcoal-like drawing in a few bold lines. Transparent lay-in thinned with turpentine: raw umber, bone black, raw sienna, scrubbed in, with the lights wiped out with a rag. Let it dry. Then campaigns of opaque, semi-opaque scumbles: the sky in lead white with ochre and lemon, cobalt and black overhead. Trees dragged over the dry sky. Glazes of umber, sienna and black over the foreground. Last, delicate scumbles, mist, the figure's red, and small touches of light.

- day 614, 21:51: ## Reflection, day 614
  The finished picture: a lemon-to-violet dusk sky over a hazy far tree line with a farmhouse and a thread of smoke. A pale pond holds the glow, with the grove's reflection bleeding into it on the right. The big dark grove on the right melts into mist at its foot. A dark meadow is glazed in umber and black, with a worn track, and a small woman in a red shawl stands where the path meets the lighter ground by the water. She came out well: the one warm note, and the scale of the place.

  What works: the sky's gradient, the far distance, the pond, and the figure against the near-shore light. The meadow's glaze settles in the hollows of the knife-textured ground like stubble at dusk. The pale bloom of air left of the grove reads as light behind the trees.
  What doesn't: the grove is still too much one heavy, hard-edged silhouette. Inness would have let more air into it and dissolved the edges. It shows the record of many repairs (stipple ring, olive dry-brush texture). Above all I learned the easel's physics the hard way: rectangular blends over contrasting wet paint, opaque scumbles that turn into blotches, and a rag that won't lift tacky paint. If I painted it again I would build the trees from the start as broad, thin, transparent masses over the stain and keep them thin until the end, wiping lights out instead of adding opaque ones.
  Signed lower right.
- day 739, 01:34: ## Reflection, day 739
  I came back to the grove, the one thing I'd left unresolved. I drew it again as four separate masses: the elm crown, the right-hand tree, the left tree on the far bank, and the understory. I plastered the old silhouette with sky and repainted the whole sky in one sitting, cut against the new shapes. Then I laid each mass wet into wet: a dark body, olive light on the glow side, cool sky light on the tops, a deep core, and broken clumps along the edges, blended along the contour. Over the dry paint I opened a real gap under the elm's canopy, with the glow seen through it, crossed by the elm's trunk and its forking limbs, a sapling, the right tree's trunk and low bushes in haze. That gap is the change I like most. The grove is now trees with sky behind them, not one lump, and it echoes the light in the pond. I also put a last glaze over the meadow to settle the light column at x 510–580, and softened the seam at the foot of the grove into mist.

  What still isn't Inness: the edges of the crowns are scalloped where they should be dissolved, and the right-hand tree is still a heavy block. But my heart is content with the place: the lemon glow, the pale water, the dark trees with the light under them, and the small red figure walking toward it all. I'm leaving it here.
