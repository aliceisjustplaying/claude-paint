## What changed across the complete record

**Sitting 1 established the geometry early and repaired it repeatedly.** The light band already resembled a road in R72; it remained the same dominant object after hundreds of later actions. Global blending dragged wet blue across dry pads and sky, and the painter correctly diagnosed it. Later cream-band blending and a willow blend again buried or dragged nearby pads. “Only blend fresh paint” was learned in words but not reliably enforced by the chosen regions. [Early image A](../../../paint-studio-12e572/out/easel/painting/cb9a71af-d82d-4e1d-99e0-6708a59bc64a.png), S1:R192–199, R283–300 and R552–566; [journal](../../../paint-studio-12e572/notes/journal.md:3), lines 4 and 13.

The white-heavy water campaign did lose color distinctions and become a pale dab carpet. Its rag/knife rescue changed little. Later flat lavender slabs lifted much better and three-point filbert gestures improved those edges. That supports the earlier observations **under these conditions**, not universal rules about white thresholds or rag lifting: one of the four water piles actually used only 0.5 parts white, and thickness, medium and drying state differed. [Lua mixtures and campaign](../../../paint-studio-12e572/paintings/lua/painting.lua:461), S1:R355–375 and R399–421; images D–H below.

**Sitting 2 did more than repeat sitting 1.** After the earlier review's cutoff at R84, it lifted the milky veil, strengthened the upper cloud colors and worked both boundaries. The S2 whole view shows a more varied blue/lavender/rose sky than S1. The handoff's four named passages gave it concrete targets. But boundary blending created another rectangular passage; a pad covered an existing flower; blending inside an ellipse recreated a flat green disc. Each was lifted and repainted. These are repeated planning and placement failures after the relevant lessons had been recorded. [Journal](../../../paint-studio-12e572/notes/journal.md:25), S2:R86–159, R176–192 and R209–242; [S2 whole image](../../../paint-studio-12e572/out/easel/painting/a15f2e33-f432-4e7f-9288-a007cb72c4e7.png), images L–N below.

**Sitting 3 modestly softened some lavender pills, then accepted the rest.** The first glaze made saturated violet bars, repeating the day-43 failure. A rag lifted them; a second recipe with more medium, lower load and partial coverage gave a lighter overlay. The painter accepted the ochre, lower diagonal boundary and regular right-side reflections as evening warmth, a foliage bank and reeds. Those are explicit aesthetic stopping decisions, not evidence that the underlying shapes disappeared. [Journal](../../../paint-studio-12e572/notes/journal.md:32), S3:R27–68; images O–Q below.

Two self-reported lessons need correction:

- **“Hand-placed helpers beat `work`” is too broad.** The replacement helper generated 123 and 137 gestures using loops and mask-edge scans; pad and flower helpers repeated stacked rows and petal templates. Some local control improved, but the resulting repetition remained. A helper-driven left-water campaign also became blurry and blobby. [Vertical helper](../../../paint-studio-12e572/paintings/lua/painting.lua:551), [raft template](../../../paint-studio-12e572/paintings/lua/painting.lua:659), [flower template](../../../paint-studio-12e572/paintings/lua/painting.lua:786), S1:R529–534.
- **“Glaze needs ≤0.6 pigment parts” is mathematically unsupported.** Tube parts normalize to fractions summing to one; uniformly shrinking all recipe numbers does not dilute paint. The successful retry simultaneously changed pigments, ratios, medium, load and coverage, then used a rag. Medium really does dilute the simulated pigment, but the record cannot isolate which changes caused the improvement or establish a universal threshold. ## Visual judgment

These are judgments from the images, rather than engine errors or claims of physical accuracy. The painter's own plan was a downward view of continuous pond water with no horizon. The final painting instead reads strongly as a pale corridor between two tall banks. The central band's straight upper walls and lower diagonal survive local softening. Horizontal rafts divide it into tiers. Long vertical reflection strokes repeat against similarly sized rounded horizontal pad strokes; the sharp flowers read as little symbols placed over softer water. Their edge and detail contrast makes the pads feel detached. The lower half has many equally emphatic clusters and limited variation in touch. [Initial plan](../../../paint-studio-12e572/notes/journal.md:1), [final painter view](../../../paint-studio-12e572/out/easel/painting/42cdb01d-5771-42ea-8321-55181bceee46.png), [foreground detail](../../../paint-studio-12e572/out/easel/painting/d468e5af-9993-4445-bb0d-37502f438bdf.png).

There are gains worth preserving: coherent warm/cool color, the dark left mass, increased sky variation in S2 and looser small pad groups. My hypothesis is that repeated local campaigns optimized recognizable symbols and smoothness while leaving the large composition unchallenged. The notes' progression from “road” and “enamel lozenges” to accepted “river of sky” and reflected reeds supports that interpretation, but does not prove why the model chose it. ## Proposed second experiment — not launched

**Fix prerequisites separately:** make the runner recognize all current canvas-marking methods and helpers, including gestures and rag operations, so it requires a real no-paint confirmation sitting. Reject extra `pile` arguments instead of silently discarding them. The first affects when the experiment ends; the second makes recipe intent auditable. Neither warrants changing the paint physics based on this run alone. [Classifier](runner/painting_chunks.py:20), [pile binding](../../../claude-paint-r26run/crates/easel/src/api.rs:1752).

**Recommended prompt amendment**, added to Working while retaining artistic autonomy:

> After the first large masses and after each major campaign, inspect the whole picture in normal color. Judge its large shapes, focal emphasis and variation of marks against the effect you intend. Record what still reads as an unintended object or repeated template. When two repairs leave the same problem, reconsider the composition or method before repeating the repair.
>
> Before repeating an unfamiliar mixture or stroke across a passage, inspect a small trial and check the existing marks in a crop. Preserve passages you intend to keep, allowing for the full stroke width. Blend only where moving fresh paint is intentional. In your journal distinguish recipe ratios from medium and brush load; treat a successful combination as an observation, not a universal threshold.

This targets the observed composition lock, bulk repetition before inspection, accidental overpainting and misleading material lessons. It does not dictate subject, coordinates or a particular pleasing result. Retain the concrete unresolved-passage handoffs; those supported the actual S2 improvements.
