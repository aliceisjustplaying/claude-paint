# Research: how Friedrich actually painted (worktree ~/src/a/claude-paint-r7-process, branch r7-process)

Alice: "when you research Friedrich, really focus on how he painted. This
is why I keep mentioning entropy. The less entropy we have, the more
digital everything looks." Another agent is researching his composition
and motifs (branch r7-friedrich); your subject is only his **process and
hand**, from written sources (conservation and technical studies, museum
technical bulletins, art-historical accounts of his studio practice,
contemporaries' descriptions; cite everything; text only: never look at or
describe images of paintings).

Start from what's already known: notes/research/friedrich_materials.md and
notes/friedrich.md (don't repeat it; deepen and correct it). Find out, as
concretely as the sources allow:
- **The sequence**: ground and imprimatura, the underdrawing (how he
  transferred and how much), dead color / underpainting, how many layers,
  where he glazed and where he painted body color, when he let layers dry,
  retouching and changes (pentimenti), final details, varnish.
- **The hand**: brushes (sizes, hair, shapes), stroke direction and length
  in skies, water, foliage, rock; stippling or hatching; how smooth or
  visible the brushwork is up close; how he painted fine things (twigs,
  grass, figures); how edges were made (lost, found, overlapping layers).
- **Pace and conditions**: how long a painting took, sittings, working
  from studies (sketches in pencil and sepia from nature, assembled in
  the studio), his bare studio and light, working distance.
- **Sources of irregularity** ("entropy") in his surfaces that technical
  studies document: uneven grounds, abrasion, layer thickness variation,
  mixing on the palette, retouchings, differences between passages, the
  way his thin paint shows the ground, craquelure types found in his work
  and why.
- **What imitations and restorations got wrong** if documented.
Then translate: for each finding, what it means for the simulator: is it
already possible with our physics and verbs (say how a painter would do
it), does it need a new physical capability or constraint (describe it,
in the spirit of notes/principles.md: physics and constraints, not
answers), or is it a painter's habit for the sketchbook.
Write notes/research/friedrich_process.md (sourced, organized by the
sequence), and a short notes/briefs/friedrich_hand.md: a one-page guide a
painter reads before painting (how he worked, in order; where the
irregularity comes from; principles, not recipes). Commit. Budget ~1.5–2 h.
Final message: concise report with the key findings and the list of
proposed capabilities.
