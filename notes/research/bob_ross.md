# Bob Ross: materials and technique for an oil-paint simulator

**Scope.** The report covers *The Joy of Painting* (Jan. 11, 1983 – May 17, 1994; 403 episodes) [W1] and current Bob Ross Inc. products. **Evidence tags:** **[doc]** = official or manufacturer document; **[tx]** = episode subtitle quote; **[3p]** = third-party source; **[est]** = my unsourced engineering estimate. The transcripts [TX] are auto-captions: "pressure and blue" means Prussian Blue and "Jesso" means gesso. Current paints are made by Golden Artist Colors [SDS]. The 1983–94 formulations may have differed, and I found no source naming the paint's maker during the show years (**uncertain**).

---

## 1. Support and preparation

- **Canvas:** "an 18 by 24 inch pre stretched double prime canvas" [tx: S31E07]. Bob Ross Inc. sells 18×24 canvases, "Double-primed and pre-stretched ... Made to meet Bob's exact requirements" [doc: CAN]. The official step project uses 16×20 [doc: STEP].
- **Variants:** Some episodes start from black or gray gesso applied with a foam brush and "let that dry completely" [tx: S31E13]. Contact paper or tape masks shapes (window, seashell, oval) before the base coat [tx: S30E07, S31E10].
- **Liquid White/Black/Clear:** "allow us to actually blend and mix colors right on the canvas ... [and] can also be used to thin other colors for application over thicker paints" [doc: LW]. "Liquid Black is just like liquid white only it has a black pigment in it" [tx: S31E09]. Liquid Clear gives "the necessary smooth and slippery surface" without "diluting the intensity of other colors, especially the darks" [doc: LC]. Early seasons used Bill Alexander's "Magic White" [W1].
- **Composition (partial):** The Liquid White/Black/Opal SDS lists hydrotreated heavy naphtha at **1–10%**, carbon black at 5–10% (Black only) and quartz at 0.1–<1%. It gives density **1.20–1.60**, a **62 °C** flash point and odor "Oil / Solvent." The firm colors have density **1.35–2.00**, a flash point >99 °C, odor "Linseed Oil" and no reportable solvent [doc: SDS]. So Liquid White is an oil-based white thinned with oil and some solvent; the full recipe is unpublished. The home recipe of about 1:1 titanium white to linseed oil is **[3p, unverified]**.
- **Application:** "cover the entire canvas with a thin, even coat of Liquid White" using the 2" brush; "Do not allow the Liquid White to dry before you begin" [doc: STEP]. On air: "a very thin coat ... So it's wet and slick" [tx: S31E07] and "just enough to cover it" [tx: S31E10]. **No thickness figure is published.**
- **Effect on mixing:** Brushing over the wet base "will allow you to blend the color right on the canvas and it also removes excess paint. So the next layer will be much easier to apply" [tx: S31E09]. Colors laid into Liquid White are lightened by it, which is why Liquid Clear exists [doc: LC].

**Palette.** The Master Paint Set holds "firm colors, 37ml each": Bright Red, Phthalo Blue, Midnight Black, Van Dyke Brown, Cadmium Yellow, Sap Green, Titanium White and Alizarin Crimson [doc: MPS]. Golden's SDS lists 14 "Bob Ross® firm oil paint" colors, the table below [doc: SDS].

**Consistency:** The product copy says "a smooth yet firm consistency necessary to the Bob Ross Wet-on-Wet Technique®" [doc: TW]. Ross said: "Our oils are unique. They're very dry. They're very, very firm" [tx: S30E01]. Golden also makes a separate Bob Ross "soft oil paint" line [doc: SDS]. **No viscosity or yield data are published** (SDS: "Viscosity: Not available").

**Thinning:** "the least little bit of paint thinner on the brush ... just enough to make it a little bit thinner than what's up on the canvas" [tx: S30E04]. For the liner brush: "It's almost running. It does run" [tx: S31E08]. Bob Ross Odorless Thinner is a hydrocarbon solvent with kinematic viscosity ≤ 20.5 mm²/s [doc: SDS].

## 3. Tools

The landscape brushes are "a blend of natural and synthetic bristles and shaped according to Bob's very precise specifications" [doc: 2IN]. The liner and the 2" Soft Blender are natural bristle [doc: LIN, BLD].

| Tool | Size | Use and handling |
|---|---|---|
| **2" Background** | head ≈50 mm wide, hair ≈63 mm long [3p: JK] | Covers large areas and "all of the effects that the 1" brush is used for" [doc: 2IN]. Tap-loading: "we loaded the top bristles ... the back of the brush is not hardly touching" [tx: S31E02]. Clouds: "tap ... one corner" [tx: S30E08], then "turn your brush on the side ... little circles" [tx: S31E11]. Mist: "tap the base of it, and then pull gently upward" with a clean, dry brush [tx: S29E13]; a wet brush "will dilute the paint and make it too soft" [tx: S31E09]. |
| **1" Landscape** | 1 in | basecoats, "clouds, skies, water, mountains, trees, bushes" [doc: 1IN]; criss-cross fog at mountain bases [doc: STEP] |
| **1" Oval** | | "evergreen trees and foothills and ... highlighting" [doc: OV] |
| **#6 Fan** | | "clouds, mountains, tree trunks, foothills, boats, soft grassy areas" [doc: F6]. Evergreens: "A lot of paint, both sides" [tx: S31E06]. Distant trees: "top corner" [tx: S18E01]. Grass: "long and flat strokes ... push it up ... back and forth like a Z" [tx: S31E11]. |
| **#3 Fan** | smaller | same effects "with more detail" [doc: F3] |
| **#6 Filbert** | | seascapes, trunks, detail [doc: FIL] |
| **Liner** | | Load with thin paint: "Turn the bristles ... Loads it full of paint. Bring it to a nice sharp point" [tx: S31E08]. Used for twigs and the signature [doc: STEP]. |
| **#10 knife** | blade ≈3¾ × 1¼ in [3p: JER] | "straight-edged blades ... larger and firmer than traditional painting knives"; "essential for creating the big, strong ... mountains" [doc: K10] |
| **#5 knife** | smaller | tight areas [doc: K5] |

**Knife loading ("a roll of paint"):** "pull it out, very flat ... And then cut across and get a little roll of paint" [tx: S31E05]; "a little roll that lives right out on the edge of the knife" [tx: S31E01].

**Knife deposit:**
- **Mountain body:** The dark body goes on firmly and the excess is scraped, but not too much: "if you scraped too much off ... you don't get enough contrast" [tx: S30E09].
- **Highlights:** "No pressure. Absolutely. No pressure. Just let the paint glide ... all these holes in it. That's what makes it look interesting" [tx: S31E09]. "You want that paint to break? Let it break" [tx: S18E06]. Solid coverage "looks sort of like you just iced the cake" [tx: S19E02].
- **Direction:** Highlight and shadow strokes follow the mountain's angles [tx: S18E06].
- **Scratching:** The knife point scratches in "sticks and twigs" [doc: STEP].

**Cleaning:** "Scrubbed the old brush and odorless paint thinner ... shake off the excess and beat the devil" [tx: S31E13]. He struck the brush against a box or trash can, sometimes the easel [W2]. The Smithsonian holds his 1983 stepladder easel and his palette, about 27×20 in [SI1, SI2].

## 4. Order of work

The official "Winter Majesty" steps [doc: STEP] show the canonical sequence:

1. Thin Liquid White with the 2" brush, left wet.
2. Sky: Alizarin criss-crossed as a low glow. Phthalo Blue plus Midnight Black above with "criss cross strokes ... without over blending." On air: "crisscross strokes. Go all the way across the top ... then begin blending downward" [tx: S31E09].
3. Water: the same colors with horizontal strokes.
4. Mountain: knife plus a dark mix (Prussian Blue, Midnight Black, Van Dyke Brown, Alizarin). Blend the body with the 1" brush and criss-cross the base into fog.
5. Highlights: a small roll of white on the lit side, with white + Phthalo Blue + base color on the shadow side. Tap the base and lift for mist.
6. Background evergreens: the base color plus a little white on the fan brush.
7. Reflections pulled down with the 1" brush, then the water line: "a very small roll of Titanium White and Liquid White on the knife." Ross: "pull it straight down ... if you make these lines go at an angle, your reflections just absolutely will not look right. Then we'll go across" [tx: S31E07].
8. Foreground evergreens and "push up" bushes in dark. Scratched twigs.
9. Highlights (white + Liquid White + a touch of blue), leaving "some dark areas." Wipe the brush when it picks up dark.
10. Sign with the liner.

**Timing:** Each show was a half-hour, shot in real time. Long paintings were partly prepared before taping. Ross made three versions of each painting: a reference, the on-air copy and a book copy [W1]. "~26 minutes" is not confirmed by any source I found (**uncertain**).

## 5. Why wet-on-wet works physically

- **Stated rule:** "The idea that a thin paint will stick to a thick paint is the basis for this entire technique" [doc: LW]. Ross: "The first layer of paint we put up here is very thick. Our oils ... are very, very firm. So by having a firm paint on the canvas, that we can take layer[s]" [tx: S30E01]. "if we hadn't started with a very thick, dry paint, this wouldn't work" [tx: S31E01].
- **Inference [est]:** Stiff paint holds its shape on the thin wet film. A slightly more fluid paint then transfers onto it by adhesion without shearing the lower layer. If the top paint is stiffer, the brush lifts the lower layer instead. Ross's fix is: "if you have trouble making it stick [add] a little bit of paint thinner" [tx: S31E13].
- **Pickup:** Brushes pick up wet color underneath: "Wipe the brush off if it picks up the dark color" [doc: STEP]. Colors also mix on the brush: "Mix them on the brush though. Tap, push" [tx: S31E13].
- **Layer order:** Dark before light and back before front. Broken light paint over dark lets the dark show through.

## 6. Motifs, composition and color schemes

**Frequencies** over 403 episodes, which I computed from FiveThirtyEight's data [538]:
- Trees: at least one 90%, two or more 84%, deciduous 56%, conifer 53%, bushes 30%
- Sky: clouds 44%, cumulus 21%, cirrus 7%
- Landforms: mountain 40%, snowy mountain 27%, grass 35%
- Water: lake 35%, river 31%, waterfall 10%, ocean 9%
- Snow and winter: snow 19%, winter 17%
- Structures and frames: cabin 17%, any frame 13%, oval frame 9%

**Habits:**
- Trees come in pairs: "Give him a friend" [tx: S31E06].
- Clouds are "light and fluffy on the top. And the bottom of it's basically flat" [tx: S31E11].
- Distant trees get little detail and some stay unhighlighted [tx: S18E01, S31E08].
- Mountains fade into mist at the base, and lakes get vertical reflections plus a water line.
- Vignettes use contact paper or tape; one episode is titled "Half-Oval Vignette" [PBS1].

**Color schemes:**
- **Skies:** Phthalo Blue over an Alizarin glow.
- **Mountains:** a dark mix with "quite a bit of Crimson ... with the blue, it'll give it sort of a lavender cast" [tx: S30E06].
- **Foliage:** a dark blue-black-brown-crimson base, highlighted with Sap Green plus Cadmium Yellow, Yellow Ochre or Indian Yellow, sometimes Crimson or Bright Red [tx: S31E13, S31E08].
- **Cabins:** Van Dyke Brown and Dark Sienna, highlighted by grazing a knife roll [tx: S31E12].

## Implications for a simulator

Every [est] value is a tunable guess; none is published.

1. **Rheology:**
   - Model paint as a yield-stress (Bingham or Herschel-Bulkley) fluid.
   - Firm colors: yield stress [est] 3–10× a soft tube oil.
   - Liquid basecoats: near-Newtonian and thin.
   - Thinner lowers yield stress, and liner paint is near zero.
   - Density: 1.35–2.0 (colors) and 1.2–1.6 g/cm³ (basecoats) [SDS].
2. **Deposit rule:** Transfer happens when tool-paint yield stress is at or below canvas-paint yield stress and contact pressure is low. Otherwise the tool picks up canvas paint. Mixing scales with shear and contact time.
3. **Liquid White:**
   - Thickness [est] 20–50 µm.
   - It stays wet all session, so no drying model is needed.
   - It lowers surface friction and adds white to each stroke.
   - Black adds carbon black; Clear has no pigment.
4. **Canvas:** 18×24 in (457×610 mm), non-absorbent and double-primed. Its weave peaks drive "break."
5. **Tools:**
   - 2" flat: 50×63 mm hair.
   - 1" flat: 25 mm.
   - #6 fan: [est] 30–35 mm. #3 fan: [est] ~20 mm.
   - Liner point: [est] 1–2 mm.
   - #10 knife: 95×32 mm straight edge.
   - Loading per side and per corner (a tap loads the top bristles only).
6. **Knife:**
   - The roll is a [est] 1–2 mm bead along the edge.
   - Body mode: high pressure, full deposit, partial scrape.
   - Highlight mode: near-zero force, so paint transfers only at texture and paint peaks. Aim for [est] 30–70% broken fill, with more gaps at lower pressure.
   - Point scratch removes paint along a line.
7. **Strokes by motif:**
   - Sky: rows of X strokes, then horizontal blending downward.
   - Clouds: corner taps and small circles with flat bottoms, then a dry upward blend.
   - Mountain: knife ridge, 1" body blend, tap-and-lift mist.
   - Evergreen: a center line, then fan dabs widening downward.
   - Bushes: corner push-ups.
   - Grass: a fan brush pushed up in a Z.
   - Reflections: a vertical pull, light horizontal passes and a knife water line.
   - Twigs: liner or knife scratch.
8. **Order:** Base coat → sky → water tone → mountains (dark → highlight → mist) → background trees → reflections and water line → middle ground → foreground darks → highlights → twigs → signature.
9. **Optics:**
   - PB15:3, PG7 and PR83 are transparent and high-tinting; PW6 is opaque.
   - Midnight Black is chromatic (PR83+PG7), not neutral.
