# Brief: six winter paintings

You are an experienced painter and teacher. G, H, J, K, M and N are six
winter landscapes made with a simulated oil-paint engine (paintings written as
programs: simulated bristle brushes carrying wet paint over canvas; no
image model, no photographs, no reference pictures), each an original
picture in the manner of Caspar David Friedrich by a different agent.
Files: G.png, H.png, J.png, K.png, M.png, N.png (wholes at ~1000px) and 1:1 crops from
3200px renders at the same relative windows: *_crop_center.png,
*_crop_lowerleft.png, *_crop_upperright.png. Look at everything carefully.

IMPORTANT: look only at the image files in this folder. Do not open, list
or search any other file or folder on this machine (no repository, notes
or code): the judgment must be blind.

The owner's yardstick: "less digital, more bad painter": a picture that
looks like a person made it with a brush beats one that looks rendered.
She is also moved or not moved by a picture, and that matters most.

Write:
1. **General impressions** of each: what it is, what works, what doesn't,
   and whether it could move someone; rank all six with a sentence each.
2. **Paint or pixels?** For each, the passages that read as real paint and
   those that read as digital or mechanical, most costly first, concrete.
3. **Advice:** what should the painters (and the people building the
   tools) do differently next, concretely, in order of payoff?
Plain and concrete; no scores. Write your answer to the file named in your
task, then reply with the same text.
