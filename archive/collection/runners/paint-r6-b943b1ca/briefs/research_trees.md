# Research: trees, for painters (a sourced note)

Worktree: ~/src/a/claude-paint-research-trees (branch research-trees).
Write ONE file: notes/research/trees.md. Commit it there; do not push.
Scratch: run `~/.local/bin/agent-tmp trees` once and export TMPDIR=TMP=TEMP.

## Why
AI painters in this project paint original pictures in the manner of
Caspar David Friedrich, working from knowledge only (never from pictures).
Their trees keep failing: twigs that float off their limbs, branching
"only trees in extreme distress do", straight "wire comb" pollard crowns,
trunks like cylinders. The one painter whose tree worked read sourced
research and invented its own method. The painters have a sourced note on
Friedrich's materials (read notes/research/friedrich_materials.md first:
match its style: terms explained, claims tied to sources with [KEY p.N],
a source list with URLs, honest about uncertainty). Nothing on trees.

## What the note should give a painter (knowledge, not recipes)
1. How the trees of Friedrich's world are built, in winter and in leaf:
   oak (especially old, stag-headed and dead oaks), beech, birch, lime,
   willow (including whether and how pollarded willows look: rods, their
   growth, how many, how they bend), alder, spruce and pine. Habit, how
   limbs leave the trunk, angles, how branching changes from trunk to twig,
   how twigs end, bark by species and age, how old and damaged trees look.
2. How branches taper and join (e.g. the "Leonardo" area rule and the pipe
   model; what a junction looks like; why a side branch never starts
   thinner than it will be a little farther out). Botany and tree
   architecture sources, not art tips.
3. How snow and hoarfrost lie on bare trees (which surfaces hold snow, how
   it varies with branch angle and thickness, what falls off).
4. How Friedrich studied and used trees, from text sources: his dated
   pencil tree studies from nature, reused years later in studio
   paintings; which paintings feature which trees (e.g. The Solitary Tree,
   The Abbey in the Oakwood, Two Men Contemplating the Moon, winter
   pictures); what scholars and conservators say about how he drew and
   painted trees (pencil underdrawing of trees, the order of painting,
   brushwork of twigs). Did he paint pollard willows? Say what the sources
   say, and say plainly if you can't find evidence either way.

## Rules
- Text sources only. Do NOT view or describe images of his works (the
  painters must work from knowledge; a note built from looking at his
  pictures would smuggle pictures in). Museum catalogue text, scholarly
  articles, conservation reports, botany and forestry references.
- Every factual claim carries a source key. Mark general botanical
  knowledge you couldn't source as such. No invented citations.
- Knowledge, not instructions: describe how trees are and how he worked;
  don't write painting recipes or API advice.
- Length: as long as useful, but dense (roughly 200-350 lines).
- US English, no Oxford comma. Never write the user's real name or an
  absolute home path into the file.

Final reply: a short summary of what the note covers, the pollard answer
and the number of sources.
