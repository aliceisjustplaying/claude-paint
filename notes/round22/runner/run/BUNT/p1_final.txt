**Quinces, Raking Light**

A low-key still life: a dark green-black glass bottle at the back left, a wide dark glazed bowl holding two quinces, and two more quinces on a warm brown table, the light coming in from the upper left and dying away into shadow at the right. The four quinces are the only light note in the picture — mottled olive-gold with dark speckling and a dry calyx at the crown — and the two in the bowl sit a step darker than the two in front so the bowl reads further back. The bowl itself is almost pure black: it is drawn by a single bright warm line along its near lip, which crosses in front of the fruit, and a thin edge light down its front-left; the bottle is held together by a green-grey turn, two broken white glints and a warm bounce off the table.

What I know is still not right, and would be the first thing I attack with another day: the wall and the table are grained but even, and Tonn's fields are built of visible standing strokes — mine are laid down and smoothed — and the fruit mottling runs a shade too close to camouflage in close-up.
