- day 1, 09:00: Plan: 'Evening at the Frozen Mere'. Winter dusk after sunset, low horizon (~y 468 of 714). Sky ~2/3: cool gray-blue at top through pale greenish to a warm rose-amber band at the horizon; thin crescent moon upper right. A stag-headed old oak on a snowy knoll left of centre, dead limbs standing above a live lower crown, snow along the tops of limbs, crows. Far off a pale line of woods with a faint Gothic church. A frozen mere in the middle distance catching the sky, reeds at its banks, three small snow-laden spruces at right, one small dark figure with a staff walking toward the church. Foreground snow with blue shadows, dead grass, stones, a fallen branch. Ground: warm red-ochre knifed layer, a patchy lead-white brushed top layer (as KOR p.284). Method after Friedrich: pencil drawing, thin smooth sky blended wet, then thin layers, details last over dry paint.
- day 3, 02:25: Sky laid in five wet horizontal bands (smalt/white at top, greenish-cream, a rose-amber glow at the horizon), first glow band too orange so I laid a paler band into it and blended twice; after drying a smalt glaze ramped in load from the top deepened the upper sky and left soft wisps. Distant woods and a ghostly church in pale violet-gray over the dry sky. Snow lay-in in bands, but the whole picture is now far too close in value: the snow matches the sky. Next layer must bring the land down, cooler and darker toward the bottom, and give the knoll form.
- day 7, 10:23: Oak: drew the trunk and six boughs by hand as ribbons (two dead ones, L3 and L4, in paler grey dead wood standing up the middle), spread the crotch so the trunk doesn't end in a knob, then grew the smaller branches from the boughs by oak habit: kinked at each node, forks clustered at tips, widths by the area rule, painted with a pointed round and a rigger at pressures for those widths. Added drooping lower limbs under the big side boughs and about 3200 fine twiglet strokes at the twig ends. Reads as an oak now. Problems: the trunk ribbon's end disc hangs below the knoll surface like a bulb; the dead limbs look too pale and beige; the knoll has no clear edge. Waiting for the dark to set before snow over the trunk foot.
- day 14, 18:24: Mistake: the hazy crown strokes along the distant woods ran across the dark spruces as pale grass-like lines (I forgot to keep them off the spruce mask). Will paint them out with the spruce pile once dry. Knoll restated with a crisp crest against the glow and a cool shaded face; this cut the oak's foot off square, needs root flares and a drift. The mere took a smalt glaze ramped heavier toward the near shore: now reads as ice but is a hard lens, too cyan; needs banks, snow on the ice, reeds.
- day 16, 07:34: Figure: a small walker from behind in a dark coat and a low hat with a staff, 27 units tall, on the path left of the mere, going toward the church. Mere banks: a thin dark lip under the far bank, snow lobes pushed over the near edge (came out as too bright a rim; blended, will glaze down).
- day 20, 09:19: Whole view, day 20: oak is the strongest passage now (darkened trunk with a glaze, fissures and pale ridges, a lightning scar of bare wood running from the dead leader down into the trunk, shaded dead limbs, small snow cushions on the limb tops - subtle against the light sky, which is right for backlight). The mere is the weakest: a cyan dish with a white rim, like a basin. It needs an irregular shore, reeds, snow on the ice, a greyer colour. The land is empty and bland: needs undulation, the path with footprints, grasses, stones, a fence.
- day 27, 10:23: Snow drifts on the ice went down as fat white floes (too many, too opaque). Blended three times while wet: now the whole mere is a pale veil, a snow-covered pond with the ellipse outline still showing. Plan: once dry, glaze back dark wind-cleared ice in long horizontal patches, leaving pale drift streaks between; then reeds to break the outline.
- day 29, 11:55: Day 29-30: dark ice glazed in as horizontal patches (came out as graphic stripes), blended wet into one slate-violet ice with faint streaks - better than any state before. Reed beds at both ends and sparse reeds on the far shore (too golden, wheat-like; to be glazed down). Path laid from the walker down through the gap in the bank; the blend dragged the wet reeds into it - repaint reeds over. Young moon, lit from below right, with a faint earthshine; a second smalt-violet glaze on the upper sky, masked round the moon, deepened the zenith and made the moon read.
- day 32, 14:00: Snow laid on the spruce tiers (zebra stripes up close, but at full view they read as snow-laden spruces). Crows: three flying in toward the oak, two perched (the one on the dead leader landed on the limb's face and reads as a knot). Grass: first scattered evenly (a sprinkle, as the studio notes warned), then clumped into dense stubble in the foreground corners, intermittent along the bank crest, by the reeds. It is all too golden for dusk; a cool glaze over the foreground should dull the grass and darken the snow together.
- day 39, 16:16: Day 39: a cool smalt glaze over the near ground (light; the grass is still golden). Footprints stepped down the path, a soft shaded wall on its left. Spruces: the zebra snow calmed with a dark transparent glaze, heavier toward the flanks - now they read as dense spruce with snow caught in the tiers. Dead limbs of the oak glazed down with umber-smalt and shaded on the right flank: silvery weathered wood now, not paper. A few clusters of withered leaves on the lower twigs, very small.
- day 46, 17:05: Day 43-46: mere broken at last - snow tongues blown out onto the ice from both shores, thin wind-streaks of snow lying on it, cool hollows in the near rim. An old fence runs out of the near right toward the spruces: six stakes leaning, one fallen in the snow (they came out thin, more stakes than posts). Two glazes of smalt on the near ground and the undulations: smalt is too weak a stainer (tint 0.45) for this; the ground still reads pale. Next glaze will carry a little Prussian blue and black.
- day 50, 17:29: Day 49-50: third ground glaze with Prussian blue and black in it - the foreground finally sits down and the undulations show a little. Snow caps on the stakes, two slack strands of wire, broken past the leaning post. Glaze haloes round the stakes closed. The walker's shadow came out a hard blue sliver; softened with a badger, now a faint cool smear along the path. Next: a line of distant pollards across the middle ground to give it a far edge and cover a grey smear there.
- day 53, 17:39: Day 53: tried to lose a grey smear in the middle distance with a snow scumble; the scumble spread warm and wide, a cooling glaze and blend made it worse, and it swallowed the row of pollards I had just put in along a far ditch. Drew it out level with a broad blender and let it be what it had become: a low mist lying in the hollow before the church, the pollards ghosting through it, the right-hand ones still clear. Accident kept rather than fought.
- day 56, 19:47: Day 56: an evening glaze of smalt, a little Prussian, black and umber over all the land - it quiets the confetti of grass. Then a mistake: a reed glaze with raw umber in it went on opaque (umber hides 0.8) and laid a brown plank along the far shore and two brown ovals over the reed beds. Lifted it while open with a clean flat, wiped after every stroke, three passes: it came off clean to the dry reeds below. Lesson: a glaze wants the low-hiding tubes - smalt, Prussian, green earth.
- day 68, 23:12: Day 59-68: tried the reeds once more with a glaze of smalt, Prussian and green earth - the detail brush laid it as bright blue strokes, worse than the gold. Lifted it all with the clean flat again; the reeds read olive-brown enough as they are, so I left them and only stood a few dark broken stems among them. A limb fallen from the oak lies downhill of it; stones by the path, half sunk; a hare's bounding track across the field toward the spruces. The snow on the limb took the whole of it (the limb is thinner than the cap); a rigger put back a dark underside and a shadow under it.
- day 73, 23:22: Day 70-73: the land is backlit and its face should sit below the glow: a deeper glaze of smalt, Prussian, black, red earth and green earth over all of it, leaving a thin lit rim on the knoll crest and the mist near the church. At last the sky's glow stands clear of the ground in the squint. It sharpened a hard-edged box round the oak's foot left by an old passage; buried it under a broader drift, lit along its crest, cool on its face, blended at its edges. It is too pale for now; to glaze into the field when dry.
- day 86, 23:23: Day 79-86: glazed the drift's face into the field; the old box shows through faintly as texture up close but at a step back it is just the drift at the oak's foot. The drift had swallowed the fallen limb, so it was laid again a little further down the slope, dark, with its shadow and a hair of snow. Calling it finished: the glow holds the centre, the oak stands against it, the walker at the edge of the mere looks toward the church in the mist, the crows come in, the young moon over the spruces.
- day 96, 23:26: Day 96: all touch-dry. A thin mastic varnish, 0.3 coats: it warms the whole a little and ties the glazes together. Saved as 'Evening at the Frozen Mere'.
