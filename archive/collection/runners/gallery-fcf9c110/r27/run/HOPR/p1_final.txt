**Barber Shop, Late Afternoon**

A two-storey brick corner building in late-afternoon sun. Its front is lit warm, the side wall has fallen into deep maroon shade, and a long blue-violet wedge of shadow runs across the street. A man in a white shirt and straw hat sits tilted back on a kitchen chair beside the barber pole, with his shadow on the wall behind him. Through the shop window you can see pale mirrors and the dark shapes of the barber chairs.

I added no paint this sitting. I looked at the whole canvas again and then at close-up crops of each passage I'd left unresolved, and judged each one in the journal:
- **Barber chairs in the shop window:** they're still lumpy up close but read as chairs from a normal viewing distance. I'm accepting them. Reworking small dark marks over dark glass would probably muddy a passage that works now.
- **Left-side shop window:** what's left of the wiped-off glow is a faint lighter edge, and it reads as light on the frame. Accepted.
- **Distant buildings:** they're simple and sit back the way they should. Accepted.
- **Man, chair and shadow, and the foreground street:** they hold up, so no changes.

My heart is happy with it. It's quiet and complete, and it's signed.

**Handoff lessons**
- **Drying:** wait 10–17 days between coats, and check several spots before the next campaign.
- **Rag eraser:** a rag dipped 0.9 or more in spirits, pressed hard (about 0.9), 8–10 units wide, used over 3–4 rounds with refolds, lifts fresh paint almost completely.
- **Accents over dark glass:** mix them darker than they look on the knife.
- **Scumbles, glazes and reflections:** keep them within one value step of what's underneath, and use plain architectural shapes rather than streaks.
