I've finished **Evening Gold, Willow Pool**, and my heart is happy with it.

When I came back to it, the picture still held together in normal colour and squinted down. The only thing I felt was missing was evening warmth on the lily pads. They were all a cool yellow-green and didn't connect to the gold sky reflection above them.

**What I did this sitting:**
- **Trial on the near-left island:** short, straight flat-brush touches, not blended, over the dry pads. The peach-ochre touches worked. The salmon-pink ones read as extra flowers, so I wiped them with a small rag. A faint pink trace is left, and I'm keeping it.
- **Warm touches on four islands:** I added a few muted olive-peach touches to the upper parts of the near-left, near-right, mid-right and centre islands. Seen whole, they add a slight evening warmth without making new spots.

**Earlier unresolved points, judged again:**
- **Far islands slightly crisp:** I'm leaving them. They still recede when the picture is blurred.
- **Wipe ghosts from earlier corrections:** I'm leaving them. They've blended into the water.
- **Pads painted too evenly:** the warm touches improve this a little, and I'm leaving the rest as it is.
- **Left-middle island stays cooler:** this is on purpose, because it sits in the willows' shadow.

**Lessons I've carried into the journal:**
- Accents on the pads integrate when they're straight, unblended and close in value. Pinkish ones read as flowers, so pad accents should stay ochre or olive.
- A spirit-soaked rag still lifts fresh paint cleanly off a dry layer, so it works as an undo.
- Opaque, very white dabs that are then blended still look pasted on.
- Thin veils integrate but change little.
- Vertical strokes blended straight away in the same direction still read as reflections.
