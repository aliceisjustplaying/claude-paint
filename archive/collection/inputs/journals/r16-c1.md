- day 1, 09:00: Plan: a still life after the Chardin manner - a cream earthenware jug, two lemons and a knife on a stone ledge against a dark warm wall. Light from the upper left. Canvas 460mm, 1000x800 units, warm grey-ochre imprimatura over lead white. Table back edge ~y520, ledge front edge ~y690; jug centred x360, rim y190, base y600; lemons right of it about (600,590) and (705,560); knife handle overhangs the front edge at lower right.
- day 1, 12:48: Wall laid in: a reddish umber/black first pass was too hot, so an olive grey (umber, black, ochre, green earth) over it. A lighter scumble at right came out as a hard-edged cloud; covered with a mid mixture and blended the whole wall twice at crossing angles -> now a quiet gradation, darker left, lighter right. Lesson: blend the whole field, not a soft-edged zone - blending a zone leaves its outline. Handle got smeared by blending; will repaint.
- day 1, 14:38: Dead colour on jug and lemons. Modelled with form-value zones (shadow/half/light) wet into wet, but the secondary passes came out as separate short dabs (scaly) and a full badger blend then averaged everything to one flat mid value - jug and lemons now read as flat cut-outs. Lesson: the 40-unit blender over a whole small object erases the modelling. Plan: let this lay-in dry, then model in a second layer over it with stronger darks, working transitions with scumbles and small-area blending only.
- day 4, 09:12: Shadow pass from the ellipsoid scaffold spilled outside the scaffold (value is low off the solid) onto the lit edge and spout; patched. Small-blender on the terminator lifted rather than fused. Change of method: let it dry, then model the jug by hand in vertical strokes following its contour, strip by strip from light (left) to dark (right) with a graded string of seven piles, so neighbouring strips fuse wet-into-wet where they meet.
- day 8, 09:25: Hand-planned vertical strips gave structure but read as stripes; one vertical blend (badger 20) fused them and again pulled the whole jug up to a pale cream. Accepting that as the light underlayer. Next: once dry, put the shadow side in with thin glazes clipped to nested soft masks (clip weighs by mask value, so soft masks give soft transitions) instead of blending.
- day 10, 09:42: Shadow glaze on the jug first went on as blotches (cow pattern) - glaze strokes with some white in them are too opaque and coverage too low; blending the new wet layer over the dry cream fused it into a readable gradation from cream light to a grey shadow side. The jug turns now. Next: cast shadows on wall and table, then rim/opening, spout, handle, reflected light.
- day 10, 10:09: Cast shadow of the jug on the wall: glazed, then blended locally -> hard outline of the blended zone again; a whole-wall blend dissolved it into a soft smoky shadow right of the jug. Keeps confirming: blend whole fields. The jug shadow side is too dark and purple-grey, and the light is a narrow band - next layer: scumble lights and half tones over it when dry, with a warm reflected light at the right edge.
- day 11, 23:25: Scumbles and strips of lead-white mixtures on the jug: clip does not soften at a mask ramp, it cut hard bands; a dark strip laid into the wet pale lifted it and came out pale; a blend then pulled the whole jug to cream again. Lead white dominates every wet mix here. Decision: treat this cream as the light body and model the shadow only with transparent glazes (no white) over it once dry, blending each glaze while wet with the blend zone reaching well into the lit side.
- day 15, 23:45: Pure umber/green-earth/black glaze on the jug went on far too dark; lifted it with a clean flat wiped after each stroke, then blended - most came off, leaving a speckled light-brown veil over the shadow side. From across the room it reads like salt-glazed stoneware, which I will lean into: a stoneware jug. Shadow core still needs a thin, dilute glaze later.
- day 15, 23:58: Lemon shadow glaze started everywhere (the value scaffold is low over most of the lemons) and blending smeared it olive; lifted with a wiped clean flat and blended back to a slightly dulled yellow. Glazing with work() is not giving me control. For the lemons I will paint direct opaque touches, dark crescent first then mids and lights, curved along the form, no blender.
- day 18, 12:11: Lemons painted in hand-planned curved strokes, five piles from olive shadow to pale yellow light - they turned. Then a badger blend at 0.6 rad dragged it all into diagonal streaks. No more blender on small forms. Will repaint lemons once dry, more steps, overlapping strokes, no blending. Now the table.
- day 18, 12:36: Table top re-laid a tone darker (cooler stone) with the cast shadows of jug and lemons worked in wet, falling right and back. Jug: opening ellipse with dark inner wall at left, lighter at right; light front rim; spout lip; handle repainted as a curved strap with light top edge. Handle a little too pale for the shadow side - revisit.
- day 20, 12:57: Lemons repainted: seven piles from olive to pale, curved strokes along the long axis, 0.075 apart, dark to light, no blending. They turn now and read as lemons; stroke ends leave a scalloped texture I rather like. Next: the knife, blade on the table from under the front lemon, handle overhanging the ledge.
- day 20, 13:01: Wrote an endless loop in a chunk (the arris strokes never reached the end of the ledge); the easel hung, I stopped its process and reopened, and it replayed the 54 logged chunks. The failed chunk left nothing. Rerunning it fixed.
- day 20, 13:05: Knife: grey blade with a light top edge and cast shadow on the table, bolster, dark wood handle over the ledge. Blending the handle shadow dragged the still-wet handle paint into a big brown oval on the front face; lifted most of it with a wiped flat. Front face to be re-laid whole when dry, with the handle shadow in wet and a whole-face horizontal blend.
- day 23, 01:35: Shadow half of the jug: broken strokes read as stripes, so I badgered the right half (t>0.4, a whole field with its edge inside the turning light). Now smooth but too uniform and pale; no shadow core. Letting it dry, then a single wet layer of core, half-tone and reflected light blended within the same field.
- day 25, 13:39: Opaque shadow mixes on the jug again came out far too pale once blended; lead white wins. The guide has pile medium (0-0.95) for transparent glazes, which I had missed. Next, once dry: a thin umber glaze in the core rather than more opaque paint.
- day 28, 13:45: Umber glaze (medium 0.88) in the jug core: laid as separate yellow-brown strokes, then badgered across and down the right-half field. It became a warm mottled half-tone, like salt glaze. Right direction, still too light; a second, cooler glaze once dry.
- day 31, 13:55: Second glaze, umber with black and green earth, badgered the same way, then one horizontal pass over the whole body to soften the terminator. The jug turns now: cream light, mottled warm-grey shadow. Remaining: the handle (too pale), the halo and scallops along the left contour, a base contact shadow, a highlight.
- day 35, 14:43: Cut the wall back along the jug contour with a lighter olive, then badgered the whole wall. The first pass pulled the fresh paint out in flame-like streaks; three more passes with a 40 badger in three directions melted them into a soft glow round the jug. Wall reads as air again, contour clean.
- day 38, 14:48: Opening repainted: dark left, warm grey lit inner wall at right. The back rim was re-drawn and the front rim put into half-tone where it turns into shadow. Added a contact shadow under the jug. A glaze on the handle came out as dark ticks; the handle is still too pale. Next, when dry: an opaque darker strap on the handle, the orange speck inside the loop, the dirty lower-left belly, and a highlight.
- day 41, 14:53: Mistake: badgering a horizontal band across the lower jug pulled the fresh cream strokes over the shadow side and left a line at the band top. Lifted most of it with a clean wiped flat. What is left, a pale haze low on the right, gets a glaze when dry. Rule again: no blends across a light/dark boundary unless the dark is the wet part.
- day 44, 15:12: Lemons: a stipple of deeper yellow for the pitted skin, small pale highlights, nib accents, and an olive glaze where the front lemon shadows the back one. Contact shadows went in too low and hard, floating under the lemons; wiped them out. Re-doing them in table colour when dry. Also a darker strap on the handle and a glaze over the pale haze low on the jug shadow side.
- day 48, 15:13: Lemons now sit on the ledge: table colour laid round their undersides and a thin dark contact line hugging the edge. A darker umber strap on the descending handle makes it turn away from the light. Whole view: jug, lemons, knife and ledge hold together in a quiet, warm, low key. I am calling it finished rather than working the jug further, since every extra pass there has cost as much as it gave. Letting it dry, then a light varnish.
- day 53, 15:15: Varnished lightly (0.3 coats) on day 53; it warms and knits the olive wall and the grey ledge together. Finished. Title: Stoneware Jug with Two Lemons and a Knife. What I learned: in this medium the shadows of a pale object have to come from thin glazes over dry light paint, badgered within a whole field, not from opaque darker mixes, which the lead white swallows.
