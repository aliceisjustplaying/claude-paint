**The Oak in Snow at Evening**

The painting is one old oak, set on the central axis at the top of a shallow snow-covered rise, just after sunset. It's finished, though some passages are unresolved (listed below). The sky takes about two-thirds of the canvas and is painted in bands with no break: cold smalt and cobalt overhead, a pale green-gray in the middle and a warm Naples-yellow glow along the ruled horizon. The snow stops at a clean edge, so the eye can't walk into the distance. Beyond it there is only a band of dark forest in valley mist and a faint ridge rising on the right. There are four kinds of things in the picture and no figure. The oak is the one object to look at: a broad dark trunk, a leader with a bare dead top, and a crown of angular branches and twigs that thins out against the light.

**What's still unresolved**
- The fine crown branches dried a warm mid-gray-brown, lighter than a Friedrich silhouette would be. Thin lines over a light sky come out paler than the paint mixed on the palette. The trunk and main limbs are properly dark. To darken the rest I would need the paths of every branch, and the first batch of them was never recorded.
- The trunk is a rather blocky column. I widened its sides to cover earlier mistakes, so its outline is simplified.
- A faint pale rectangle from an old wipe remains in the upper-left sky. I softened it and it's nearly gone.
- I left the snow field deliberately plain. A long, quiet modulation of shadow across it could still make it richer.

**Lessons for next time** (all in the journal)
- **Patching the sky:** small opaque patches in a thin, blended sky dry paler and show. What worked was repainting the whole band with the original paint mixes, clipped around the tree, in two coats about 10 days apart.
- **Painting over a passage:** the area to repaint must be the old paint minus what is kept, never a bounding rectangle. Using a rectangle once buried twigs and sky, and it took about 25 rag passes to lift it off.
- **Wiping:** use a fresh rag soaked in spirits for each pass and press hard. Refolding one rag drags the dark paint it has lifted across the light.
- **Branches:**
  - Twigs have to grow out of drawn branches. Scattered starting points read as fluff.
  - Oak branches need straight runs with occasional sharp kinks. Smoothly alternating bends read as snakes.
- **Drying:** checking whether paint is dry at one point doesn't tell you about the whole path you plan to wipe. Thick, light patches heavy in lead white took about 20 painting days to dry to the touch.
