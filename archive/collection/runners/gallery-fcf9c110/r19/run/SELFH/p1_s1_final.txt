**"Self-Portrait with Palette"**

A half-length figure in a dark painter's smock faces the viewer directly, as in a mirror — grey-haired, plainly lit from the upper left, one ear and a hint of jaw-shadow the only concessions to volume. Behind the head a warm, broken halo of ochre and umber churns up out of the near-black ground, as if the studio's lamp (or the paint itself) were restless. Held low in front is a kidney palette carrying seven true dabs from the tubes actually used on this canvas — white, ochre, vermilion, red earth, cobalt, green earth, bone black — a smear of mixed grey, a thumb through the hole, and three brush handles fanned up over the shoulder toward the unseen canvas.

It's a plain, direct likeness rather than a virtuosic one — I have no face of my own to paint, so I gave it the generic dignity of the trade: palette, brushes, and a steady look out at whoever's looking back.
