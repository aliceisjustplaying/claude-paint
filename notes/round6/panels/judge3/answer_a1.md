A recurring difference is visible, but not consistently under either letter because X and Y are shuffled independently. X in pairs 1–4 and Y in pairs 5–6 tend to soften overlaps and absorb later lights into underlying paint; their counterparts preserve more separate, opaque stroke shapes. Integration helps the cloud, reflection, rock base and valley hedge, but costs the broadleaf crown some sunlight and turns the winter wood muddy. Neither treatment reliably solves the hard cutout silhouettes or repeated surface marks visible in the crops.
