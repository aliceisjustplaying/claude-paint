## 1. General impressions

**1 — P:** The small traveler beneath the burial stones and enormous bare tree gives the strongest feeling of human life meeting something older and indifferent. The yellow light behind the stone openings is especially good: emptiness becomes the subject. The restrained snow leaves room for silence. But the tree is too cleanly constructed and the stones look modeled rather than painted. I would keep looking despite that. This wins for the relationship between person, monument and sky, not its surface. (`P.png`, `P_crop_center.png`, `P_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

**2 — S:** A traveler follows a winding route toward a ruined church through winter fields. The pale ruin recedes convincingly at its foot, and the broad snow and sky marks feel more like a painter working across a surface. This could move someone through its sense of a long, cold approach. But the repeated bends announce the route too insistently; fence, footprints, birds and firs explain a story that could work with less explanation. The tree's blunt awkwardness is preferable to polished geometry. (`S.png`, `S_crop_center.png`, `S_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

**3 — Q:** A dead tree and snow-covered firs flank a tiny figure beside an oval frozen-looking patch. The great open sky and low horizon offer genuine loneliness; the twisting left tree has some bodily strain. But the separate objects never quite become one place: the fir group is a cutout, the oval is a diagram and the distant shore is a ruler-straight strip. I feel the intended stillness more than a particular person's experience of it. (`Q.png`, `Q_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

**4 — T:** A summer pasture with a monumental leafy tree, a couple, small livestock and a distant settlement. The pair beneath the tree offers a welcome possibility of tenderness, and the red dress gives the greens a useful interruption. But the giant crown, cylindrical trunk and evenly distributed little objects make the scene feel assembled. Warmth could move someone here; for me, the surface keeps replacing intimacy with a display of generated vegetation. (`T.png`, `T_crop_lowerleft.png`, `T_crop_center.png`.)

## 2. Paint or pixels?

Problems within each entry are ordered by cost.

### P
- **Tree construction:** Long, dark limbs have clean tapering boundaries and smooth highlight ribbons. Small forked marks recur, sometimes apparently detached from branches. The focal tree reads as a branching diagram before it reads as loaded brushwork. (`P_crop_center.png`, `P_crop_upperright.png`.)
- **Stone and snow:** The capstone has softly faceted shading beneath a neatly fitted white lid; nearby little rocks repeat the dark-base/white-cap formula. They resemble shaded objects placed on the field. (`P_crop_center.png`, `P_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
- **Surface treatment:** Fine cracklike lines cross unrelated forms, while the sky carries an even granular texture. These suggest an aged picture surface rather than evidence of painting decisions. (`P_crop_upperright.png`, `P_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
- **What reads as paint:** The rubbed gray horizon and broad, subdued snow transitions are the best passages. They merge rather than inventory things. The upright stone supports also have useful rough vertical marks. (`P_crop_lowerleft.png`, `P_crop_center.png`.)

### S
- **Outlined symbols:** The ruin's pointed opening and outer silhouette are very clean compared with its broken, misty base. The two birds are emphatic black emblems. These are the moments where a painted atmosphere becomes illustration. (`S_crop_center.png`.)
- **Repeated line recipes:** Bark highlights run down the trunk as parallel stripes; snow follows branches as tidy icing. The winding route and dotted tracks add more conspicuously organized marks. (`S_crop_lowerleft.png`, `S.png`.)
- **Surface treatment:** A fine crossing mesh and cracklike lines remain visible even in quiet sky. More surface activity is not necessarily more paint. (`S_crop_upperright.png`.)
- **What reads as paint:** Broad diagonal sky strokes, overlapping cool snow strokes and the scumbled disappearance of the church's base are the most convincing brush passages across the set. They interrupt and partly cover one another. Preserve that incompleteness. (`S_crop_upperright.png`, `S_crop_lowerleft.png`, `S_crop_center.png`.)

### Q
- **Disconnected boundaries:** The horizontal shore ends in a perfectly level bottom edge. The oval patch has a closed, designed perimeter filled with separate pale slivers. Both flatten the space. (`Q_crop_center.png`, `Q_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
- **Decorative repetition:** Snow on the firs forms repeated pale tiers. The moon's isolated speckled halo reads as a separate effect rather than light spreading through the surrounding sky. (`Q.png`, `Q_crop_upperright.png`.)
- **What reads as paint:** Broken dark strokes and pale skips in the large trunk give it more physical resistance than P's tree. The overlapping blue-gray and cream snow patches also suggest brush-sized decisions, though their similar sizes become repetitive. (`Q_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

### T
- **Tree rendering:** Thousands of discrete leaf-shaped flecks describe nearly every part of the crown, while the trunk has smooth cylindrical shading and a conspicuous horizontal change near its base. Neither leaves much room for a broad, uncertain brush statement. (`T_crop_center.png`, `T_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
- **Repeated vegetation:** Small orchard trees share a similar construction, grass repeats little forked shapes and long shadows behave like clean strips. These repetitions make the landscape feel populated from a kit. (`T_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
- **Artificial age and clouds:** The prominent crack network competes with the sky. The long bright cloud consists of dense pale dots with a sprayed-looking margin. Damage and stippling do not make this passage feel brushed. (`T_crop_upperright.png`.)
- **What reads as paint:** Broad olive field patches and the couple's simple clothing shapes come closest. The dress needs fewer marks than the foliage yet carries more human interest. (`T_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

## 3. Advice, highest payoff first

1. **Keep the emotional structure; remove competing information.** P: protect the tiny person and luminous stone openings. S: interrupt the winding route with snow and remove some explanatory accessories. Q: make the person's relation to the oval clearer through placement, not more detail. T: let the couple and sheltering tree dominate instead of the inventory of leaves and plants. These are the strongest relationships already available in the whole images.

2. **Repaint the main objects with fewer, larger statements.** P's tree needs broad dark strokes with uneven joins, not more twigs. T's crown needs a handful of connected light and dark masses before any leaves; leave most individual leaves unpainted. S's ruin needs some sky dragged into its hard contour. Q's oval needs interrupted edges that become indistinguishable from surrounding snow. Judge these changes at whole-picture size first. (Targets: the four center crops, `Q_crop_lowerleft.png` and `T_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

3. **Let adjacent paint interfere.** Tool builders: prioritize a stroke picking up existing color, thinning as its load runs out and partially dragging an earlier edge. Painters: actually use those behaviors where tree meets sky or snow meets stone. Keep a few crisp edges; lose others. S's softened church foot is the useful visible model, not a mandate to blur everything. (`S_crop_center.png`; contrast `P_crop_center.png`.)

4. **Break the recipes by omission, not random jitter.** Leave whole stretches without grass, merge firs into one uneven dark mass and vary which branches receive snow. Give repeated orchard trees different silhouettes and conceal some. The recurring grass forks, fir tiers and branch icing need different decisions, not merely different coordinates. (`T_crop_lowerleft.png`, `Q.png`, `S_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

5. **Disable the antique finish for the next comparison.** Render without cracklike lines and all-over grain. Then ask whether the actual strokes still carry the picture. The visible overlays are especially distracting in T's sky and the enlarged winter fields; they cannot repair smooth object shading underneath. (`T_crop_upperright.png`, `P_crop_lowerleft.png`, `Q_crop_lowerleft.png`.)

6. **Test tools on one difficult overlap, not another complete landscape.** Repaint one dark trunk crossing pale sky and entering snow. Compare versions for broken contact, local color pickup and convincing brush width, with texture overlays off. Choose the version that feels deliberately painted even if its drawing is clumsier. P's clean trunk, Q's broken trunk and S's striped trunk provide three concrete starting points. (`P_crop_center.png`, `Q_crop_lowerleft.png`, `S_crop_lowerleft.png`.)
