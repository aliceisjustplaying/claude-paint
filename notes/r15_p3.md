# r15_p3: Morning on the Baltic shore

`paintings/src/bin/r15_p3.rs` → `out/r15_p3_full.png` (2400 × 1846 px,
aspect 1.3, `Style::friedrich()` with a whitish brushed top ground).

## The picture and why

A calm sea before sunrise. The sky runs from a cool slate-blue zenith
down through grey to a pale yellow, and the horizon glows apricot to the
right of centre, where the sun will come up. A thin waning crescent hangs
high on the left. It is the old moon, still up at dawn, and its lit side
turns down-right toward the sun below the horizon. Long thin stratus
strands lie low over the sea. Their undersides are lit salmon near the
glow and violet-grey away from it.

- **The horizon** is a single ruled line at 468 of 769 units (0.61 of the
  height). The sky takes most of the picture. It is empty in the way his
  *Monk* and his shore pictures are: the subject is the light and the
  waiting.
- **The woman** stands at the water's edge right of centre (x = 602), seen
  from behind. Her head just breaks the horizon, the height it should be for
  a figure standing on the same ground as the viewer. She stands at the
  left flank of the glow's path in the water, so her dark silhouette is set
  against the brightest part of the sea. She wears a near-black dress and a
  dark red shawl, the one warm accent in the figure. Her reflection lies in
  the wet sand at her feet.
- **Left:** a low dune bank, grass along its crest against the sea. Two net
  poles stand on it with a net hung between them, and a third stake leans.
  They are the verticals that cross the horizon: human work, idle at this
  hour.
- **Right foreground:** a dark granite erratic bedded in the sand, a
  smaller stone near it. Together with the dune it frames the beach in a
  shallow V that opens toward the figure and the light.
- **Distance:** a brig hull-down on the horizon under the glow's right flank,
  and three gulls.
- **Colour and value:** light in the sky and the sea, dark in the lower
  corners. The corners are darkened by glazes, following Carus's advice:
  "growing darker toward the picture's edges" (research notes §6).

## Working method, stage by stage

Stages (checkpointed): drawing, sky, sky2, sea, ripples, beach, shore,
pools, boulder, moon, ship, poles, figure, grass, gulls, glazes, and then the
finish.

1. **Ground.** `Style::friedrich()` knifed layers, with the top brushed
   ground replaced by a whitish #d9c9ae, 55 µm, stiff 0.4, as in r15_p1
   and r15_p2.
2. **drawing.** 2H: the horizon (ruled), the water's edge and the dune
   crest. HB: the boulder, the three poles and the figure. The figure and
   the dune were later painted elsewhere than drawn (pentimenti, see
   critique).
3. **sky.** One broad lean layer (filbert 22, medium 0.3, coverage 4,
   160–340-unit strokes nearly level, swept down), aimed at a gradient
   function. Then a stipple into the wet paint (tip 2.6, cluster 0, dips
   every 8) and two badger passes.
4. **sky2.** Dry, then a second thin layer (medium 0.42, coverage 3) and
   two badger passes. Into it: the stratus strands (filbert 6, clipped to
   band masks, strokes along each band), their lit lower rims (round 2.4),
   and one light badger pass over the bands only.
5. **sea.** Dry; a broad level lay clipped hard at the horizon, then a
   level badger pass. The colour field treats calm water as a mirror: at
   depression d below the horizon it shows the sky from 2d above (the
   swell's facets tilt toward the eye), mixed with a dark body colour by a
   reflectance falling from 0.92 to 0.57. A darker band sits just under the
   horizon, and a warm path widening toward us sits under the glow.
6. **ripples.** Dry; four depth bands of short level sable strokes, finer
   and shorter toward the horizon (tool 0.9 → 2.4, length 4–12 → 14–50).
   Dark strokes (−0.045 L) everywhere; light warm glints loaded mostly in
   the glow's path.
7. **beach.** Dry; body strokes clipped below a scalloped water's edge. The
   colour runs from wet sand mirroring the water into sand darkening toward
   us. A badger pass follows. Then the dune: body strokes down its slope,
   its colour blending into the beach colour where it fades out, and a
   badger pass.
8. **shore.** After 4 h: a broken light lip on the lapping wave and its
   shadow just below, faint tide ridges, the wrack line and a sparse
   pebble stipple.
9. **pools.** Three shallow tide pools mirroring the sky at the mirrored
   elevation (reflectance 0.6). Warm strokes carry the glow's path across
   the wet sand beside the figure.
10. **boulder.** A `Form`: two unioned ellipsoids cut by three planes,
    ridged and fine noise. The light comes from behind-left, (−1, −0.35,
    −0.5), with ambient 0.35. The colour comes from the sample: a dark,
    slightly cool face, a sky-lit crown and a narrow warm rim. Filbert
    along the planes, a light blend, sable texture along the fall lines,
    rim strokes, sand thrown against the foot and a soft shadow toward
    us.
11. **moon.** A pale halo glaze (0.12), the crescent as three sable strokes
    along its arc clipped to disc − offset disc, and an almost invisible
    ashen light on the dark part.
12. **ship.** A polygon mask (hull, two gaff sails, jib, masts) painted with
    the sable by masstone, grey sails over a darker hull.
13. **poles.** Net: sparse crossing rigger twine, then a transparent
    #4b453d glaze whose depth varies in vertical folds. Then the three
    poles as single drags of a pointed round, and the head rope.
14. **figure.** A glaze for her reflection (0.7) and one for a dark contact
    at her feet, the body with the sable (shawl V in #3f2322, dress
    #262422, hair #221c1a), a warm rim on the glow side and a knot of
    hair.
15. **grass.** Tufts of pointed-sable blades (6–20 per tuft) along the
    crest every 4–20 units, and 26 clustered patches down the bank.
16. **gulls.** Three M-shaped birds, one drag per wing.
17. **glazes.** A warm transparent #e9a66b (0.22) on the glow and the head
    of its path; a cool dark #4f4a52 toward the top corners (0.2) and the
    bottom (0.55); an umber #5b4a37 (0.45) over the dune bank, leaving its
    crest light.
18. **finish.** Varnish #e6d3a4 at 0.22 ± 0.06 coats; faint cracks (width
    4 µm, dirt 0.12, veil 0.15, hierarchy 0.5, patchy 0.6); the style's
    relief.

A from-scratch render takes 203 s (ground 38 s, each sky layer
about 60 s, beach 20 s, the rest a few seconds each). Resumed iterations
on the late stages took 4–40 s. The final from-scratch render is
byte-identical to the one assembled from resumed checkpoints.

## Tool limitations met, and workarounds

1. **Staleness counts whitespace and no-op edits.** Inserting a helper
   function below `main` with a `// ckpt: from figure` region still staled
   every checkpoint. My insertion left one extra *untagged* blank line
   after the `// ckpt: end`. Removing redundant parentheses in the shore
   block staled everything after it too. *Workaround:* keep tagged
   regions tight (no stray blank lines outside them), and use
   `--stale-ok --ckpt` when I know an edit changes nothing painted.
2. **A tag's stage must be the first stage that uses the code.** I tagged
   the cloud-band helpers `from sky` when only `sky2` used them, so editing
   them staled the `sky` checkpoint (60 s). *Workaround:* retag
   `from sky2` and adopt once with `--stale-ok`.
3. **Helpers used by the drawing stage tie every checkpoint to them.** The
   dune crest function was drawn in pencil, so changing it would redo the
   ground and both sky layers (~3 min). *Workaround:* I left the drawn
   crest alone and extended or reshaped the painted crest in a closure
   after the sky stages. The difference is a real pentimento. Its pencil
   line showed through the first dune paint as a thin dark "crack", until
   a fuller dune layer (coverage 3.6, load 0.8) and the bank glaze hid it.
4. **Aiming strokes that cross two fields.** A `color_over` pass (darker
   than what is under) of vertical strokes across the horizon mixed its
   pile for the pale sky and laid it over the darker sea, so the net's
   veil and the figure's first reflection came out *lighter*. The aim
   samples the underlayer along the stroke weighted to its start, which
   is right for one field and wrong across two. *Workaround:* where a pass
   only needs to darken, use `Canvas::glaze` with a transparent pigment
   and a mask; it darkens each pixel relative to itself.
5. **Aiming small shapes by the handling's assumed thickness.** The
   `detail()` preset at coverage 4 expects about 8 coats, so the aim chose
   semi-transparent pale smalt for grey sails. On a sail only 9 units wide
   the laid film was much thinner, and they came out pale. *Workaround:*
   `.by_masstone()` with a full load (`dips(1, 1.0, 0.3)`).
6. **`Mask::roughen` re-thresholds the whole mask.** A dune mask with a
   200-unit soft fade at its foot, roughened afterwards, came back with a
   hard diagonal edge where the fade crossed 0.5. It read as a cliff.
   *Workaround:* roughen only the crest, then multiply the fade in.
7. **Blender lifting on a single lean layer.** Three badger passes over
   one lean sky layer lifted paint off the weave crowns, and pale lattice
   wisps showed the ground through. *Workaround:* two layers (dry between)
   as the research notes describe for his skies, and no third badger pass.
8. **Checkpoints are large:** 443 MB each through the beach, 549 MB from
   the shore stage on; 17 stages filled 7.6 GB of `out/`.

## Critique

What works:
- At the whole view the mood is right: a quiet, cool dawn with the light
  concentrated low, and a single figure turned toward it.
- The horizon is crisp. The sea reads as calm water: its ripples shrink
  with distance, a darker band lies at the horizon, the glow's path is
  broken into glints, and a thin lap of foam runs at the edge. The figure,
  small and dark against the brightest water, carries the picture. Her
  reflection and the warm streak on the wet sand beside her tie her to the
  light.
- The second sky layer gave the smooth, luminous gradation I wanted. The
  stratus strands, once long, thin and rarely broken, read as dawn
  cloud.
- The boulder, once dark, reads as a stone seen against the light rather
  than a pillow.

What is weaker:
- The strands are still more even and horizontal than real stratus. At the
  whole view one or two read as ruled lines.
- The dune is plain. The grass along its crest works against the sea, but
  the bank is a soft brown slope without his zigzag grass marks, and the
  glazes leave its lower half almost empty.
- The beach between the dune and the boulder is a hazy, nearly
  featureless plane. The pools and ridges barely register. That fits a
  pre-dawn haze, but a firmer structure of wet and dry sand would give the
  ground more weight.
- The figure is a clean silhouette but stiff and symmetrical, a little
  like a chess piece. A slight turn of the head or a hand at the shawl
  would make her live.
- The net's veil is convincing as cloth but tidy. The ship is legible but
  generic.
