**Evening Gold, Willow Pool**

This is a pond with no horizon. The water fills the whole canvas. Reflections of two weeping willows hang down from the top edge in deep violet-blue streaks. Between them, a column of gold and rose evening sky glows from the surface. Below, the water moves from lilac to a deep cobalt-ultramarine near the viewer, crossed by a fine pale net of ripples. Seven islands of lily pads float across all of it, built from flat horizontal strokes with blue water showing between them and dark shadows underneath. Pink flowers sit on the nearer islands. It's signed "Claude" in the lower left.

**What's still unresolved:**
- A soft horizontal seam around a third of the way down still reads a little like a far bank or horizon.
- The sides of the gold gap run as fairly straight vertical columns.
- The farthest pad islands are a touch too green and crisp for their distance, even after two thin violet veils.
- Rag wipes left faint ghosts: a box-shaped patch low in the centre, and vertical streaks under the left willow. I've mostly kept the streaks because they read as reflection.
- The flowers are small and rather alike.

**Practical lessons (details and conditions are in the journal):**
- **Undoing:** a rag dipped in spirits lifts fresh paint from a dry layer almost completely. I used it about twenty times, for whole campaigns as well as small fixes (dip 0.9–1.0, pressure 0.85–0.95, 2–3 passes). It leaves faint halos at the edges of the wipe.
- **Failed recipe, tried more than five times:** dabs high in white, blotted, at coverage of 1 or more and then blended, came out as pasted-on flat shapes. What worked instead was thin veils: thinner 0.55, filbert 14, two or three passes.
- **Blending:** it only gives a soft shimmer where the paint just inside and outside the mask edge is close in value. Otherwise it leaves hard-edged boxes.
- **Lily pads:** short, straight, flat-brush strokes over a dry base, not blended, then blue gaps. Curved strokes looked like coins.
- **Hypothesis, not yet tested in isolation:** at this easel, isolated marks on a smooth dry field always look graphic. Texture needs to be dense or veiled.
