- day 1, 09:00: Plan. A summer evening on the flat meadows outside a small Baltic town, in the manner of Friedrich's Greifswald meadows but my own composition. Wide format 1.55:1, low horizon at y~392 so the sky takes three fifths. Town silhouette with three church towers right of centre (x 560-740), windmills on the horizon at left and right, a ditch of still water crossing the middle ground reflecting the sky, a row of pollard willows along it at left, grazing horses and haycocks in the middle meadows, and in the foreground a darker rising bank with a path and a couple seen from behind looking toward the town. Light: late sun low from the left, warm glow at the horizon, pale smalt sky above. Ground: warm red-ochre knife layer under a patchy brushed lead-white top, as his bought canvases.
- day 1, 12:34: Day 1. Pencil underdrawing (2H): horizon, ditch, towers, mills, willows, the two figures. Sky laid in ten graded bands, first far too pale (smalt is weak), so restated the upper third with smalt+cobalt and a touch of Prussian, then three rounds of long level badger passes: now a smooth gradient from mid blue to a warm cream at the horizon. A low violet-gray cloud bank at left (very faint, may glaze it stronger later) and thin warm evening streaks. Land laid in thin in five horizontal zones, blue-gray far strip to olive foreground; unclipped strokes left spikes above the horizon line, to be hidden under the tree lines and town. Blending the land raised a few pale blotches; it is only the underpainting. Letting it dry before the second layer.
- day 4, 17:03: Day 4. Distant tree lines stippled along the horizon: came out as round gray balls, too blobby; a paler haze stipple into them just fused. Will restate them dry with flatter, more varied silhouettes. Town: first pass pale and minaret-thin; restated with broader towers (a tall spire left, a massive tower with low cap and lantern in the middle, a second spire right) and long high nave roofs. Far fields: laid as a patchwork of strips which looked like a striped flag, so blended it level and veiled it; the veil strokes left pale arcs, three more rounds of long badger took them out. Now a soft, hazy band that needs structure (hedgerows, field edges) once dry. Middle meadow green restated; foreground graded from a warm lit crest down to a dark umber-green bottom. Waiting a day.
- day 10, 12:05: Day 10. Town restated when dry: lit west faces in a warm rose-gray, shadow sides glazed with smalt/umber, belfry slits and nave windows with a pointed round. The ghost of the first right-hand spire showed as a split apex; painted the spire a little blunter to swallow it. Distant woods rebuilt as rows of crowns with flat bases, plus a few poplars; still rather flat gray. Stepping back: the far-meadow band (398-467) is too pale and sandy, and meets the green middle meadow in a hard horizontal line across the whole width. Needs a greener, deeper, graded restatement with hedgerows, and a transition into the green. Waiting for it to dry.
- day 15, 21:12: Day 15. Far meadows restated in four graded greens (blue-green at the horizon to warm sunlit green), blended level: the plain now recedes. Lit upper-left rims stippled on the distant woods once dry. Footpath laid up the rise: came out as a bright tan ribbon with scalloped edges, and the glaze/lose pass made it look like crumpled paper. Will narrow it with grass and glaze it down once dry. The ditch: a clean white diagonal line straight across the plain, reading as a scratch, and it leads the eye away from the town. Decision: keep only the near-left stretch by the willows, paint the rest out with meadow green once dry, and leave a couple of small glints of water farther along.
- day 20, 14:03: Day 20. Painted the ditch out east of x~336 with matched meadow greens (two small glints kept). Left stretch toned to a pale blue-gray with a dark far-bank lip; still too clean and straight, willows and reflections should break it. Path narrowed with grass from both sides; a glaze laid across it left ladder stripes, blended along the track, now a thin worn trace. Two post mills on the horizon, trestle, body with lit west side, lattice sails. Hedgerows across the plain: beaded and too gray/blue, to be broken and greened when dry. Five pollard willows along the ditch: crowns came out pale cotton-balls, shade stipple fused into the wet; second pass when dry.
- day 22, 07:29: Day 22. The couple painted from behind on the path: he in a dark blue-black greatcoat, cap, light trousers, a stick; she in a red-brown dress with a cream shawl and her hair up; long cast shadows to the right. Shawl picked up red from the wet dress, to be restated. Four horses grazing in the middle meadow (bay, gray, black, chestnut), a dozen haycocks with short shadows on the right. Stepping back the picture now reads: plain, town, figures. To do: willows darker and more silvery-green, ditch reflections, a proper foreground (thinking a granite erratic at right with stones, grasses, a few flowers), hedgerow tones, lit edges on figures and horses, birds.
- day 27, 17:42: Day 27. Willows second pass when dry: silver-green stipple over the pale crowns, darker shade side and hollows, pale silvery lights at the upper left, fine rods rising from the heads; grayer bark, knobbly heads. Water veiled cooler and darker; trunk reflections as short broken dark strokes. Foreground broken with clustered stipple of darker and lighter greens (the lighter reads as a buttercup meadow along the crest). A granite erratic at right, modeled with a form: came out pale as a loaf, and the speckle made terrazzo. In value+squint it is the brightest thing on the land. It must go much darker with only the upper-left catching the evening light; waiting for it to dry.
- day 34, 09:39: Day 34. Boulder repainted dry: body mid-dark gray, darker shadow side, warm scumbled light on top. A wet blend then smeared it all to a light muddy beige; must glaze it down dry. Foreground: ~7000 pointed-round blades, dark and mid first sparse light ones read as yellow hairs, so a second, denser dark/mid layer unified it. Hay wagon on the right with two horses harnessed, a man on the load with a fork and one handing up hay. Seven birds flying home above the horizon left of the town. Horses given cast shadows and warm light on their backs. Hedges greened and lit on top. Ditch glazed cool so it sits down in the meadow, a few sky glints. Willow crowns glazed: far too dark and dull, lifted a little with a clean badger; they need silvery touches when dry. Added three cows beyond the willows and a white stork on the near bank.
- day 39, 12:28: Day 39. Silvery stipple on the willows' upper-left clumps brought back the willow character up close, but in the whole view they now read almost as white blossom, spring not summer; a light green glaze needed. Foreground flowers: yarrow, daisies, buttercups, a few red clover and cornflowers, grass stalks with seed heads; subtle, only visible up close, as intended. Boulder: a dark umber/smalt glaze made it chocolate; a cool scumble into the wet glaze then covered the left half too strongly, so it reads as a two-tone blob, snow on top. Lesson: stop working it wet. Let it dry fully and build it with thin passes.
- day 55, 14:29: Day 55. Boulder finally: dry cool veil to unify, then a transparent smalt/umber/black glaze graded by the form's value and toward the ground; the glaze sits in the weave and reads as granite grain. Warm stipple on the upper-left ridge, grass tufts dark and mid over its foot (a first row of light blades made a yellow fringe). Willows given a light green glaze, now summer silver-green. A post-and-rail fence receding across the shaded meadow on the right; finials on the three spires, eave and ridge lines on the naves; a small flock with shepherd, crook and dog in the far fields. Mistake: the red dress glaze used the skirt polygon, which runs up under the shawl, so the shawl is streaked red again; restate it when dry. Also a pale smear where the ditch ends, at x~335.
- day 61, 02:45: Day 61. Ditch end tapered with converging grass banks and a clump of rushes instead of the hard cut. Shawl restated clean in cream over the dry red, with shade on the right, fringe along its point and a fold. Faint windrows of cut hay between the haycocks on the right, so that field reads as being mown. I think the picture is complete: town, plain, figures, detail at every distance. Letting everything dry thoroughly before a light mastic varnish.
- day 106, 02:48: Day 106. After 45 days everything was touch-dry; a light mastic varnish (0.25 coats) laid over the whole. It warms and binds the greens and grays the sky a little, as his pictures look now. Finished: 'Summer Evening on the Meadows before the Town'.
