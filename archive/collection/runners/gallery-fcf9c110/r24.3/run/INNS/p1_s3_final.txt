I've finished the painting and given it the title **October Dusk, the Pond Meadow**.

The time is about twenty minutes after sundown. A lemon glow fades upward into violet-gray over a hazy far tree line, where a small farmhouse sends up a thread of smoke. A pale pond in the middle distance holds the last of that light. On the right a grove of elms stands dark against the glow, with the bright sky showing through a gap under the main crown. Across the dark, glazed meadow a worn path leads to a small woman in a red shawl near the water. She is the one warm note in the picture and gives it its scale.

In this last visit I softened the crowns' scalloped edges by dragging thinned dark paint out across them. I also glazed the bottom of the meadow and the field past her darker, so the patch of light around her and the near shore is the brightest part of the field.

Two things went wrong. A glaze I brushed over the whole grove left horizontal stripes, and I took almost all of it back off with a rag dipped in spirits. A rag wiped over some stray streaks in the sky right of the elm barely lifted them, because that paint had already dried.

The crown edges still don't dissolve into the sky the way Inness's would. But I'm happy with the place: the light caught twice, in the sky and the water, and the small figure walking toward it.
