## Composition and why

A ploughed field rises from the painter's feet to a low, rounded crest.
The crest dips into a saddle left of center and climbs to a shoulder on
the right. Over the saddle, three or four kilometers off, a small Baltic
town stands in morning mist: a slim spire (Jacobi), the tall tower with
its bulbs and needle (Nikolai), the squat tower (Marien), naves, a low
line of roofs and a windmill out on the flat to the right. A cart track
comes in from the right edge, two ruts with a grass hump and a little
standing water, and winds up to the saddle toward the town. Crows work
the furrows. Beside the track sits a heap of gathered field stones with
long morning shadows. The sun is low and off the canvas to the left, and
the whole sky over the town is one quiet, pale gradient with a few thin
level streaks. The warmth sits low on the left.

What it takes from Friedrich (from notes/research and the workshop
notebook, not from pictures):
- The motif of a ploughed field on a rise with a town's silhouette low
  on the horizon beyond. Friedrich painted his home town's skyline from
  the meadows more than once, and a field with crows under a big sky. This
  is my own arrangement of those things: morning instead of evening, a
  saddle and track, the Greifswald towers as I know them in words.
- A low horizon (eye level 63% down) under a big quiet sky, and a dark
  near stage. The one lightest band is the glow along the horizon, where
  the town stands.
- Particular things instead of an empty foreground: the track, the stone
  heap (the stones every Pomeranian farmer picks off his field), the
  crows. The notebook says every earlier painter named these and none
  painted them.
- No figure from behind, no dead oak, no moon. The notebook says free
  painters kept converging on those, so I stayed away from them on
  purpose.
- Furrows radiate to a vanishing point just right of the town. The eye
  runs up the furrows and the track to the town. The stones on the right
  and the crows on the left keep that from being a bull's-eye.

## Working method, stage by stage

1. **Ground**: `Style::friedrich()` as bought (red-brown knifed lower
   layers, a brushed top layer).
2. **sky**: the atmos `Sky` for a sun at azimuth −58°, 5° up, with haze
   and a thin haze layer, and a stratus deck of long level streaks. I
   quieted its color 30% toward its own grey, the way a painter mixes a
   sky from lead white, smalt and a touch of ochre instead of copying the
   blue, and added a slow ±3% banding along the horizontal. The sky went
   on in long level arcs of thin paint, fused with the badger and
   stippled into the wet. I waited an hour and a half, laid the cloud
   streaks level into the still-open paint and fused them. When it was
   dry I added a finer stipple, denser toward the glow on the left.
3. **far**: the lowland between horizon and crest (and a village church
   far off on the right): pale land, woods a step
   darker in unequal stretches, all heavily veiled. The town is one firm
   shape per building, cut in with a small sable in vertical touches, its
   color going to the mist color toward its foot. The windmill sails are
   four drags. Then the mist goes over the lowland and the town's foot in
   thin level strokes, wet, and gets fused.
4. **field**: the dead color. Loam laid along the furrow direction, which
   I computed per point from the world (ground point, then the furrow's
   heading projected). Four depth bands, each with a brush sized to its
   furrows (7 units near, 1.5 at the crest). The color is darker near and
   cooler and paler up the rise, and the furrow banding is already in it
   where furrows come too close for a line each.
5. **furrows**: every furrow is traced on the ground (X solved for each
   depth) and projected. The trough goes in as long dark pulls, a new
   stroke where the hand would run out. The lit face follows as broken
   runs of a filbert, warm where the sun takes it and occasionally cool
   where the sky light lies on it. Brush width comes from the furrow's
   size at that depth, so the brush shrinks up the rise. Lit faces are
   skipped inside the stones' shadows.
6. **track**: the lateral offset from the track's middle is a per-pixel
   field. From it come the verges (packed earth, greyer), the ruts
   (darker) and the grass hump, laid along the track by depth band.
   The ruts are then drawn as long dark pulls along their length, like
   the furrows. The standing water's edges are fused into the rut with a
   small badger. The hump gets fine upturned strokes of dry grass in
   clumps, clipped to the near part.
   Standing water in a few stretches of the ruts mirrors the sky from
   2·HZ − y, darkened.
7. **clods**: sparse, clustered stipple of dark hollows and a few lit
   tops on the near bands.
8. **stones**: a heap of eight ellipsoid/block bodies at the track side and
   one broken block half sunk near the lower frame, placed on the ground with
   `World::place` and lit by the world's sun, painted with
   `rocks::paint_solid`. Their shadows aren't a separate glaze. They are
   part of the earth's own color function (`stone_shadow`) and so went on
   with the dead color and the furrows.
8b. **crest**: the ploughed crest against the mist broken into low,
   uneven lumps (skipped here and there), each colored from the paint
   just below it with `c.sample`.
9. **crows**: six birds, each written with a pointed round: the body
   pulled tail to breast with rising pressure, head, beak, legs and a
   sheen of sky along the back. Sized by `spot.s` for their depth.
10. **cranes**: a loose skein of seventeen cranes high on the right,
    each a wing-body-wing stroke of a pointed round with a swell in the
    middle and a thin neck. This is an October detail, and it gives the
    empty right half of the sky something to do.
11. Final veil: a thin umber glaze darker toward the foot and the lower
    corners. Then aged varnish and cracks, made finer and given a grain.

## FRICTION

(numbered in order found; the top five are listed in the final reply)

1. **The Xcode license broke linking mid-session.** `cc` began failing
   with exit 69 ("You have not agreed to the Xcode license agreements").
   Workaround: `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Library/Developer/CommandLineTools` for
   every cargo call. This is the environment, not the engine, but it
   stops a painter cold.
2. **No stroke along a ground-plane direction.** `Handling::angle` takes
   a canvas angle. To lay strokes along furrows, a track or anything else
   lying on the ground, I wrote `along(x, y)`: to_ground, then step along
   the heading in the world, then project both points and take atan2.
   That's twenty lines each time, and it runs a ray march per stroke. A
   `World::ground_angle(x, y, heading)` would be the natural tool.
3. **Stroke size doesn't follow depth.** A handling has one tool, so a
   receding field needs its mask cut into depth bands with a handling per
   band. The seams between bands are where the brush size jumps. The
   notebook already says "the stroke size still never changed with
   depth"; the engine offers no `width_at(x, y)`.
4. **A single long curve painted with many brushes.** Furrows are
   hundreds of long lines receding in perspective, which `work`'s random
   placement can't make. So I wrote drags: 140-odd furrows, each cut into
   pieces with its own tool width. `drag` is serial and needs a new
   `Held` or a swapped `held.tool` per width. It was fast enough (7 s),
   but none of the handling's hand-like character comes with it (drift,
   dips, reload by fullness), so I had to reinvent pressure, swell and
   gaps.
5. **Crest line per column is mine to compute.** `World` knows the ground
   but not "where the ground's silhouette is against what's behind it".
   `view.sky()` would give it, but only with a full View, and a View
   builds ground-cast for every pixel. I marched each column myself
   (`crest_y`).
6. **Handlings whose color and angle come from the ground are slow.**
   The "track" stage takes about 60 s and "field" about 34 s. That's not
   the masks: a resume from a later stage, which still builds every mask
   between stages, takes 6 s. My color and angle closures call
   `to_ground`, a 72-step ray march plus bisection, and a handling calls
   them per stroke (and more for aiming and fill). I suspect that is the
   cost, but I didn't profile it. A cached ground-point field (X, Z per
   pixel) that closures could sample would make ground-aware painting
   cheap.
7. **Checkpoints go stale from helper edits anywhere.** Editing helper
   functions outside `main` (the crow, the stone list, the town's
   shapes) made the sky stale, as the model says. I used
   `--resume sky --stale-ok --ckpt` when I knew the sky hadn't changed.
   That's the reflex override the notebook warns about, but the
   alternative was 77 s per iteration on the stage I wasn't touching.
8. **Glaring perspective pieces I had to build myself:** a track as a
   lateral-offset field, since `ribbon` gives a shape but no "distance
   from the middle" to put ruts in, and furrow lines solved per depth.
9. **Puddles/rut water: level strokes of sky color read as "road
   markings".** The same fault as the notebook's shore touches. Darkening
   them helped. A proper fix wants the water's own dark with the sky only
   in a thin grazing gleam.
10. **Crows with a round brush are pills with beaks.** Uniform-width
   drags have round caps. A pointed round (`Tool::point`), whose width
   follows pressure, let the body taper from breast to tail.

11. **Crop checkpoints are a separate chain.** `--crop … --resume clods`
    refused because crops keep their own checkpoints. Resuming the whole
    canvas from a late stage turned out to take 6 s, so I stopped using
    crops for the late stages and only cropped the PNG to look. For early
    stages, which are the slow ones, a crop still has to repaint from the
    start.
12. **Changing brush size mid-work means swapping `held.tool`.** A `Held`
    is built for one tool. I reassigned `held.tool` per piece to get
    width by depth. It seems to work, but nothing documents whether the
    bristle set built for the old width stays valid for the new one.
13. **A crest serration first came out as a row of nails.** Pointed,
    upright touches at the furrow spacing made evenly spaced spikes, the
    notebook's sawtooth. Low, broad, skipped lumps colored from the paint
    just below (`c.sample`) finally sat in the crest. That's not engine
    friction, but it shows `c.sample` as the right tool for "take the
    paint from beside it".

## Critique

What works. The composition reads at once: dark ground, a rise, a
saddle, the town in the lightest band, a big quiet sky. The furrows do
the drawing. They fan out from just behind the crest and carry the eye
into the saddle, and the rise reads as a rise because the brush and the
spacing shrink up the slope. The town silhouette (spire, bulbed tower,
squat tower, naves) is the one firm, found shape in the picture. Its
foot dissolves into mist laid wet over it, which is where I wanted the
picture's tension. The sky is calm and thin, with no bare ground
showing, and its few streaks sit in it instead of on it. The finer,
grained craquelure no longer competes with the town.

What doesn't.
- The **foreground** is better than an empty snowfield, but it's still
  mostly one kind of mark repeated: dark trough, broken lit face and a
  scatter of clods, furrow after furrow. Late on I added a half-sunk
  broken stone by the lower frame and drifts of small field stones with
  their own short shadows. They help at full size and barely register at
  a glance: at viewing size the pebbles are pale specks. Stubble and
  couch grass at the headland are still missing. At full size the lit
  faces still read a little like brushed bars and not quite like turned
  earth.
- The **right half of the field** in the middle distance goes smooth and
  streaky. It's correct in perspective, since the furrows there run
  nearly across the view, but it's dull. In the last pass I enlarged the
  stone heap and brought it nearer to hold that side. It now weighs
  enough, but it sits in the darkened corner and reads as dark masses
  with speckled lit rims (the speckle is `paint_solid`'s accents over
  small stones), more lumps than granite. The picture still leans left.
- The **track** reads as a track since the ruts were drawn and the hump
  got grass. The few puddles are still flat pale slips, though. Only
  darkening them made them tolerable.
- The **crows** read as crows, just, mostly because they're pecking.
  Close up they are black silhouettes with stick legs, not birds with
  weight.
- The **sky** is quiet, and quiet can tip into the "correct gradient"
  the notebook warns about. Its structure is faint. I chose calm over
  the risk of cloud cut-outs. One streak on the right is still a little
  too white and edged, and a viewer may find the upper left empty.
- The **lowland** band is thin (the crest is only about 15–30 units
  below the horizon). Its woods are a faint line, and the right-hand
  horizon is a straight crest edge against mist with little happening.
- I enlarged the **town** by a quarter late on, and it now holds the
  glow. Its towers are the one firm found edge in the picture. The line
  of roofs between them is nearly lost in the mist, which is right for
  the morning but leaves the churches looking a little like they're
  standing alone.
- The **cranes** read as a skein of birds at viewing distance. Close up
  they're ticks. They are the least Friedrich thing in the picture and
  the one I'm least sure of.
- The strong umber veil over the lower corners came out with a fine
  wrinkled texture in raking relief at full size (visible under the stone
  heap). It reads as crusted earth there, but I didn't intend it and
  haven't traced its cause.
