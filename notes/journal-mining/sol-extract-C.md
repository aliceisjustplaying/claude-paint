# Lessons from batch C: painting, repairs and repeated sittings

The strongest recurring lesson is to test a laid mark under the passage's actual conditions, then preserve what made it work: guessed values, similar-looking piles and generic finishing glazes repeatedly triggered repairs (35c488:106; 49b647:10; r31-smoke:30). Broad repaints often removed seams, but later engine 5 sittings also found successful local repairs with thinner, original recipes or interlocking dry hatches (d95ea6:74; 49b647:57; r31-smoke:73). Blending could make reflections convincing or destroy form and texture; the useful distinction is the passage's intended effect and what wet paint the blender can reach (12e572:10; aef22d:29; r31-smoke:69).

These are journal observations and the painters' judgments, not an independent assessment of images or physical accuracy. Numerical values and drying times below are reported observations, not measurements made for this extraction. The summary, **Lesson** sentences and final candidate lines are my extraction interpretations; **Evidence** records the journal observations supporting them. Engine labels and known rounds come from [batch-C.txt](batch-C.txt); unknown rounds remain unknown. No journal here reports an absorbent-ground experiment: the ground lessons concern the primed grounds actually described below.

## Ground and drawing

### 1. Choose the ground for the surface you intend to leave visible

- **Lesson:** Check the prepared ground before planning broken strokes that depend on its weave or tone.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2; 762 mm canvas, linen 26/22, knife grounds 140 + 70 µm; engine 5 examples use warm primed grounds.
- **Evidence:** The still-life painter found “no weave relief to break strokes over,” contrary to the darker, thinner ground intended (ba1453:2–5). Thin paint over warm ground later read olive or pink (35c488:5). Neither observation isolates a universal thickness cutoff.
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:53–58 explains knife leveling; T:28–30 and S:14–15 explain toned grounds showing through. The immediate setup check is less explicit.
- **Still valid?** Mechanisms remain documented. The exact two-layer result is engine 2 evidence; do not generalize its thicknesses to every canvas.

### 2. Keep drawing light where the first paint will remain thin

- **Lesson:** Treat visible underdrawing as part of a thin passage and plan body paint where a firm construction line must disappear.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2; pale primed ground, 2H/HB underdrawing; engine 5 r28 used ruled 2B.
- **Evidence:** Low-pressure pencil was a “faint dotted line” (b08dc3:2). Another painter planned to hide oak construction beneath dark paint (3a958c:1). Erased square windows left ghosts after being redrawn tall (002de5:4); that journal has no later confirmation of their appearance under paint.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: G:457–461 and S:172–175 describe ghosts, sealing and thin versus body coverage.
- **Still valid?** Documented; the journals do not establish that erasing gives a clean ground.

### 3. Fix the silhouette before adding fine branches

- **Lesson:** Inspect the trunk and main limbs at detail scale before adding a twig network that cannot repair their underlying shape.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; birch trunks, oak limbs, `body_of`, ribbons and pointed gestures.
- **Evidence:** A twig mesh improved a tree but could not “untaper a limb that was drawn as a bar” (f99282:38). A birch silhouette wandered from x88 to x107 and was replaced with a many-point ribbon (35c488:116). Engine 5 angular secondaries read better than sinuous “snakes”; random crown-annulus sprigs read as floating fluff (d95ea6:10–13).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:91–94 addresses unified forms; G:416–440 describes adjustable outline character. The before-detail silhouette check is covered generally by B:37.
- **Still valid?** The process lesson survives. “`body_of` has a bad silhouette” is an engine 2 diagnosis, not a current universal rule; G exposes character controls.

## Color mixing

### 4. Judge mixtures from a laid trial, not arithmetic or the held pile

- **Lesson:** Compare candidate mixes laid with the intended handling on the intended substrate before expanding the winning trial.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; opaque and thin films, light and dark substrates, palette and held-pile views.
- **Evidence:** An engine 2 value ladder initially read the pale ground through unfilled gaps rather than opaque paint (35c488:106). A held pile looked close but accents over dark glass went on too light (7c4dd1:26). Reports even disagree on the direction of guessing error: near-black grays in 35c488:91 versus overly light still-life piles in ba1453:81–83. A universal “mix darker” correction would lose that distinction.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: B:39 explicitly requires a laid trial and distinguishes thick paint on steel; G:652 does too. T:105–109 supplies palette inspection but is less specific about substrate.
- **Still valid?** Supported across engines; exact arithmetic offsets are unreliable journal heuristics.

### 5. Recover the original recipe and handling when matching an old passage

- **Lesson:** Retrieve the recipe actually used from the log and match its handling before choosing a lookalike pile for a repair.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5; dry glazed stack, field hatch, flat architectural fields; original versus aerial, white-rich copies.
- **Evidence:** Distant-stack piles made a pale pink skirt on the main stack; re-knifing the original darker recipe matched (r31-smoke:30–31). The log's field recipe removed a rectangle seam in a later sitting (r31-smoke:43–45). An exact original pile matched a real-edged hem in one coat; an approximate mix left a value step (49b647:52,57).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:16–17 makes the log authoritative and G:29 exposes it. B:39 asks painters to carry conditions forward but does not explicitly connect repair matching to the recorded recipe.
- **Still valid?** Repeated engine 5 support; matching the recipe alone does not guarantee matching thickness, substrate or texture.

### 6. Keep white subordinate where hue differences must survive

- **Lesson:** Test whether added white preserves the intended differences between neighboring color piles before using them for broken color.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5; water piles with lead white 1.2–1.8 parts, roughly two parts color; engine 2 dark foliage and pale scumbles.
- **Evidence:** Four water piles converged to one pale gray-blue rather than retaining hue differences (12e572:8,23). High-white opaque transitions hid thin frond passages as cream slabs (aef22d:24). In the earlier grove, a nominal light foliage rim became opaque pale khaki (33a5bf:24).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: T:129–130 warns about chalky white; S:32–35 distinguishes a white veil from a tint. These journals add concrete failed conditions.
- **Still valid?** Supported, but the journal's ≤0.6-part recommendation is recipe-specific, not a transferable absolute amount or ratio.

### 7. Test thin dark and earth marks against the actual pale field

- **Lesson:** Check the laid hue and value of a thin dark or earth line over light paint before repeating it as twigs, stalks or texture.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; warm grounds, pale sky or snow, narrow rigger lines; umber, smalt, black and ochre mixtures.
- **Evidence:** Fine branches dried warmer and lighter than their dark pile (d95ea6:19). A raw-umber/ochre grass tuft read as saturated yellow-ochre on snow and was lifted (d95ea6:90–92). Earlier “neutral” umber/smalt mixtures became warm mauve (35c488:108). The painter's strength-ratio explanation is not an isolated experiment.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes for the material effect: S:27–31. Partly for explicitly testing the line: B:39.
- **Still valid?** Current notes support thin-film shifts; no universal cool-gray recipe follows from these examples.

## Thin vs thick paint

### 8. Diagnose hiding, gaps and film thickness separately

- **Lesson:** When a cover fails, inspect whether it lacks hiding pigment, leaves stroke gaps or lays too thin a film before increasing coverage again.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 lean broad/glaze passes and repeated dark covers; engine 5 turps lay-ins and pale window blinds.
- **Evidence:** “Three separate calls” first seemed to cure gray covers (35c488:67), then the painter revised this to opaque pigments (35c488:80) and finally pressed paint with fill rather than repeated light passes (35c488:110,130). Engine 5 lay-ins improved when the second pass used fill and higher coverage (49b647:4); two pale coats covered dry glass (49b647:5–6,17).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:164–168 distinguishes hiding, G:321 defines coverage and G:331 explains fill. The diagnostic distinction is not presented as one sequence.
- **Still valid?** The distinction is useful interpretation. Engine 2 claims that coverage 10 equals coverage 3 or that transparent paint can never hide should not become current laws; the guide promises neither.

### 9. Use fill according to the passage's intended surface

- **Lesson:** Inspect a filled trial before covering a large field, because closing gaps may introduce a dab pattern or flatten the passage.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; `fill=true`, opaque paint, broad fields versus reflections or pads.
- **Evidence:** Engine 2 fill left offset rows of oval “wallpaper” dabs (35c488:112,132). Engine 5 fill plus blending made lavender poster slabs (12e572:9,21). Conversely, fill plus clip gave suitable clean architectural fields (49b647:18) and a good graded straw stack (r31-smoke:4).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:331 and S:75–77 explain gap filling but do not state this aesthetic tradeoff; B:39 requires trials.
- **Still valid?** Both failure and success persist in engine 5. Neither “always fill” nor “never fill” is supported.

### 10. Test fluid same-color paint when softening dry impasto

- **Lesson:** Trial a fluid coat of the same color to soften a dry crust when reduced relief is the intended effect.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 smoke scratch; recreated sun disc on sky, dried 60 days; filbert 6, medium 0.4 versus 0.65, lay 3 versus 2; later coats aged 40 days.
- **Evidence:** The knife caught ridge tops and left raised slivers; medium 0.65 leveled better than 0.4, with a second coat smoother again (r31-smoke:53–56). One transferred coat softened the sun without a normal-color change (r31-smoke:57). The later sitting accepted it as sufficiently smooth (r31-smoke:68).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:261–278 and T:144–164 explain fluid leveling and a knife riding dry ridges. This repair comparison is absent.
- **Still valid?** One scratch comparison plus one painting use; the two-coat advantage remains a hypothesis. A fluid poplar cover later destroyed desired brush texture, so this is not a general relief-removal recipe (r31-smoke:63–64).

## Blending and edges

### 11. Inspect after one blend before repeating it

- **Lesson:** Start with one blend and inspect whether it has already lifted, averaged or flattened more paint than the passage needs.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2–5; lean wet films, contrasting layers, sparse body hatch and stipple.
- **Evidence:** A blender killed dark foliage clumps (33a5bf:5) and thinned a tree into pale ghosts (ea8319:1). The smoke painter repeatedly concluded that “one pass is plenty” after straw and eave losses (r31-smoke:3,22). A fresh sparse narrow hatch became a buff slab despite earlier warnings (r31-smoke:67–69).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:314 quantifies lifting and S:131–136 records loss of touches and weave exposure. An inspect-after-one instruction is not explicit.
- **Still valid?** Current guide supports strong lifting. The journals also contain successful multiple-pass blends, so one pass is an inspection checkpoint, not a universal maximum (12e572:10; aef22d:43).

### 12. Protect dry neighbors from paint moved by a blender

- **Lesson:** Exclude passages to preserve from the blend's reachable area even when those passages are already dry.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5; wet water or cloud paint beside dry pads, sky reflection and willow masses.
- **Evidence:** A whole-canvas blend dragged wet blue across dry sky and pads (12e572:3). A rectangle containing a willow veiled its dry reflection with pale cloud paint (aef22d:18). A later restricted cloud blend kept far pads out and improved the sky band (12e572:26).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: S:126–130 explains transport and G:340–346 explains clipping. Dry neighbors being safe from pickup is easily mistaken for safety from incoming wet paint.
- **Still valid?** Repeated engine 5 observations; dryness protects the old layer from lifting, not from being covered.

### 13. Put blend boundaries where the resulting paint can meet its neighbor

- **Lesson:** End a blend at a natural passage boundary or matching color rather than assuming a softened rectangle will hide a seam.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2, 3 and 5; local rect blends, strong wet contrasts, blurred masks.
- **Evidence:** A blurred rectangle still dragged a gray skirt into fog (3a958c:25). Engine 3 rectangles churned contrast into gray boxes (24be5f:17,20). Engine 5 soft-mask blends left hard boxes, while directional strokes plus restricted blending later repaired reflection boundaries (aef22d:26,43–48,61–62).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes for seams: S:124–130. Partly for the practical choice of matching-value endpoints.
- **Still valid?** Supported across engines. “Never blend contrasting paint” is too broad: deliberate wet edge fusion improved the grove in 24be5f:15–16 and reflections in aef22d:61–62.

### 14. Separate edge character from protection of nearby shapes

- **Lesson:** Check a pass's actual overrun and exclusion masks before using soft or lost edge handling near finished incident.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 long broad/glaze/body strokes; engine 5 hatching near a dry distant-stack silhouette.
- **Evidence:** Soft/lost handling repeatedly buried objects, including an engine 2 claim that it escaped even `clip=true` (ba1453:201–202,220). Blurred-mask clipping looked soft in one early report (b08dc3:2), but a later oak repair described a crisp boundary near mask value 0.5 (3a958c:17). Smoke field hatch overshot and nibbled the stack rim (r31-smoke:43–45); a separate wipe restored the dry silhouette.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: G:340–358 and T:183–185 describe overrun and clipping; S:62–74 distinguishes mask shape from stroke extent.
- **Still valid?** Unclipped overrun is current. The engine 2 assertion that `edge` defeats `clip=true` conflicts with the current guide's promise that clipping stops every bristle; retain it as historical evidence, not current API advice. Current weighted clipping (G:327; S:62–64) does not support a universal claim that all softened masks become crisp at 0.5.

### 15. Trial interlocking dry marks when a seam survives blending

- **Lesson:** Try overlapping marks of both neighboring recipes over dry paint when blending or glazing repeatedly restores a hard seam.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 smoke; dry stubble/shadow and warm/lilac haze; haze filbert 3, coverage 0.7–0.8, load 0.45, pressure 0.3–0.55, noise edges 14–20 units.
- **Evidence:** Opposite-color hatching broke a band-like cast shadow and integrated it with stubble (r31-smoke:12–19). Two haze repairs with blend/glaze failed (r31-smoke:67); interlocking dry hatches removed the step and held at the look-only return (r31-smoke:73,76,88).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:118–128 describes separate broken color and dry-layer marks; this seam repair is absent.
- **Still valid?** Repeated within one engine 5 painting. Interpretation: use where the passage already accepts dab or hatch texture, not as a guarantee for smooth skies.

## Glazing/scumbling

### 16. Test the actual veil before trusting the hand name or medium amount

- **Lesson:** Trial a glaze or scumble at the intended contrast, load and stroke size before treating it as a harmless atmospheric veil.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2–5; white-rich scumbles, saturated cadmium/madder glazes, dry flat or thin broken passages.
- **Evidence:** Medium 0.7 cadmium/madder glazes made lemon and magenta bars (12e572:5). A later violet retry failed, then lower load and medium 0.75 with a different pigment balance gave transparent shadows (12e572:33). Low-load white scumbles made opaque spots on architectural fields (49b647:10–11). Engine 2 medium-rich tree glaze went near-opaque dark (496bb9:7).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:170–179 names transparent pigments and thin scumbles; S:32–35 warns about white veils. B:39 already supplies the required trial.
- **Still valid?** Repeated engine 5 support. “Very little pigment” in recipes given as relative parts is the painter's phrasing, not proof of absolute dilution; recipes, medium and load changed together.

### 17. Consider thinner for a dry pale scar in a thin blended field

- **Lesson:** Test a thinner-based tint on a dry pale scar before replacing it with another opaque patch.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 oak sky; original sky mix with thinner 0.6 then 0.35, light filbert 4/6 passes, small grown masks and a small blend; engine 5 willow veils used thinner 0.55.
- **Evidence:** After opaque sky repairs repeatedly failed, four thin passes removed a long-accepted pale rectangle at full view without a new slab (d95ea6:74,79). The next sitting confirmed it remained a soft cloudlet (d95ea6:85). Thinner veils also integrated willow-foot passages, though they sometimes changed a seam too little (aef22d:30,37,43).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:179–208 explains thin-film deposition and evaporation. The successful reversal of the “no local sky patch” rule is absent.
- **Still valid?** Supported engine 5 example; it tints rather than removes existing dry relief or guarantees an invisible patch.

### 18. Match a glaze repair to the texture you want to retain

- **Lesson:** Repair glaze caught in unwanted furrows with a tested body recipe when a fluid leveling coat would erase desirable brush texture.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 smoke poplars; recreated body paint plus gold glaze/blend, dry 60 days; three covers aged 40 days.
- **Evidence:** Fluid medium 0.65 paint covered the crackle but became pale airbrushed lilac; violet glaze made worse veins; original body paint at coverage 1.2 and load 0.5 covered bands while keeping texture (r31-smoke:62–66). The next return found only faint close-up crackle, acceptable at full view (r31-smoke:89).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:170–174 explains pooling in valleys. The contrasting repair choices are absent.
- **Still valid?** One reproduced scratch defect plus painting use and later check. The glaze mechanism is documented; the winning recipe is passage-specific.

### 19. Treat a unifying glaze as a full campaign with consequences

- **Lesson:** Require a visible purpose and a small successful trial before adding an overall finishing glaze to passages that already work.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 medium 0.28–0.35 overall glazes; engine 3/5 narrow and whole-region glazes.
- **Evidence:** A still-life glaze put pale curls over everything and forced rebuilding (ba1453:140–143). A sky-deepening glaze dragged recently repaired wood and cliff paint into flags (3a958c:15). Smoke narrow haze glazes pooled, and the last sitting rejected another glow after checking the whole picture (r31-smoke:67,92).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: B:35–39 requires campaign inspection and trials. T:170–174 describes glazing benefits without this repeated finishing trap.
- **Still valid?** The process caution survives; do not turn engine 2 crust at medium 0.35 into a current prohibition on medium. Successful foreground glaze is also recorded (r31-smoke:7).

## Drying and timing

### 20. Check the entire route before relying on dryness

- **Lesson:** Sample drying at several relevant points before wiping a path or laying pale paint across a dark passage.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 mixed-age branch paths, tacky dark glass and white-rich blinds; engine 2 repaired limbs and nearby glazing.
- **Evidence:** A rag still lifted a pale track on a supposedly dry path because only one point had been checked (d95ea6:11,29). Pale blinds on tacky glass broke into stick-slip patches; the dry-layer second coat covered (49b647:5–6,17). A glaze crossing four-hour-old limbs and a one-hour-old cliff dragged both (3a958c:15).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:601–615 defines pointwise drying states and T:178–179 says dry under glazes. Multiple-point checking is not explicit there.
- **Still valid?** Direct engine 5 support. Setting/tacky can preserve some separate marks but does not guarantee clean pale coverage or safe lifting.

### 21. Carry observed drying states forward instead of a fixed wait rule

- **Lesson:** Record the layer's material and observed drying state and recheck it before reuse rather than applying one journal's day count everywhere.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 oily white glaze; engine 5 thin turps films, low-medium body coats and earth/black details.
- **Evidence:** Oily white glaze stayed tacky for five weeks (f99282:22). A turps field dried in roughly 33 hours (aef22d:2), while low-medium body coats over previous layers took 10–17 days (7c4dd1:6,14), and earth/black details took 10–20 days (49b647:21,49). The journal descriptions do not isolate all thickness and pigment differences.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes in principle: G:581–615 explains the simulated clock and varying drying times; B:17–19 does too.
- **Still valid?** State-dependent checking is documented. These are simulated times reported under different conditions, not current universal constants or real elapsed work durations.

## Corrections and lifting

### 22. Lift a failed fresh trial before it sets when the underlayer is dry

- **Lesson:** Test spirit-rag lifting while a failed overlay is still open and confirm the dry layer survives before treating it as a practical reversal.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engines 3 and 5; fresh thin paint over dry sky, flat fields and turps lay-ins; often dip 0.8–1.0, pressure 0.7–0.95, repeated passes.
- **Evidence:** Engine 3 wet grass lozenges lifted while the dry layer survived (24be5f:21). Engine 5 fresh willow paint came almost completely off the dry lay-in (aef22d:4); failed shadow-wall glaze also left almost no trace (7c4dd1:9). A thicker medium-free water layer lifted poorly with rag or knife (12e572:8), limiting the word “undo.”
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: G:476–512 explains rag lifting, saturation and the set-layer limit; B:13–14 mentions lifting. The comparative thickness limitation is journal-specific.
- **Still valid?** Repeated engine 5 support; “always comes off” and “perfect eraser” in some entries overstate the evidence.

### 23. Expect rag streaks, halos and transport near crisp edges

- **Lesson:** Inspect the wipe's edge and neighboring shapes after lifting, using small or fresh rags when transported paint would be costly.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 dark branches beside light sky, architectural frames and sparse hatch; fresh versus refolded rags.
- **Evidence:** About 25 fresh high-pressure dipped-rag passes removed a sky accident but left a faint veil (d95ea6:14,26). A facade wipe smeared residue onto dry frames and sills (49b647:10). Smoke wipes left an end streak later covered with original-direction strokes (r31-smoke:30,32). A gentle wipe integrated sparse flecks but barely changed denser body marks (r31-smoke:23–25,73,76).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: G:479–500 describes residue, smear, folds and saturated cloth. Partly for selecting the rag treatment by texture.
- **Still valid?** Documented. Fresh-rag superiority is local evidence, not grounds to discard all refolding; gentle partial lifting is one-trial evidence.

### 24. Restrict a carve to the paint being replaced

- **Lesson:** Build a carve from the old painted shape outside the new outline, then check that it excludes fine incident and unrelated background.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 oak narrowing; opaque sky in a bounding rectangle; old trunk and main-limb masks but unprotected fine twigs.
- **Evidence:** A rectangle-minus-kept-trunk mask buried twigs and the sky gradient; lifting took roughly 25 fresh-rag passes (d95ea6:14). The handoff explicitly changed the carve to “old paint ∩ outside the new outline” (d95ea6:25). Later correct narrow path restatement avoided background damage (d95ea6:60–62).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:392 documents mask operations and T:183–185 describes clipping. B:39 covers trials, not this replacement-mask construction.
- **Still valid?** Repeated geometry distinction, independent of drying behavior; the exact carve still needs a checked old-shape mask.

## Detail and texture

### 25. Match stroke size to the shape instead of repeating a lozenge

- **Lesson:** When a mark looks like a blob or lozenge, compare stroke length and brush width with the passage's dimensions before changing color again.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 90 × 12-unit mist shapes versus 20–60-unit body strokes; 40-unit still-life objects versus 120–300-unit glaze strokes; engine 5 flat-field scumbles.
- **Evidence:** Smaller 4–14-unit detail strokes replaced mist lozenges with fine broken tone (f99282:60). Detail marks plus one wet directional smear then improved the far-shore reflection (f99282:68–70). Long glaze strokes combed a small still-life form (ba1453:91–93). Engine 5 field scumbles likewise remained isolated patches (49b647:10–11).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:306–314 lists default scales and T:62–69 asks for a mark hierarchy. The diagnostic size comparison is absent.
- **Still valid?** Default scales are still documented. Smaller strokes can fix a scale mismatch, not every value, opacity or texture problem.

### 26. Replace repeated ticks with marks that follow and cross the form

- **Lesson:** Trial overlapping form-direction strokes when evenly spaced marks along an edge read as beads, teeth or a fence.
- **Kind:** craft.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; roughly two-unit lit slivers, bottle highlights, roof straw ticks and narrow reflections.
- **Evidence:** Thin clipped birch lights became beads (35c488:118); repeated narrow bottle highlights did too (ba1453:123–126). On smoke scratch, pointed strands became fangs and blunt evenly spaced strands stayed a fence; a roof-direction skirt above a sagging eave worked (r31-smoke:28–31). The distant stack's ticks remained acceptable at its smaller viewing scale (r31-smoke:35).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: S:78–83 and S:119–120 describe dashed bands and beaded lines; T:68–69 says follow the form.
- **Still valid?** Supported engine 5 example. The fence rule remains a hypothesis, with scale-dependent exceptions explicitly preserved.

### 27. Keep lily-pad marks distinct from reflection blending

- **Lesson:** Build pads with loose straight horizontal touches and water gaps, leaving them unblended when their separate surface marks are the intended effect.
- **Kind:** craft.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 lily ponds; dry base, flat brush, near-straight strokes, blot 0.15, coverage about 0.8, blue rim slits.
- **Evidence:** Curved pad marks looked like coins or hamburgers; straight unblended marks with water between finally read as pad islands (aef22d:28–29,39). The other pond's loose rafts read better than solid bases, and blending inside a pad made a flat disc (12e572:13,20,30). Later warm olive touches integrated, while pink touches read as flowers (aef22d:53–56).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:68–69 and T:131–134 cover form direction and varied touch; the pad/reflection distinction is absent.
- **Still valid?** Confirmed across two engine 5 ponds, with later pad trials. It is manner-specific advice, not a requirement for every painted leaf.

### 28. Use controlled gestures and directional blending for reflected foliage

- **Lesson:** Trial broader frond strokes with controlled wobble and a restricted blend in their direction when fine tapered lines read as drips or pipes.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5; one failed wobble 6, pressure tail 0.05; successful filbert strokes commonly load 0.4–0.75, pressure about 0.55 tapering toward 0.2, then vertical badger.
- **Evidence:** High-wobble willow lines left pale furrows and dripping tails; a vertical blend rescued the mass, and lower-wobble gestures later read as reflection (12e572:2,4). Separate frond trials yielded outlines, pipes or beads before loaded filbert strokes plus a vertical blend worked (aef22d:21). Later light and dark variants fixed seams and the right willow foot (aef22d:43,61–62).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:251–259 describes gestures and T:68–69 gives form direction. The trial sequence is absent.
- **Still valid?** Repeated within engine 5. Masks must still spare pads and neighboring passages; this is an intentional wet edge transition, not a license for whole-canvas blending.

## Composition and looking

### 29. Evaluate the big value relationships before adding more incident

- **Lesson:** Check whether the intended light is supported by distinct dark and middle masses before expanding small details.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; dusk landscapes and low-key still life; whole view, value and squint.
- **Evidence:** A supposed finish revealed sky, mist, water and reflections all around reported values 70–95; the later dark middle restored a reported 15–90 ladder (35c488:42–46,120,125). A full meadow and grove-shadow repaint “holds the picture together” (33a5bf:20). The still-life painter judged the picture stronger after removing the failed cloth instead of repairing it again (ba1453:68–78,113).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: B:35–37 and T:14–16,43–58.
- **Still valid?** Cross-engine process evidence. Interpretation: carry a value intention forward, but do not adopt the journals' rigid “never change a value” rule when observation calls for reconsideration.

### 30. Identify what a visible mark belongs to before repairing it

- **Lesson:** Locate a suspect passage with a grid and compare it with the neighboring forms before deciding it is a defect.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 near-bank bright streak; engine 5 shadow convergence and tiny architectural detail.
- **Evidence:** A streak judged as floating scum proved to be the bank's lit crest once measured against a grid (f99282:72). A street-shadow parallelism initially listed as wrong was later accepted because the other edge belonged to a roofline (49b647:15,31). Those are revised painter judgments, not independent validation of perspective physics.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes for coordinates: T:17–19 and G:636–640. Partly for checking interpretation before repair.
- **Still valid?** The diagnostic process is stable. The specific physical explanation in the shadow journal was not checked beyond that journal.

### 31. Inspect the surface as well as normal color before finishing

- **Lesson:** Compare whole and detail normal-color views with gallery or relief light before deciding whether a surface defect needs repair.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 snow slabs, sun-disc ridges and poplar crackle.
- **Evidence:** A snow rectangle nearly invisible in normal color showed a raised rim in gallery/relief views (d95ea6:50,87). The painter's initial “only under raking light” acceptance was qualified by later gallery visibility (d95ea6:93). Smoke gallery inspection exposed a crusty sun and later moth-eaten poplar texture, prompting tested repairs (r31-smoke:52–58,61–66).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: T:150–153 and G:632–633. B:51–52 explicitly asks for whole and normal detail views but not surface light.
- **Still valid?** Current tools support these views. The journals establish simulator appearances, not how the slab would look under real gallery lighting.

### 32. Decide whether an empty area serves the picture before filling it

- **Lesson:** Judge an empty passage against the picture's intended effect before adding texture or another subject.
- **Kind:** craft.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 solitary oak on snow; engine 2 dark bank and water; small foreground stalk trial.
- **Evidence:** Seven winter grass stalks introduced a near foreground and second subject, so the painter lifted them: “The emptiness of the foreground is a decision” (d95ea6:90). Empty lake and bank passages were accepted after repeated light incidents made sparks or scratches (f99282:53–56,74). This does not make all empty passages successful: the meadow's structure improved with a shadow (33a5bf:20).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes in general: B:37,41–43,51–56 and T:131–134 leave purpose and quiet passages to the painter.
- **Still valid?** A compositional judgment, not an engine restriction.

## Process and sittings

### 33. Match scratch trials to the intended treated underlayer

- **Lesson:** Recreate the passage's underpaint, treatment and drying state on scratch before trusting a material comparison.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 r31 smoke only; dry shadow hatch, gold field hatch, roof/band/ticks, sun impasto and glazed poplar relief.
- **Evidence:** Dry shadow trials rejected pink and salmon accents before choosing warm plum (r31-smoke:12). Dry gold trials compared rag wiping with blending and transferred the partial-lift result (r31-smoke:23–25). Sun and poplar trials reproduced 60-day-old treated surfaces and compared distinct repairs (r31-smoke:53–58,62–66). A scratch-tested skirt still failed when transferred with the wrong aerial piles (r31-smoke:29–30).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: B:39 and G:698–715 explicitly require matching conditions, separate scratch state and deliberate transfer.
- **Still valid?** Strong observed scratch use, but one smoke painting does not establish a general productivity gain. Scratch availability alone does not ensure a faithful transfer.

### 34. Stop a rescue cycle when it keeps restoring the same defect

- **Lesson:** After a repeated failed local repair, reconsider the passage's structure or handling before expanding another version of the same fix.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2–5; sky patches, grove silhouettes, meadow seams, reflections and haze bands.
- **Evidence:** The engine 2 birch painter reported about 150 chunks learning to repaint the composition's fields (35c488:84). Engine 3 boxed lower-half patches led to a full natural-shaped campaign (24be5f:19). Engine 5 flat sidewalk scumbles failed, then a dry whole-field two-pile gradient succeeded (49b647:11,29). Smoke changed from failed glaze/blend to unblended interlocking hatches and resolved the haze step (r31-smoke:67,73).
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes: B:37 explicitly says to reconsider method or composition if a repair recreates the problem.
- **Still valid?** Broad support. Whole-field repaint is one successful escape, not the only acceptable one; thinner local repair also worked (d95ea6:74).

### 35. Finish the background before vulnerable fine incident

- **Lesson:** Complete the background campaign before fine incident, or retain exact protection for every branch, figure and small shape a later pass can reach.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2–5; fine twigs, wood and reflections, figures, pads and shop detail.
- **Evidence:** Background repairs buried the oak's twig net repeatedly before a whole-valley coat finally used all 1,360 recorded polylines as guards (3a958c:6–8,22). Engine 2 meadow work buried trunks and later lost pond, trees and figure again (496bb9:9,21–24). Engine 5 water work buried pads when their masks were not subtracted (12e572:8); later restricted blends spared them (12e572:26).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: T:183–188 covers clipping and persistent masks. B:35–39 requires inspection and trials but does not state a background/detail order.
- **Still valid?** Cross-engine support. “Trees last, once” is a useful sequencing intention, not a ban on later background repair with exact guards.

### 36. Preserve repairable paths and inspect all recorded globals at handoff

- **Lesson:** Keep the paths, widths, piles and protection masks needed for later restatement and check all relevant globals before calling a passage unrecorded.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engine 5 oak; `SEG`, `GROUPS`, `CROWN`; smoke handoffs list masks, piles, shape functions and drying exceptions.
- **Evidence:** By-eye restatement missed a branch by roughly two units and doubled it (d95ea6:35,41). Later inspection found 223 GROUPS entries and 13 CROWN entries with usable paths; one trial landed exactly and enabled a darker armature (d95ea6:59–62,69–70). Smoke handoffs explicitly tracked new globals and the not-yet-dry seam or sun coat (r31-smoke:40,60).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:668–674 explains globals and randomness; T:186–188 says retain later-needed masks. Complete handoff records and auditing more than one path table are absent.
- **Still valid?** Direct engine 5 confirmation. Width multipliers and stopping at width 0.55 belong to that oak restatement, not every line.

### 37. Reopen with a fresh judgment of each unresolved passage

- **Lesson:** Begin a later sitting by checking the whole picture and the recorded unresolved passages, then improve or accept each for a stated visual reason.
- **Kind:** process.
- **Conditions:** Engines 2 and 5; multiple finishes, detail crops and look-only returns.
- **Evidence:** The birch painter reversed two earlier “finished” judgments after a proper detail look (35c488:19,42). Later engine 5 sittings tracked improvements and accepted weaknesses passage by passage (12e572:25–35; 49b647:36–42). The barbershop's final sitting added no paint after checking all open passages (7c4dd1:32–40); smoke's ninth sitting likewise left an already-resolved transition unchanged (r31-smoke:87–94).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: B:51–56 requires final whole/detail inspection and reflection. It does not supply an explicit carryover format for repeated sittings.
- **Still valid?** Supported; neither “never reopen” nor “every sitting must add paint” fits these records.

## Tool confusion

### 38. Check both ends of every band and the dimensions of rectangles

- **Lesson:** Sample a new mask inside and outside its intended extent before painting a consequential pass.
- **Kind:** failure-pattern.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 `smoothstep` half-planes, inverted ramps and rectangles; engine 5 `below` notch construction.
- **Evidence:** Unbounded ramps flooded the birch painting twice; the painter recommended `m:at(x,y)` at five heights (35c488:95–104,129). Passing a second y as `rect` height caused three lake floods (f99282:27,33). A supposed oak notch filled downward instead and had to be lifted (d95ea6:33,38).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:386–401 defines `rect(x,y,w,h)` and above/below; S:144–145 says every mask covers the whole canvas. Explicit point sampling before a broad pass is absent.
- **Still valid?** Definitions remain current. The receipt supports checking bounds, not replacing all rectangles with functions: functions also caused the floods.

### 39. Apply growth before the final object exclusion

- **Lesson:** Recheck object protection after growing or blurring a background mask and apply the final exclusion after any operation that expands it.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 wall mask grown 12 units after object subtraction; pond surround blurred into pond; engine 5 exclusion masks around thin architectural shapes.
- **Evidence:** `(mask - obj):grow(12)` brought the protected bottle and quinces back into the repaint and sliced their edges (ba1453:221). A blurred pond surround reached into the pond and smeared it out (496bb9:22). Engine 5 sky repaint instead grew the protection around thin shapes and removed the visible box (49b647:30,45).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:392–395 supplies the operators and T:183–185 says clip; operator ordering and checking the expanded protection are absent.
- **Still valid?** Geometric interpretation consistent with the current operations. The required guard width depends on the passage; 0.8 or 12 units are case conditions.

### 40. Verify historical option restrictions against the current guide

- **Lesson:** Check the current signature and option types instead of carrying a past engine's API failure forward as a painting rule.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 `work.coverage` functions, ribbon widths and mask operations; engine 5 thinner syntax.
- **Evidence:** `work.coverage` functions repeatedly errored (106bf6:5; f99282:15,63; ba1453:120–122), and one painter spent four failed chunks on ribbon widths (35c488:71). The same journal claimed shape-mask subtraction failed (35c488:69). A later engine 5 repair recorded that `thinner` belongs inside the pile table (d95ea6:80).
- **Already in the prompts?** Partly: G:321 describes work coverage as layers without a function signature, whereas G:362 explicitly permits a function for stipple coverage. G:336–338 and G:665–667 permit functions for options documented that way; G:392 supports mask arithmetic, G:390 shows scalar ribbon width and G:174 shows thinner syntax.
- **Still valid?** The old work-coverage errors are consistent with that distinction in the current guide; current runtime acceptance is not independently verified. G:407 mentions noise passed to coverage without specifying the hand, so it does not conclusively resolve work coverage. Mask arithmetic failure is not a current prohibition. The claimed one-width-per-point ribbon requirement is not universal because G also accepts a scalar width; list-cardinality behavior is not independently checked here.

### 41. Distinguish oil medium from solvent in trial records

- **Lesson:** Record medium and solvent separately and avoid turning an old oil-rich failure into advice about thinner or turps.
- **Kind:** easel.
- **Conditions:** Engine 2 medium 0.3–0.75 films and glaze crust; engine 5 thinner 0.35–0.6 repairs and turps lay-ins.
- **Evidence:** Oil-rich films developed pinholes in the birch account (35c488:110,131); a still-life account called medium above 0.2 “lean and dry-brushing” after a crust failure (ba1453:57–60). Engine 5 thinner tints later succeeded where opaque sky patches failed (d95ea6:74–80). These observations do not isolate the cause of the older pinholes or crust.
- **Already in the prompts?** Yes for distinction: G:97–118 and G:179–208. A documentation inconsistency remains: T:199–200 says oil species and ground absorbency are not drying controls, while G:67–70,111–112 says absorbency quickens setting and oil choice changes drying.
- **Still valid?** Use the current guide's explicit definitions. “Medium makes paint lean” contradicts added-oil medium and is not current advice; old numerical caps and causes remain historical claims. No absorbent-ground finding is inferred from this batch.

## Across the batch: time sinks, changes of approach and limits

- **Background/detail burial was the largest repeated rescue pattern.** The oak journal numbers eleven mistakes and reaches day 719 after repeated sky/fog coats, twig rebuilding and seam repairs (3a958c:6–28). The birch journal reports about 150 chunks of recovery, then floods again and repeats the cycle (35c488:84,95–104,137). The r23 meadow repeatedly loses pond, figures and trees to land repairs and ends with a plan rather than a confirmed final resolution (496bb9:21–24). What helped: completed fields before incident, recorded guards and fewer broad late operations (3a958c:22,27–28). These day counts are simulated painting time, not measured labor.
- **Patch seams and overly pale additions drove another long cycle.** The engine 3 grove reached day 772 with rectangle repairs, light crown rings and repeated opaque meadow patches (24be5f:19–35). The engine 2 still life reports roughly 40 chunks chasing gradients and repeated alternation between veils, gold discs and mottle (ba1453:171–209,226). Whole-field treatment helped the meadow and sky, but sometimes lost small shapes or over-smoothed the intended manner (496bb9:24; ba1453:228). The later sidewalk gradient was a narrower success bounded by real edges (49b647:29).
- **Reported improvement followed a changed method, not merely more passes.** Full-width meadow plus a coherent grove shadow improved the light structure (33a5bf:20). Separate grove masses and a real opening beneath the canopy changed a lump into trees with sky behind them (24be5f:29). Small reflection marks followed by one directional wet smear transformed the far-shore comb (f99282:68–70). Straight unblended pad marks replaced coins (aef22d:29). Recorded-path restatement darkened the oak without doubling its lines (d95ea6:59–62). Thinner repaired the accepted sky scar (d95ea6:74–85). Dry interlocking hatch dissolved the smoke haze seam after glaze/blend failures (r31-smoke:67–76). “Improved” here is the journal's visual judgment.
- **Trials became useful when their comparison matched the passage.** Early on-painting swatches occupied future foreground strips, creating later covering obligations (106bf6:5; b08dc3:1; 3a958c:1,7). A poorly covered value ladder instead measured exposed ground (35c488:106). The smoke scratch trials separately reproduced dry shadow hatch, gold hatch, eave construction, sun relief and glazed poplar texture (r31-smoke:12,23,29,53,62). Scratch did not prevent wrong-pile transfer (r31-smoke:30), so trial records need both material conditions and the actual winning recipe. The journal does not test scratch resets, separate clocks or palette isolation; those are documented capabilities, not extracted outcomes (G:704–715).
- **Tool misreadings are better candidates for a concise clarification than aesthetic prohibitions.** Rectangle height, ramp direction, insufficient mask bounds, growth after subtraction, clipped blend masks containing the wrong neighbors and a held pile mistaken for a laid film all have direct costly examples (f99282:27; 35c488:102–104; ba1453:221; aef22d:18; 7c4dd1:26). The r25 initial four-kind/no-figure plan later gained a moon and crow and rejected grass as a second subject (d95ea6:5,51,67,90). The r31 brief explicitly gives subject and manner to the painter and permits reconsideration, so these additions are not evidence of a current brief-rule violation (B:3–7,41–43).
- **Sittings and handoffs helped distinguish unresolved problems from deliberate choices.** Later journals list affected passages, drying exceptions and live globals, then rejudge them after a fresh look (d95ea6:57–94; r31-smoke:40,60,87–94). Missing paths sometimes existed in another global table, while old categorical “no local patches” rules became stale after a new thinner trial (d95ea6:59–70,74–85). Look-only final sittings were explicit successful outcomes rather than unfinished campaigns (7c4dd1:32–40; aef22d:64–71; 49b647:60–70).

## Ten strongest candidate prompt improvements

These are candidate clarifications or examples, not edits. Several requirements are already present in B:35–39; the improvement would be a concrete diagnostic or reminder at the point of use.

1. **Before a consequential mask pass, sample inside and outside the intended region; a band needs both boundaries and `rect` takes width/height.** Prevents whole-canvas floods rather than prescribing a different shape tool. `35c488:102–104`; `f99282:27,33`; `d95ea6:38`.
2. **Grow or blur first, then apply and verify the final exclusion of foreground shapes.** Makes object protection survive mask expansion. `ba1453:221`; `496bb9:22`; `49b647:30,45`.
3. **Match the trial's substrate, treatment, age and handling, then transfer the winning recipe exactly.** Reinforces the current scratch requirement with the observed wrong-pile transfer and reproduced aging trials. `r31-smoke:29–31,53–58,62–66`; `35c488:106`.
4. **To match an existing passage, recover its recipe and handling from the log before inventing a similar pile.** Repeatedly succeeds where white-rich approximations fail. `r31-smoke:30–31,43–45`; `49b647:52,57`.
5. **After one blend, look again; exclude dry neighbors as well as wet incident.** Makes the strong lifting and transport warning actionable without imposing a universal one-pass cap. `12e572:3,26`; `aef22d:18`; `r31-smoke:3,67–69`.
6. **For a failed cover, distinguish insufficient hiding, unfilled gaps and a thin deposit before merely raising coverage.** Preserves the journal's corrections to its own coverage rule. `35c488:67,80,110`; `49b647:5–6,17`.
7. **Before expanding small detail, resolve the background or keep exact guards and recorded paths for every vulnerable mark.** Reduces repeated twig, figure and pad burial. `3a958c:6–8,22`; `12e572:8,26`; `d95ea6:59–62`.
8. **When repeated repairs restore a seam, change the method: whole fields up to real edges, thinner tints or interlocking dry marks according to the surface.** Replaces “patch again” with supported alternatives while preserving local-repair successes. `49b647:29–30`; `d95ea6:74–85`; `r31-smoke:67,73–76`.
9. **When a passage becomes lozenges, beads or pickets, inspect stroke scale, regularity and direction before changing its color.** Adds a concrete diagnosis to the mark-hierarchy guidance. `f99282:60,68`; `aef22d:28–29`; `r31-smoke:28–31,35`.
10. **At each new sitting, rejudge named unresolved passages from whole and detail views, keep dry-state and path records and permit a look-only finish.** Preserves handoff usefulness without forcing another repair after acceptance. `d95ea6:57–70,82–94`; `7c4dd1:32–40`; `r31-smoke:87–94`.
