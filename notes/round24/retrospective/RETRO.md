What went well:

- **Both records replayed correctly.** Replay matched the live image, saved canvas state and committed record. This establishes reproducibility for these runs, not physical accuracy or artistic quality. [Fable check](evidence.txt#L15), [Opus check](evidence.txt#L66).
- **The painters inspected and revised their work extensively.** Fable made 113 look calls in its first sitting. Opus made 277, then 32, 29 and 24 across its sittings. Opus's return after declaring completion rebuilt the grove as separate tree masses and opened a sky gap under the elm. [Call counts](evidence.txt#L24), [Opus counts](evidence.txt#L75), [second-sitting changes](evidence.txt#L107).
- **Materials sometimes behaved usefully.** Fable reported dry-sky stippling and spirit-rag cleanup working. Opus reported lifting fresh yellow scumbles while preserving the dry layer below, successful dark meadow glazes and removal of a bad fresh glaze. These are painter observations, not controlled measurements. [Fable journal](evidence.txt#L180), [Opus journal](evidence.txt#L205), [later reflection](evidence.txt#L217).
- **Their self-assessments were specific.** Fable named a hard pool edge, straight oak base and malformed monogram. Opus consistently identified heavy crowns, scalloped edges and visible repairs. [Fable reply](evidence.txt#L32), [Opus first reply](evidence.txt#L83), [last reflection](evidence.txt#L219).

The main struggles:

- **A written lesson did not reliably change the next action.** Opus documented rectangular blends mixing contrasting wet paint into gray boxes on day 268, decided to stop rectangular patching on day 379, then recorded doing it again on day 457. Fable independently described rectangular cut edges and wet dark paint dragged into sky. This supports a failure to apply learned constraints during painting, beyond a missing warning in the notes. [Opus journal source lines 17–20](evidence.txt#L201), [Fable journal](evidence.txt#L182).
- **Soft tree edges remained unresolved despite many techniques.** Opus tried sky holes, light scumbles, stippling, wholesale overpainting and wet-in-wet masses. Success in one passage often caused halos, streaks or mismatched repairs elsewhere. Its final satisfaction coexisted with a remaining scalloped crown problem. [Opus chronology, source lines 9–20](evidence.txt#L193), [last reflection](evidence.txt#L219).
- **Low load did not deliver the expected subtle opaque texture.** Opus reported yellow lozenges at load 0.3 and, in the last sitting, another flat opaque olive patch. It then repeated substantial rag cleanup before switching to a transparent glaze. This motivates checking the relationship among load, coverage, lead-white mixtures and the hand presets; it does not by itself prove incorrect deposition. [Day 554](evidence.txt#L205), [day 772](evidence.txt#L219), [actual last-sitting calls](evidence.txt#L149).
- **The API added avoidable interruptions.** Opus's first sitting had seven rejected oversized crops, seven Lua/argument paint failures and one unanswered easel request. Its last sitting had another rejected crop. Fable had one rejected crop and one paint conversion failure. The unanswered request was a 16-day simulated wait; Opus queried drying afterward rather than repeating the wait. [Errors](evidence.txt#L84), [last crop](evidence.txt#L148), [Fable errors](evidence.txt#L35), [recovery calls](evidence.txt#L279).

On giving up early: **Fable has the clearer evidence of a shallow return visit.** It knew of specific weaknesses, but its return contained only one `look({})`, no crop, no journal read and no paint. Eight seconds is the measured whole visit, not a judgment about how much art should take. The original runner accepted that as completion. [Known weaknesses](evidence.txt#L32), [return](evidence.txt#L38), [exact look](evidence.txt#L281).

**Opus's individual sittings closed with unresolved issues, but the full run shows substantial persistence.** The first sitting lasted 164 minutes. Three returns added 16, five and six painting chunks. The artist said it was content each time, then improved the grove, edges and meadow on another invitation. Final closure was the runner's cap, not a no-work review, crash or usage-limit interruption. The newer runner requires a completed no-paint sitting with whole and detail review; that never happened. The four-sitting cap therefore prevents deciding whether a fifth sitting would have produced a legitimate finish. [Chunk progression](evidence.txt#L50), [ordinary replies](evidence.txt#L107), [runner completion rule](evidence.txt#L287).

On the rag: **failure to remove tacky or dry paint is expected in the exported guide.** It explicitly limits lifting to open paint and says paint past gel does not come off. Opus's tacky scars, setting-paint squares and dry streaks are therefore not sufficient evidence of a bug. The painter also recorded successful cleanup while fresh. [Exported guide](evidence.txt#L273), [journal](evidence.txt#L195), [fresh cleanup](evidence.txt#L217).

**The strongest concrete rag concern is automatic refolding during a dipped wipe.** In the last sitting Opus repeatedly used `refold=0.12` to `0.2`, high pressure and multiple passes. In the exported `aeaf582` source, mask wiping calls `rag.refold()` when its load threshold is crossed; that operation sets dampness to zero. A dip before the mask operation does not wet each subsequently exposed face. The guide describes these behaviors separately, but the painter's loops repeatedly dipped before entire multiwipe operations. This can make part of an intended spirits cleanup act as a dry wipe. Its contribution to this painting remains an inference: no intermediate rag dampness trace was recorded. [Actual calls](evidence.txt#L149), [refold reset](evidence.txt#L223), [automatic refold](evidence.txt#L245), [guide](evidence.txt#L258).

The highest-value follow-up work, in priority order:

1. **Keep export status separate from painter completion.** High confidence, high diagnostic impact: preserve `NOT FINISHED / cap reached` in watcher and gallery metadata, since Opus was exported and preserved as finished despite the runner's explicit result. Any cap change should retain a safety limit; more sittings alone do not resolve the observed repeated mistakes. [Cap and export](evidence.txt#L60), [watcher](../night-watch/watch.mjs#L89).
2. **Make learned painting constraints survive the next action.** High confidence, high quality impact: put the current journal's concrete lessons into the return context and require the artist to examine its acknowledged weak passage before closing. The existing Opus prompt already invites long work, so adding more encouragement alone addresses less than the demonstrated repeat errors. [Repeated rectangle mistake](evidence.txt#L201), [return prompt](evidence.txt#L315), [Fable single look](evidence.txt#L281).
3. **Make damp mask-wipe behavior legible and reproduce it before changing physics.** High confidence in mechanism, medium confidence in visual impact: expose or report automatic refold/dampness transitions, document a working small-wipe/refold/redip recipe and compare it against the actual failing meadow cleanup. Keeping new faces wet automatically would change behavior and requires an explicit design decision. [Calls](evidence.txt#L149), [exported source](evidence.txt#L223).
4. **Investigate the tree-edge and opaque-texture workflow using these actual recipes.** High confidence in friction, unproven engine defect: compare narrow contour blends against rectangular repair masks and low-load opaque scumbles against transparent glazes at the run's canvas scale. Judge deposition and mixing separately from whether the picture is attractive. [Both painters' lessons](evidence.txt#L180), [Opus repeat failures](evidence.txt#L193).
5. **Reduce repeated crop and Lua friction.** High confidence, smaller impact: make crop limits and outline-to-mask conversion obvious at the failed call, and identify missing global names in errors where feasible. Preserve the transactional guarantee that failed chunks change nothing. [Actual failures](evidence.txt#L84).

No fix or new painting run was performed. Findings about rag behavior use the exported Opus commit `aeaf582`, not later main-branch changes. The two runs used different versions, prompts and canvas sizes, so this is not a controlled comparison of the models.
