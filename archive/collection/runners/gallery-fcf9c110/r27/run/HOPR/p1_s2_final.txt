**Barber Shop, Late Afternoon**

This is a two-storey brick corner building in late-afternoon sun. The front is lit warm, the side wall has fallen into deep maroon shade, and a long blue-violet wedge of shadow runs across the street. A man in a white shirt and straw hat sits tilted back on a kitchen chair by the barber pole, with his shadow on the wall behind him.

In this sitting (day 163 to day 215) I went back to the passages I'd left unresolved:
- **Shop window:** it now shows pale mirrors, a counter line and the dark shapes of two barber chairs, so the shop has a life inside it. This was a retry of the reflection that failed before. It worked this time because I kept the value within one step of the glass and used plain rectangles instead of a diagonal streak.
- **Upper windows:** two of them now have a curtain drawn to one side.
- **Door:** I darkened the shade in the door glass to suit a door in shadow.
- **Distant red building:** it now has stone sills. My first try at window shades back there came out as pale holes, so I wiped them off with a spirits-dipped rag and repainted over the scars once they were dry.
- **Side-wall shop window:** I tried a lighter glow inside it. It went on far too light, so I wiped it off, and only a faint trace is left.

I'm leaving some things as they are on purpose:
- The brick has no coursing. Flat, warm colour suits the light better.
- The cream cornice on the shadow side stays light. It's plausible as a top surface catching the sky.
- The background buildings stay simple so they sit back.
- The barber chairs look a little lumpy up close, and the wiped glow left a faint mark.

My heart is happy with it. The painting is signed and finished.

**Handoff lessons**
- **Drying:** let each coat dry 10–17 days, and check several spots before the next campaign. Small details were dry in 12–14 days.
- **Rag eraser:** a rag dipped 0.9 or more in spirits, pressed hard (about 0.9), 8–10 units wide, used over 3–4 rounds with refolds, lifts fresh paint almost completely.
- **Accents over dark glass:** checking the mix against the canvas first is still useful, but it doesn't show how light paint will go on over dark glass. Mix those accents darker than they look on the knife.
- **Scumbles, glazes and reflections:** keep them within one value step of what's underneath, and give them plain architectural shapes rather than streaks.
