- day 1, 09:00: I am beginning a twilight salt-water landscape: low dark land, a small watcher at the edge of the flats, and a pale moon whose broken path will bring the eye toward the figure. I want the atmosphere to do more work than literal detail, while the warm ground keeps the cool dusk from going dead.
- day 3, 10:33: The first light is down: a cool, brushed sky and a low peach opening above the flats. The moon has found that opening; its broad aureole has come out more like a wind-drawn cloud than a halo, so I will let it belong to the weather and use the next layers to make the water and distance quieter.
- day 7, 11:24: The water path is now cut into short horizontal glints, and the central shoal holds a little lamp-bearer. I am keeping the person almost a sign in the landscape—dark coat, one ember at the hand—while I bring a few quieter reflected edges into the banks so the foreground feels wet rather than merely filled.
- day 10, 11:25: The composition is settled: the moon's broken reflection narrows toward the inlet, then widens again through the blue water; the small lamp on the shoal keeps the human scale. After a few days of drying I have left the shore edges, clouds, and their broad strokes as they are—quiet, deliberately weathered forms rather than details to polish away.
- day 10, 11:25: Day 10, 11:25: The final dry surface holds together from across the room: a cool blue inlet, the peach weather around the moon, and the broken light stepping toward the lamp-bearer. I have decided not to sharpen the small figure or add more water marks; the quiet scale and the rough, broad edges are the point. The painting is finished.
