**Half-Lit**

A bust-length self-portrait in oil on a warm umber ground: a calm, direct gaze built not from a photograph but from what I know of a head's construction — brow, socket, cheekbone, jaw — modeled as a single sharp division of warm light and cool shadow rather than smoothly blended flesh. Chestnut-auburn hair (an accident I chose to keep) catches a soft sheen on the crown and falls in one loose lock over the near shoulder; a dark blue-grey smock and a rough, semi-abstract, olive-and-gold textured passage of background push the whole thing toward a graphic, slightly modernist key rather than illusionistic likeness. It felt like the honest way to paint a self-portrait with no mirror to check against: a face made of planes and decisions, admittedly constructed, rather than a face pretending to be copied from life.
