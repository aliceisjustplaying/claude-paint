- day 1, 09:00: Subject: an estuary at low tide just after sunset. Low horizon at y~400. Sky warm glow low-left-of-centre (x~380), cooling to blue-grey above; a long band of dark stratus crossing it. Right: a spit of land with a clump of trees, dark against the glow. Foreground: wet mud banks (warm umber) with channels of bright water reflecting the glow; a small boat lying over on the mud at left; a line of stakes receding at right. Ground: lead white knifed, then a thin warm ochre/umber imprimatura. Plan: pencil drawing, thin lay-in of sky and water, dark land and mud, rest, then second layer for clouds, glow, reflections; details last; varnish.
- day 1, 15:50: Day 1 afternoon. Sky laid in four bands (cool slate top, violet-grey, warm pale, yellow glow at horizon) and blended twice across; reads as a soft gradient. Water laid as the sky inverted, blended horizontally. Mud banks laid in with a warm umber — too flat and too domed: they read as hills, not flat banks in perspective. Will reshape their tops later by pulling water over them and add sheen. Next: far shore and the spit with its tree clump, dark against the glow.
- day 3, 17:41: Day 3. Trees repainted as rounded crowns (roughened union of ellipses): reads as foliage now, though some grey lifted patches remain in the lower mass. Mistake: laid a vertical-stroke reflection under the spit and blended it with a rect mask; the badger smeared it into a hard-edged grey rectangle over the dry water. Will let it dry and repaint the glow water over it, then do the reflection with short horizontal strokes and no big blend. Meanwhile clouds on the dry sky.
- day 6, 20:39: Day 6 evening. Repainted the whole sky over the dry lay-in with a stronger gradient, set the clouds wet-in-wet from stretched-noise masks, and blended the whole field twice at crossing angles: the sausage clouds dissolved into soft streaks, much more like air. Discovered the tree mask had a gap between crowns and the base strip; the sky paint now shows under the canopy as an arch of light. Will keep the idea (light between trunks) but break it with trunks and low foliage. Repainted the glow water across, covering the grey block, and laid the tree reflection wet-in-wet with horizontal strokes then one horizontal blend: soft, hazy, acceptable.
- day 7, 00:27: Day 7, night. Reworked the left mud bank with a near-dark / middle / pale-rim gradient and blended: it now reads as a smooth rounded boulder. The dome outline plus smooth modelling is the problem. Flat mud in perspective wants horizontal shelves, ragged near-horizontal edges, pools of sky in it. Plan once dry: paint water over the dome top to cut the edge into shelves, add sheen pools and ripple ridges; same for the right bank. Also a second pass over the trees for variety and to cover streaks.
- day 9, 01:53: Day 9. Cut the mud banks down to near-horizontal shelves by painting water over their domed tops; added sky pools and a textured pass (flat brush dabs of dark, green and pale mud) that looked like cobbles until one horizontal blend fused it into streaky mud. Pools still look pasted on. Whole view: sky good, but the lower half is too pale and too even in value; the channel margins have pale strips that make the banks look like cliffs. Plan: darken the foreground water toward the slate of the upper sky it reflects, deepen the near mud with a glaze, ripple lines on the middle water, restore the far shore line, then boat, stakes, second pass on the trees.
- day 11, 03:23: Day 11. Realised why the banks read as cliffs/mesas: the channel between them WIDENS toward the horizon, so its edges diverge instead of converging in perspective. Fix: extend both banks so the channel narrows into the distance (edges converging), keep their far edges horizontal. Also the smooth blended gradient on the mud makes rounded forms; horizontal sheen streaks help flatness but my uniform ones look like stone slabs.
- day 17, 16:18: Day 17. Channel repainted in one wet layer, narrowing into the distance; mud given a cool dark glaze toward the foreground. Banks read flatter but the channel edges are still clean ruled lines and the far mud is one slab. Now: thin bright horizontal wet-sheen lines across the far mud (alternating mud and shine, closer together with distance), a wet lip along the channel edges, and dark bank reflections in the channel.
- day 17, 17:23: Day 17 evening. A scumble over the far mud went on as an opaque scalloped block; three horizontal blends of the whole bank fused it into a hazy recession, light far, dark near, with the fine crackle texture of the glaze still in the foreground. Far shore line restored along the horizon. Thin ripple strokes on the middle water (warm glints under the glow, violet elsewhere) make it read as water. Next: boat on the left bank, stakes along the channel, bank reflections.
- day 18, 23:53: Day 19. Boat painted on the left bank (dark slate hull, warm interior, mast and stay, contact shadow): reads as a boat, a little toy-like. Line of six stakes along the right channel edge with wavering reflections. Edge treatment along the channel (dark reflection band + pale lip) looked piped; a narrow badger blend softened it into a wet margin but also smeared two stakes and left a lattice patch near 720-840,575. Repair after drying.
- day 21, 12:54: Day 21. Second pass on the trees: filled most of the light arch under the canopy leaving four small openings with trunks, stippled a warmer olive along the upper-left crown edges where the glow would catch, and integrated the odd oval crown with touches round its rim. Restated the six stakes with flat lit tops and wavering reflections. Tried lit undersides on the mid cloud: thin warm dashes looked like morse; a soft badger took most of them away again - the sky is better quiet.
- day 23, 13:25: Day 23. Darkened the boat hull and interior with glazes, gunwale highlight, three distant birds. Closed most openings under the trees (they had become a row of arches, like a viaduct). Soft glow core in the sky above the horizon at left-centre. Weed clumps and stones on the mud in perspective (bigger nearer); they read as olive-brown wrack. Value check (squint): the range is too narrow, mud barely darker than water. Next: a stronger dark glaze over the near mud so the pale channel leads in and the foreground frames the light.
- day 27, 13:52: Day 27. Horizontal ripples in the channel (softened with a narrow badger so the glints sit in the water rather than on it). Strengthened the trees reflection with broken dark horizontal strokes right under the waterline, then blended within a mask shaped like the reflection itself (not a rectangle, the mistake of day 3): it now hangs from the spit and fades down, mottled like slightly moving water. Squinting, the picture holds: dark tree mass and dark near mud frame the pale glow and the channel that leads into it. I judge it complete. Letting it dry fully, then a light varnish.
- day 147, 13:54: Day 147. All touch-dry after four months. A light mastic varnish (0.3 coats): warms the slate of the upper sky toward grey, pulls the glow and the mud together. Finished. Title: "Low Water, After Sunset".
