I've finished **Evening Gold, Willow Pool**, and my heart is happy with it.

I looked at it fresh, at full size, blurred and in close crops. The picture still holds together. Dark violet-blue willow reflections hang on either side of a gold sky reflection. Lilac water runs down into a deep blue foreground. Seven lily-pad islands float on it, with pink and white flowers.

**What I did this sitting:** under the far island I found two small dark-blue marks that looked like stray "legs" hanging off it. With the paint dry, I covered them with a few short horizontal touches of a pale lilac mixed from colours I already had. Seen whole, the spot is gone. Up close a faint trace is left, which reads as a slight reflection under the island, so I'm keeping it.

**Earlier unresolved points, judged again:**
- **Far islands slightly crisp:** I'm leaving them. They still recede when the picture is blurred.
- **Wipe ghosts:** I'm leaving them. They've blended into the water.
- **Pads painted fairly evenly:** I'm leaving them. Last sitting's warm touches already vary them enough.
- **Left-middle island stays cooler:** this is on purpose, because it sits in the willows' shadow.

**Lessons I've carried into the journal:**
- A rag soaked in spirits lifts fresh paint cleanly off a dry layer, so it works as an undo.
- Opaque, very white dabs that are then blended look pasted on.
- Thin veils blend in well but change little.
- Vertical strokes blended straight away in the same direction read as reflections.
- Accents on the pads should be straight, unblended, close in value, and ochre or olive rather than pink.
- A small stray mark can be covered with short touches of a colour mixed to match its surroundings, kept off the neighbouring shapes.

I'm stopping here because more touches would start to fuss a picture whose strength is its softness.
