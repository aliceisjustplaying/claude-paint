## 2. The decisions

**Relief: 0.06 with the sheet's 0.006 gloss.** In `notes/round6/relief/green_sky_sheet.jpg`, 0.12 still emphasizes diagonal ridges while 0.06 quiets them without entirely flattening the surface. It cannot repair the rock rim or punched tree holes.

**Oak: merge.** `r6-oak:notes/oak/p2_2_oak_1000_before_after.jpg` replaces looping ropes with branching elbows; `p2_3_crown_edge_3200_before_after.jpg` shows the cost: thorny, sometimes excessively angular tips. The connectivity repair is concrete: local-width versus base-width selection omitted 897 units of wood (`r6-oak:notes/oak.md`). However, “changes the study” understates scope: oak/lime geometry changes in every season, including the leafy crown in `p2_5_oak_in_leaf_before_after.jpg`. Unchanged benchmarks merely don't exercise that API.

**Wet: keep developing it, but don't merge as the default yet.** The branch's discrete marks are a valuable capability and a real regression in several current pictures. Both can be true. `notes/round6/panels/judge3/PANEL.md` records foliage and near winning 4–0, rock losing 4–0 and sky, water and green losing 3–1. The images support the central tradeoff: clearer spruces versus reflection teeth, scalloped rock planes and hedge dots (`r6-wet:notes/wet/pass3_*.jpg`).

The two-layer direction is credible; “the physics is right” is not established. `r6-wet:notes/wet.md` explicitly calls its stirring constants estimates. Its final pass has river ground exposure at 35%, not the first pass's 18%; rock/snow recovery falls to 0.69/0.74 from pass 2's 0.80/0.78. Those are tradeoffs, not a completed fix. Stroke endings are a plausible missing piece, not the only demonstrated cause. Require controllable softening without sacrificing loaded lights before promotion.

**Lab: water B, sky B, rock B, bare tree C, foliage A.** Those are current-image choices, not universal recipes. Foliage C is the next working candidate, but its regularly lit domes look more like broccoli than A's leaf clusters (`notes/lab/foliage_A.jpg`, `foliage_C.jpg`).

## 3. What the lab taught

“Selection beats tone” is justified for this foreground oak implementation, not all fine structure. C changed selection, taper, depth color, connectivity and ground handling together; its roughly 1,540 wood strokes versus A's 5,500 do not isolate selection (`notes/lab/baretree.md`). Test a distant crown before banning tonal twigs.

My earlier simplification advice was too easy to read as “less detail always wins.” Foliage A retains useful intermediate-scale irregularity that B's blobs and C's repeated domes lose. Wet handling also handicapped the simplified recipes: `foliage.md` documents swallowed lights and abandoned shade veils. The lesson is hierarchical detail, not maximal subtraction.

## 4. Panel discipline

Blind labels, whole views and matched crops are useful. But two runs per model are not four independent human eyes. The briefs prime named defects; painters' self-verdicts are hypotheses, especially bare-tree B's claimed victory before its 0–4 rejection.

Correct “three identified the engines”: judge3's `answer_g2.md` reverses sky relative to `key.txt`. Identical-log replay fairly measures compatibility, not each engine's attainable quality. Add equal-budget repainting on each engine and one unseen composition. Freeze exact revisions: the panel judged pass 2, not pass 3.
