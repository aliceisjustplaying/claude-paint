# Impressionist materials (research for the giverny and impressionist boxes)

Gathered by a research pass over technical and conservation sources, reported
as materials only (what pigments, grounds and media analyses found), so that a
painter working from it learns nothing of what the paintings look like.

Sources: National Gallery, *Art in the Making: Impressionism* (1990) and
*Degas* (2004), via ColourLex; NG Technical Bulletin 24 (Kirby et al. 2003,
Seurat) and 28 (Roy 2007, Monet's late palette); Butler 1984 (AIC preprints,
ten Cézannes 1877–1906); Otero et al. 2017, *Heritage Science* 5:46 (barium,
zinc and strontium yellows); Newfields' Pissarro catalogue; Keegan 2015 (AIC,
Renoir); Pozzi et al. 2014, *J. Raman Spectroscopy* (red lakes); a 2022
*Microscopy and Microanalysis* paper on an 1887–89 Monet. The AIC catalogue
pages were not reachable; their findings come from secondary summaries.
Beware: French *vert émeraude* is viridian, *vert Véronèse* emerald green.

## Pigments found

- **Monet, 1869–77**: lead white, cobalt blue, Prussian blue, artificial
  ultramarine, cerulean, emerald green, viridian, chrome yellow, barium
  (lemon) yellow, vermilion, red lake, cobalt violet, red and yellow ochre,
  ivory black, a manufacturer's chrome green. **c.1880**: no black, no earths.
  **1887–89**: zinc white mixed into the colors, cobalt blue and violet,
  cadmium yellow, vermilion, on a lead white ground. **1899–1926** (Roy
  2007): lead white, cobalt blue, French ultramarine, cobalt violet,
  viridian, cadmium yellows and orange, zinc and barium yellow together,
  vermilion, a madder lake; no chrome yellow, no emerald green (it reacts
  with the cadmiums), no earths (yellow ochre still documented). Cochineal
  as well as madder lakes in AIC samples. Mostly poppy oil, white in linseed.
- **Renoir**: lead white; cobalt blue, then French ultramarine by about
  1885; chrome, strontium, barium and zinc yellows, Naples yellow after
  about 1885; vermilion, madder and cochineal lakes (the carmine has faded);
  emerald green, viridian; ivory and bone black; iron oxides.
- **Pissarro**: ultramarine, cobalt blue, vermilion, red lake, viridian,
  emerald green, chrome and cadmium yellow; ochres and a little black in
  the 1870s, earths largely given up later; zinc white with lead white.
- **Sisley**: cobalt blue, red lake, viridian, an ochre, chrome yellow, lead
  white, traces of black.
- **Morisot**: cerulean, cobalt, ultramarine, emerald green, viridian,
  cadmium and chrome yellow, ochres, vermilion, a brownish lake, ivory black,
  lead white.
- **Degas (oils)**: vermilion, red lead, red and yellow ochre, viridian,
  emerald green, ultramarine, madder lake, black, lead and zinc white.
- **Cézanne**: above all lead white, vermilion, emerald green, ultramarine;
  red lake, iron oxide yellow, viridian, charcoal and bone black; chrome
  yellow until his last decade; cobalt blue from about 1898; Indian yellow;
  a yellow lake; chalk as 20–47% of some paints.
- **Seurat**: vermilion, red lake, burnt sienna, iron oxide yellow, chrome
  and cadmium yellow, viridian, emerald green, ultramarine, cobalt blue,
  lead white, black (1884); from 1885 no earths or black, zinc yellow added;
  strontium yellow, madder and cochineal lakes, an organic yellow.

**What has changed since**: zinc yellow browns or greens; carmine and madder
fade; barium yellow greens; chrome yellow darkens; emerald green blackens with
cadmium sulfide; zinc white forms brittle soaps. (Not modeled.)

## The tubes added

| tube | numbers from |
|---|---|
| strontium yellow, barium yellow, zinc yellow | measured dry-powder L\*a\*b\* and tinting strength relative to lead chromate (Otero 2017), darkened for oil; hiding from refractive index; drying average (no lead) |
| carmine lake | estimates; a transparent cool red like rose madder |
| yellow lake | estimates; transparent, weak |
| vine black | estimates; bluish, weaker than bone black |

Ivory black is bone black (the two can't be told apart by analysis). Chrome
green and cadmium green were manufacturers' mixtures: mix them. Pigments that
existed but were not found in these painters' works (synthetic alizarin,
geranium lake, aureolin, chromium oxide green, manganese violet) are not added.

## Not modeled yet (from the same research)

- **Oils**: lead white usually in poppy oil, colors in linseed; drying and
  yellowing run linseed > walnut > poppy (rough rates 1 / 0.8 / 0.6).
- **Driers**: litharge-cooked oil, siccatif de Courtrai or de Harlem: much
  faster drying, darker and more brittle with too much.
- **Turpentine**: lean, fast, matte films; Degas's *peinture à l'essence*
  (oil blotted out, then thinned with turpentine).
- **Absorbent chalk-glue grounds**: they draw the oil out: matte, faster
  setting, risk of sinking. Oil grounds were white, off-white, cream, pale
  grey (white with black) or beige.
- **Varnish**: the Impressionists preferred their pictures unvarnished:
  matte, reading lighter than varnished.
