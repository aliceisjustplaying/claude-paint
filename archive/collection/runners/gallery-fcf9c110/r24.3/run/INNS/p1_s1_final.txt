**Evening, the River Meadow**

This is a late-October dusk on a 3:2 canvas, about twenty minutes after sunset. The sky runs from a smoky violet-gray overhead down to a pale lemon glow over a hazy far tree line. In that tree line a small farmhouse shows a lit window and a thin trail of smoke. Below it a still pond catches the last light. On the right a big dark grove of elms runs down into mist at the water's edge, and its reflection bleeds into the pond. In front is a dark meadow glazed with umber, black and a little Antwerp blue, with a worn track crossing it. A small woman in a red shawl stands where the track meets the lighter ground by the water. She is the one warm note in the picture and gives it its scale.

I worked the way the notes say Inness did. I stained the white-lead ground with raw umber and rubbed it back with a rag, drew in chalk, and laid in thin, turpentine-thinned paint, wiping the lights of the pond and the path out with a rag. After that came many campaigns over about 600 simulated days. Opaque sky and meadow layers went on, then dark glazes left to dry between sessions. Twice I covered the trees completely with sky paint and started them again ("plastering the wall").

The sky, the far distance, the pond and the figure work best. The grove is the weak part. It's still too heavy and too sharp-edged against the sky, Inness would have let more air into it, and you can see the layers of my repairs on it. I've signed it lower right.
