**The Oak in Snow at Evening** is finished, and the picture is the same as at the end of the last sitting.

An old oak stands alone on the central axis, on a shallow snow-covered rise just after sunset. The sky runs from cold smalt-cobalt overhead down to a warm Naples-yellow glow at the horizon. Beyond the snow there's only a forest band lying in valley mist and a faint ridge. A thin crescent moon on the right and one far-off crow on the left keep each other company across the sky.

**This sitting (day 341)**

I looked at the whole canvas and at close crops of each passage I'd left unresolved.
- **The old pale patch in the upper-left sky:** resolved. Close up it's only a soft cloudlet.
- **The pale specks in the fork:** kept. At full view they read as sky through the fork.
- **The raised slab of paint in the snow:** kept. It shows as a rectangle under raking and gallery light, but in normal colour it's invisible apart from two tiny flecks. Every patch I've laid on this snow has made it worse.
- **The violet fleck in the corner:** kept. A frame would cover it.
- **The pale fine twigs:** kept. Over the dark main branches they read as twigs dissolving into the dusk.

**One trial, wiped off**

I tried a small tuft of dry grass stalks in the lower-left snow. They dried a bright ochre, far more saturated than the paint looked on the palette, and they brought in a near foreground that the picture doesn't want. I lifted them at once with fresh spirit rags, and the snow there is clean again. The trial made it clear that the empty foreground is a choice, not a gap.

**Handoff lessons (all earlier ones still apply)**
- Opaque patches on the dry, blended sky or snow dry paler than the paint around them and show. Thinned paint (thinner 0.35–0.6), built up in several light passes, can tint a pale patch in the sky instead.
- Only restate a line where its path is recorded (in GROUPS or CROWN), using pressure_for().
- Wet paint over a dry film always comes off with a fresh spirit rag on each pass.
- **New:** thin earth-colour lines over pale snow dry as bright ochre. Mix them grayer and cooler than seems right, or first ask whether the picture wants anything there at all.

**Still uncertain:** whether the raised slab in the snow would show under real gallery lighting. I accept that it might.

My heart is happy and at rest. The one thing I added today is gone again, and the picture is complete: snow, oak, far land and sky, with the moon and the crow.
