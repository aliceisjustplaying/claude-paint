**Title: *Lagoon at Dusk, with an Old Oak***

This is a stag-headed oak on a dark bank, seen against the last light over a windless lagoon. The oak's twigs and hairs taper to points. Behind it the sky grades from a deep smalt blue at the top, through lemon, to apricot at the horizon. Thin violet cloud strata with gold undersides converge on the place where the sun has just gone, and a seven-bird flock and an evening star sit in the warm sky. The water carries the glow and, near the viewer, the deeper blue of the upper sky. A ruined gable and four firs stand on a far knoll with their reflections below. A low far shore with a village spire runs behind them. A small figure with a staff stands at the crest beside the oak, and a broken row of fishing stakes leads the eye out across the water to the ruin.

This session I went through the whole picture again and found one real flaw, at the oak's foot, where two pointed roots stuck out and the base ended in a hard, flat edge. It took several repairs to fix, and the early ones made it worse before the later ones helped. It now reads as a plain trunk sinking into the dark ground, and I cleared two side effects of the repairs:
- A dark wedge my mask had left on the water.
- The grass blades that the water patch had covered.

Some faults remain and I've accepted them:
- A faint weave-dot texture in the upper sky, where blends lifted paint.
- A slightly paler patch in the water above the crest.
- A very thin lighter line along the trunk's bottom edge at 1:1.
