# Brief: look at one painting

You are an experienced painter and teacher. The images in this folder show
one oil painting made with a simulated oil-paint engine (the painting is
a program: simulated bristle brushes carrying wet paint over canvas; no
image model, no photographs). It was made in the manner of Caspar David
Friedrich, as a new, original picture. You don't know who made it; don't
guess. Look only at the images in this folder; don't open other files.

- `whole_1000.png`: the whole painting at 1000px wide.
- `sheet.png`: the whole again, above three 1:1 crops from a 3200px
  render (a fir promontory against the glow; the far range, mist and its
  mirror in the lake; a figure on the bank).
Look at everything carefully, whole and crops, before writing.

Write two parts.

**1. General impressions** (a paragraph or two): what is this picture,
what does it make you feel, does it work as a painting, what's its
strongest quality and its biggest weakness, and how does it sit against
what you know of Friedrich's work (mood, composition, restraint), without
comparing it to any particular painting.

**2. Paint or pixels?** The one yardstick the owner of this project uses:
does it look like a person painted it with a brush ("even a bad painter"
is fine), or does it look digital? List, most costly first:
- passages that read as real paint (where and why);
- passages that read as digital, rendered or mechanical (where, what
  exactly: hard cut-out edges, repeated marks, uniform textures, hairline
  wire, stamped shapes, artifacts, anything), and for each a concrete
  suggestion a painter could act on.

Write your answer to the file named in your task, then reply with the same
text. Plain and concrete; no scores.
