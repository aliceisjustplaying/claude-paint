Across all ten studies, three defects recur consistently. Most costly is the reliance on procedural micro-marks in place of massed form, seen in the hairline grass strokes of 2X and 5X, the noodle-like squiggles of 2X, the uniform leaf dabs of 1X and the steel-wool branch fuzz of 5Y; drawing thousands of microscopic individual hairs or dabs destroys painterly economy and betrays digital generation immediately. Second is the appearance of razor-sharp, unintegrated edges, exemplified by the vector horizon of 3Y, the knife-edge reflection of 4X, the hard polygon cast shadow of 2X and the cookie-cutter sky holes of 1Y, all of which ignore wet-in-wet blending, lost edges and atmospheric perspective. Third is a disconnected, uniform impasto or embossing texture applied mechanically across shapes without following planes or light, most damaging in the flat relief silhouette of 1Y, the synthetic sky weave of 3Y, the dimpled bump mapping of 4X and the haloed branch contours of 5Y.
