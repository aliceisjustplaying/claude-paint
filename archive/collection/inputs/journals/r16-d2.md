- day 1, 09:00: Setting up the easel. Beginning a still life in oil on linen: 'Quince, Stoneware Pitcher, and Bay Leaves on a Wooden Ledge'. Planning a warm, luminous chiaroscuro with raking light from the upper-left, modeling the earthen volumes and ripe textures with traditional earth pigments, lead white, and glazes.
- day 1, 09:00: Prepared linen canvas 400mm (aspect 1.25) with a warm double ground: knifed lead white/ochre/umber base with a thin brushed warm ochre imprimatura. Good tooth and subtle grain.
- day 1, 09:02: Laid in the composition with black chalk: jug on the left, tilted ripe quince on the right, wooden shelf plane, and a bay sprig crossing the front ledge. Proportions and placement feel balanced and grounded.
- day 1, 10:18: Background lay-in established the chiaroscuro division: olive-umber on the left, deep bone black/umber on the right. The transition at mid-canvas needs unification while both passages remain open.
- day 1, 10:37: Glazed and blended the background wall horizontally with a rich umber-ochre veil. The field now transitions smoothly from muted olive-umber on the left to deep, luminous chocolate dark on the right.
- day 1, 11:30: Blocked in the primary masses: earthenware pitcher with terracotta half-tones and deep umber shadow, luminous golden quince, and cast shadows across the wooden ledge. The lay-in establishes the volume and weight; next step is carving the contours, refining the edges against the background, and modeling the spherical and cylindrical curvatures.
- day 1, 12:05: Evaluating the ébauche: the underpainted masses give solid weight, but the unclipped brushwork left ragged boundaries around the jug handle and fruit crest. Unifying the background field around the silhouettes, then allowing the underpainting to set before building up opaque body coats, glaze transitions, and brushwork.
- day 1, 15:25: Inspected close-up crops of pitcher and quince. Quince shows rich chromatic depth—warm chrome yellow, olive terminator, and delicate stem. Pitcher shoulder needs glazing to fuse the striped highlights into a unified curved ceramic sheen; handle needs strap thickness; contours and cast shadows need edge integration.
- day 1, 15:46: Pitcher volume and handle loop now read clearly in space. Softening the right contour of the quince into the deep background shadow, then laying down the foreground sprig of bay leaves with curled tips crossing the wooden bevel, fine leaf veins, and finishing specular accents.
- day 1, 15:50: Completed work on 'Quince, Stoneware Pitcher, and Bay Leaves on a Wooden Ledge'. Evaluated in full view, close-up crops, value mode, and squint mode. The tonal hierarchy, textures, and color relationships are resolved. Saving the painting and closing the easel session.
