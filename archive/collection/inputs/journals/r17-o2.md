- day 1, 11:28: Subject: an estuary at low tide, evening. Low horizon at y=415, big soft sky, dark mudflats, a channel of water carrying the sky's light up to the horizon, a small beached boat at left, a few posts. Warm pinkish-ochre imprimatura. Sky laid in three bands (blue-grey top, lilac middle, pale gold low) and blended; upper cloud bank in violet-grey worked wet and blended; thin stratus bars blended into the mid sky. The sky reads soft and atmospheric but pale - the land has to carry the darks.
- day 1, 13:19: Land lay-in went wrong-ish: the near mud's top edge bulged up into a dune, the channel reads like a road, and the far-water strip laid wet over the right bank came up dirty and streaked. Decided to treat all this as an underpainting: let it dry, then redraw the land flatter and more horizontal over it (thin water strip at horizon, bank tops flatter, channel narrowing to a sliver).
- day 4, 08:58: Second campaign over the dry lay-in. Redrew the land: big sheet of water on the right carrying the sky (gold near the horizon, lilac-grey in front), the left land as a wet sand flat with blurred water lenses (the lenses first came out like crosswalk stripes; a horizontal blend fused them into soft reflections, which works), and the near mud. A vertical blend on the mud combed it into a cliff-face pattern; a horizontal blend over it saved it, but the mud is too light. Values are bunched pale overall. Plan: let it set, then darken the near mud, restate the tree line and a church tower, put the boat on the mud, posts in the water.
- day 6, 13:38: Day 6: darkened the near mud (umber/black), restated the tree line with some taller clumps, painted a church tower with a nave at x=232 - it gives the scale. Heavy upper cloud bank in violet-grey with a warm lit rim scumbled on its underside. Water foreground: tried to deepen it but the blend lifted most of the dark - it still reads pale lilac. Next: wait, then deepen the near water with reflection of the dark cloud, break the ruler-straight bank edge, and put the boat and posts in.
- day 10, 14:35: Day 10: rebuilt the bank edge as a ragged, indented shoreline with the mud reaching out into the channel in lobes (the first try at separate lobes looked like stepping stones, so I painted one continuous new edge over them). Near water deepened with a lilac-grey reflection band. Tried pale gleams of wet on the mud; they came out as ruled stripes, a soft badger blend sank them into faint wet sheen, which is better.
- day 16, 06:18: Day 16: boat painted (tar-black hull, red strake, a pale gunwale line, ochre interior, thwarts, a thin contact shadow), mast and two stays; posts restated darker. Right sand bar repainted as a clean wedge with its reflection. Corrected the saw-tooth bank edge near the horizon into one smooth curve. Low sun glow scumbled in at right, a scatter of glints on the water under it. Tried to warm two white cloud blobs over dry paint - they turned into chalky scrubbed marks, and blending wet cloud paint with a wide badger dragged the upper bank into swirls. Blended a horizontal seam out of the mid sky. Next: after it dries, restate the top of the sky as a calmer, darker cloud canopy to frame the picture.
- day 19, 04:56: Day 19: a calmer darker canopy of cloud across the top, and a small skein of birds over the horizon glow (reads well at small size). Then trouble in the lower right: ruled horizontal streaks on the water, a patch of mud to hide streak ends that came out as a lighter wedge, and an unclipped badger blend that smeared mud into the water and water into the mud. Lesson again: no unclipped blending across the shoreline. Will let it dry and repaint the water and the mud there as separate clipped passes.
- day 22, 17:13: Day 22: repainted the mud along the shoreline with a new, simpler edge (clipped to a shore mask) - the shoreline now reads as one cut bank. But two unclipped/diagonal blends combed the mud and smeared the boat's bow. Must let it dry and restate the boat hull, bow and shadow firmly. No more blends near the boat.
- day 34, 00:04: Day 34: restated the boat's red strake and pale gunwale with a warm catch of light on the bow, repainted the lower right water as clean clipped horizontal bands of lilac-grey (fixing the earlier smears), laid a mooring rope from the bow to a stake in the mud. The squint view holds: dark mud wedge and boat below, the pale channel winding back to the glow on the horizon, a dark tree line with the church giving the distance, a heavy lilac canopy on top. Some flaws stay (a faint rectangular repaint by the stake, softness in the upper clouds), but the picture is together and further work risks muddying it. Calling it finished: 'Low Water, Evening'.
- day 45, 06:23: Day 45: came back with fresh eyes. The near mud was a flat, empty brown and the bottom edge didn't hold the picture in. Put a thin umber-black glaze over the lower mud (clipped, boat excluded), getting darker toward the bottom edge. Then four small puddles of different sizes: at first they looked like bright chalky pills, so I glazed them down (heavier on the near half, since the near part reflects the higher, darker sky), broke their ends with mud dragged in, gave them a dark lip on the far edge and a short broken gleam of sky light. They now read as wet hollows at full view. The lose strokes left faint lighter halos, which I've accepted. The foreground is darker and the eye goes up the channel. Stopping here.
- day 54, 06:46: Day 47: back once more. At close view the boat floated above its shadow - the keel line was crisp and a cool grey slab sat under it. Laid a ragged lip of mud over the hull's lower edge (first too light - it read as a pink sausage), restated it dark once it was tacky so the bottom of the hull is lost in its own shadow, and a thin warm umber glaze over the cool shadow slab; the glaze dragged some of the wet lip into faint horizontal streaks, which read as wet sheen round the hull. At full size the boat now sits down in the mud. Leaving it there.
- day 54, 06:46: Day 54: back again with fresh eyes. Two things still bother me: the post reflections are pale lilac stubs, lighter than the posts - reflections of dark posts in bright water should be nearly as dark as the posts themselves. And the puddle gleams are white scribbles, like cracked plaster. Plan: repaint the puddle interiors quietly in a mid lilac-grey (sky seen in a hollow), clipped, then restate the post reflections dark and broken.
- day 54, 12:00: Day 54, 12:00: Puddles repainted in a clean lilac-grey, darker in the near half (laid again once the first coat had set, so it stayed), ends broken with a little mud. They read now as thin slivers of sky lying in hollows instead of white scribbles; a little smooth at close view, but quiet at full size. Post reflections restated dark, each in two broken strokes with a pale waterline glint between post and reflection, so the posts stand in the water. Stopping: 'Low Water, Evening'.
