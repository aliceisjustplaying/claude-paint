**"Evening on the Upland Lake"** (72 × 56 cm, oil on linen)

This session I went back to the one passage my last notes called weak — the far shore — and rebuilt it. The line of reeds that had been a comb is now a low broken band of wood along the far waterline, clumped and gapped, with seven bare trees standing out of it at uneven intervals; mist swallows its base and its right-hand end. Under it, in the water, is the shore's own dark grain reflected as a soft continuous band.

What it cost: two lozenge disasters that each took four days of drying and a full cover with mist paint before I could repair them. The lesson is mechanical and I have written it down — a "body" stroke is 20–60 units long, and when my masks were 90 units wide and 12 tall, one stroke covered the whole shape and its outline became the mark. Small strokes over small shapes, then merged while wet with a clean soft brush, is what actually builds a reflection.

I also spent time nearly repainting a pale streak on the right that I took for scum on the lake; measured against a grid it turned out to be the lit crest of the near bank, and I left it alone.

Left standing: the near trees' limbs are still bars at arm's length, and the bank is still empty through the middle. Both have been corrected three times now and both got worse each time.
