## 1. General impressions, ranked

1. **H — strongest overall.** A traveler follows a winding route toward a ruined church; the warm, rubbed-looking sky and broken snow make the journey feel inhabited, though the tidy fir cluster and emphatic zigzag make it too illustrative. This could move me: the ruin feels distant enough to be a destination rather than a prop. (`H.png`)

3. **K — promising awkwardness.** The damaged tree, tiny figure and broad frozen pool give the scene an exposed, uneasy stillness; rough snow and bark help it feel made, but the oval pool and sawtooth forest make the space diagrammatic. 5. **N — coherent weather, insufficient resistance.** The traveler, curving blue route and bare tree sit in a convincing family of cold grays; the gentle light is appealing, but the smooth branch curves and neatly cut route make this feel like a tasteful illustration rather than an encounter with winter. ## 2. Paint or pixels?

For each, the most costly visual problem comes first.

- **Digital:** The person is a hat-and-coat emblem, while isolated grass clumps have repeated spacing and weight. The snow is much softer than either, so they seem placed on top. (`G_crop_lowerleft.png`)
- **Paint:** The capstone's muted brown-gray changes and uneven white top suggest paint building a solid mass. The soft distant ridge settling into snow is another good passage. Keep those rather than adding more sky grain. (`G_crop_center.png`, `G_crop_lowerleft.png`)

### H
- **Digital:** The fir group has repeated tiered construction; the path's successive bends are too insistently legible, explaining the journey instead of letting us discover it. (`H.png`)
- **Digital:** Long parallel bark stripes and continuous white branch ribbons describe surfaces too consistently. The crisp bird symbols and church outline also survive more sharply than their surroundings warrant. (`H_crop_lowerleft.png`, `H_crop_center.png`)
- **Paint:** This has the best sustained evidence of a hand moving across a surface: broad directional sky strokes, uneven thin coverage and overlapping snow strokes. The ruin's base dissolving into scrub is particularly useful edge handling. The crack web is unnecessary competition. (`H_crop_upperright.png`, `H_crop_center.png`, `H_crop_lowerleft.png`)

## 3. Advice, highest payoff first

1. **Preserve the emotional relationship; remove competing explanation.** H: keep traveler and ruin, simplify the firs and lose portions of the route. G: keep the overwhelming tree and small person, remove much of the twig chatter. K and M: keep the person facing empty cold space, make the pond less explicitly outlined. N: retain the weather, interrupt the route. J: clarify the stone opening before adding anything else. These priorities follow the whole-image problems above.

2. **Repaint boundaries, not just interiors.** Let snow strokes cover parts of roots, stone feet and path edges. Lose selected branches into sky. Let the ruin disappear further at its base. H already shows why this works around its church; G's tree and N's route show where it is most needed. Tool builders: prioritize strokes that cross and revise existing contours, not merely texture contained inside them. (`H_crop_center.png`, `G_crop_center.png`, `N.png`)

3. **Use fewer marks with more distinct jobs.** Paint a fir as several unequal dark masses before suggesting a few branches. Paint a trunk with broad changes of plane before adding bark. Stop detailing every twig. K's broken trunk is a better direction than N's internal striping or M's evenly tiered firs. Tool builders: vary pressure, loading and depletion within a stroke; avoid solving repetition with scattered random specks. (`K_crop_lowerleft.png`, `N_crop_lowerleft.png`, `M.png`)

4. **Judge without artificial aging.** Make the next G–M versions without visible cracks; remove K's moon halo too. Do not replace them with more grain. H's directional sky marks remain worth looking at without those effects; M's cracks currently overwhelm its quieter color decisions. (`H_crop_upperright.png`, `M_crop_upperright.png`, `K_crop_upperright.png`)

5. **Allow contact to change the paint.** At selected snow–earth and snow–bark boundaries, drag a little existing color into the next stroke; elsewhere leave a dry, broken edge. Do not soften everything equally. N's smooth fields against wiry branches and G's isolated figure show the separation to overcome. Tool builders: test pickup and overpainting at these contacts before adding finer bristle detail. (`N_crop_lowerleft.png`, `G_crop_lowerleft.png`)

6. **Keep awkward decisions that carry form.** Keep K's scraped trunk, H's uneven snow and J's broad stone dabs—but reorganize J's dabs into light-facing planes, side planes and an intelligible opening. “More bad painter” should mean a visible attempt to see and revise, not a clean diagram covered with damage. (`K_crop_lowerleft.png`, `H.png`, `J_crop_lowerleft.png`)
