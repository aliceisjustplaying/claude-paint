- day 1, 09:00: Design: an oblique street view of a small American cleaners, afternoon. Pale sunlit storefront against a brick side wall in blue shade; a seated woman in the right-hand window, a folded paper and an unused chair. The empty pavement will carry the strongest diagonal. I want the architectural intervals to feel longer than the human action. Warm cream priming, lean broad lay-in, then opaque, largely wet-over-dry planes; no borrowed image.
- day 3, 14:02: The first broad films were too patchy; dry body coats have made the outside planes calm. Keep that distinction: quiet exterior and a more worked interior, but remove the accidental swirling from the window before introducing the woman. The cool foreground wedge now carries the oblique street direction. Upper sash windows and the recessed green door should supply depth without competing with the lit right-hand pane.
- day 19, 23:04: The seated pose is readable at the first silhouette: knees to the left, chair behind, the near forearm carried across the body. The head and bare arm picked up their wet underlayers. Let them harden, then restore a few broad flesh planes rather than describe every feature. The paper/table will explain the gesture. The sign will be hand-lettered, not perfectly typographic.
- day 63, 05:17: The whole picture is now legible: cream facade, green recesses, blue pavement shadow, rust blouse. Value check keeps the figure inside the bright pane, while the unused chair and hanging coats make the left pane quiet. The figure's planes need a little fusing at the cheek and forearm; retain the lowered eye. Finish the glass, sash joinery, door hardware, and restrained pavement seams. Do not fill the empty street with activity.
- day 71, 07:16: The pale glazes caught the high points as conspicuous little lacy marks—wrong for these calm windows. Paint those passages back into the room colors, keep only sparse vertical glints. The street now has scale from the hydrant, drain, and converging paving joints. Final work: a few sash bars in the shaded building, newsprint suggested rather than written, and softer garment edges. The picture should remain about waiting, not about inventory.
- day 102, 08:19: Complete: “Between Customers.” The last cheek scumble was too broken; a clean flesh plane and three restrained values restored the profile. Final whole, mirrored, close, and squinted checks: the store sign establishes the public street, but the woman and empty chair hold the private pause. Keep the broad road and the sun/shade boundary quiet. No further narrative objects or surface decoration.
- day 102, 08:19: Returning to the easel: the whole view, close figure, and squinted value study confirm the picture is finished. The warm window holds the seated woman without breaking the long, quiet architectural intervals. Leave the empty pavement and the unoccupied chair as they are; no further paint.
