# r14_p1: Morning in the Mountains

Program: `paintings/src/bin/r14_p1.rs`. Render: `out/r14_p1_full.png` (2400 px wide,
`cargo paint r14_p1 -- --full --width 2400`).

## The composition and why

From a granite summit before sunrise. The glow sits low on the left, on the long flank of a far
mountain that rises from its ridge to a worn cone, where the sun will come up. The ranges step
back in value toward the horizon, each one paler, warmer and softer than the one in front of
it, and each one's foot is lost in mist. A sea of mist fills the valleys; a ridge lies half
drowned in it, and two dark wooded ridges rise out of it, serrated with small fir tops. The near
summit runs across the bottom, dark, with heath and granite blocks. On the highest block at the
left a small man seen from behind stands with his stick, facing the glow. On the summit's high
right side a group of spruces of unequal heights stands against the mist and the lower sky. The
old moon, a thin crescent, hangs above the glow.

What in Friedrich it draws on (from notes/research/friedrich_materials.md and the notebook, never
from pictures):
- the Riesengebirge and Bohemian pictures: a high viewpoint, ranges receding in value steps,
  mist lying in the valleys, a sky that takes most of the picture's light;
- stippled sky, mist and far hills [NG p.56]; thin paint that pools in the ground [CATS p.127];
- firs as short hatched strokes over a dark mass [NG pp.49–50; notebook §6];
- grass as fine upturning strokes laid last [NG p.56];
- a Dresden ground: warm lower layers under a patchy whitish lead-white top [KÖR p.284]. I
  chose the whitish top so the cool upper sky wouldn't be pulled toward khaki (notebook §1).
- a Rückenfigur, small and still, the only hard dark against the brightest mist;
- the old moon at dawn and a glaze darker toward the edges (his advice to Carus, [MET p.35]);
- a still, balanced arrangement: the figure and his rocks on one side, the spruces on the
  other, the glow and the mountain between, and the horizon as the picture's most important
  line.

## Working method, stage by stage

Stages in the program: sky, far, ridges, summit, spruces, figure, heath, veil, moon, finish.

1. **Ground.** Style::friedrich's three warm layers, plus a fourth: a whitish lead-white top,
   brushed, hiding 0.6, 30 µm (the Dresden "patchy whitish ground", [KÖR p.284]).
2. **Sky** (about 105 s of a 173 s render at 2400 px). My own color field, not the physical
   sky: I ran `atmos::Sky` once to see what a sun 2° under the horizon gives (pale blue-gray
   top, a yellow band, orange at the horizon) and then chose the colors myself: a smalt-gray
   top, a pale greenish middle, a warm glow off center over where the sun will rise, and
   rose-lilac low down away from the glow. The height fraction is wandered by a horizontally
   stretched noise, so the bands are unequal. Six level cloud streaks are part of the same color
   field, violet-gray with warm undersides, so the lay-in lays them into the wet sky. Then:
   smaller strokes along the streaks while wet, the badger top to bottom, a wet stipple aimed at
   the field, then dry, a finer stipple barely lighter than the field and denser toward the
   glow. The sky paint is carried 30 units below the horizon, so the ranges are cut over it.
3. **Far ranges** (far1, far2, far3, plus a "ghost" ridge half drowned in the mist). Each is a
   `Layer`: a crest profile I drew as a function (a broad massif on the left, a jagged range
   from the right, the farthest a long low line), a body color by x (warmer toward the glow),
   and two depths below the crest where the mist starts and is complete. The mist is mixed
   into the range's *own paint* below an uneven line (noise), so there is no veil laid on top.
   Each layer is painted down past the next layer's crest; the next one covers it. Where a
   range should end, a "sink" factor turns its whole paint to mist, so ranges dissolve instead
   of stopping. The badger fuses only the misted part, never the crest.
4. **Ridges.** The two wooded ridges rising out of the mist are the same kind of layer, darker
   and bluer, their crests serrated by a row of small fir tops in groups with gaps.
5. **Summit.** Granite blocks as `Sdf` blocks (turned, cut three times, weathered) in one
   `Form`, lit from the glow behind and above (contre-jour, ambient 0.4). The ground and the
   rocks' dark sides go down in one pass of one dark (the rock/ground seam is the same paint).
   The sky-facing tops are laid into the wet dark in broken strokes, a little lighter and cool.
   A thin warm rim on the edges turned toward the glow, broken by noise.
6. **Spruces** (five, unequal, the farther first). Each is built from boughs: tiers at uneven
   spacing, now and then a bough missing or short; each bough a drooping band with its tip
   turned up and a ragged curtain of twigs hanging under it. That union, plus a narrow core, is
   the silhouette, hatched down and out along the boughs. Then twig ends pulled past the
   curtain's edge, sky light on the bough tops toward the glow, the leader with a rigger.
7. **Figure.** A man seen from behind on the top of the tilted block, facing the glow: a long
   coat narrowing to the shoulders and flaring at the hem, legs apart, a soft cap, a stick in
   his right hand, a thin warm rim only on the side toward the glow. His feet are found by
   scanning the rock form at his x.
8. **Heath.** In the summit stage, after an hour's wait (the dark has begun to set), short
   upright hatching patch by patch in close darks: olive, the rust of heather, a cool
   gray-green, larger nearer. Then in its own stage: tufts of blades from one base, fanning,
   larger and more numerous nearer, mostly dark, a few catching the sky (more high up), a
   lighter rim color on the skyline, and dark tufts along the skyline against the mist.
9. **Veil.** Friedrich's advice to Carus, a glaze over the whole picture growing darker toward
   the edges [MET p.35]: a thin film of cobalt and black in much medium, its depth rising with
   distance from the glow and varied by a slow noise, laid with `Canvas::glaze` (a film that
   levels and pools in the surface's hollows), not brushed (see friction).
10. **Moon.** A thin waning crescent over the place where the sun will rise, its lit limb turned
   toward the sun under the horizon (so it tilts), laid last in short touches along the limb.
11. **Finish.** `Finish::aged` with the cracks narrowed (primary opening 12 µm), no corner
   cracks, less grime.

Iterations so far (all judged at 2400 px, whole and in crops):
- v1: the ranges each ended in a level cut, so the mist between them read as **lakes** with
  ruled shores; the left massif was a mesa; the spruces were a row of Christmas trees (a smooth
  sawtooth silhouette); the summit was olive and too light; the rocks were pillows with snow-cap
  tops; the crack net covered everything like a grid.
- v2: layers painted down past the next crest with mist in their own paint → the mist reads as
  mist. But the spruces, rebuilt from gestures only, became dark spikes with hairline whiskers:
  a pointed round at light pressure lays a hairline, not a needle-clad bough.
- v3: spruce silhouette built as a union of bough bands with hanging curtains → spruces.
- v4–v6: mist a step below the glow and banded; ghost ridge; boulders; a figure on the outcrop;
  cracks narrowed.
- v7–v8: far ranges given more body before the mist takes them; the sky just above each far
  crest carried back over it in broken places (first as sky color, which left a pale ragged
  fringe; then as a half tone between sky and range, laid mostly below the crest); the
  bottom-right block removed (a pale box); spruces regrouped unevenly (two overlapping).
- v8→v9: the cloud streaks read as dotted chains of dark beads. Cause: the wet stipple aimed
  each dip at the streak's target color, but the badger had already fused the streak lighter,
  so the touches landed darker than what they sat on. Fix: the wet stipple takes its color
  from the paint under it, moved only 30% toward the target. The beads vanished, the clouds
  became soft banks.
- v10–v11: heath texture and tufts. First the tufts were pale checkmarks evenly scattered
  (confetti) and the heath colors lightened the ground to olive; darker colors, mostly dark
  blades and stronger clumping fixed both.
- v12–v13: the moon (first a "smile" with its horns straight up; moved right of the glow so
  the line to the sun tilts it, made thinner and nearer the sky's value); the summit cooled
  and its upper slope given a broader cool sheen from the sky.
- v14: the veil brushed with `st.glaze` and fused with the badger drew dark worm-like squiggles
  across the upper right sky and a dark line down the right edge (fluid glaze pushed into the
  rims of strokes). v15: the same veil as a `Canvas::glaze` film: clean, and it gathers the
  picture onto the glow without a visible oval.
- v16: the spruces' sky light was invisible. A test in pure white showed why: the touches
  were laid, but into the wet dark boughs, and mixed there into a faint smear over the whole
  bough. Letting the boughs set (two hours on the drying clock) and mixing the light lighter
  gave the bough tops a green-gray sheen, and the trees some volume.
- v17: small granite stones scattered in the heath came out as pale soft smudges ("ghosts"):
  in that light their lit tops were the only thing to see. Removed.
- v19–v21: the far massif redrawn as a long ridge rising to a worn cone right of the sunrise
  (the way the Schneekoppe ends its ridge). First it was a symmetrical pointed cone that read
  as a volcano out of a Japanese print; made asymmetric (long flank toward the glow, steep
  away), lower and rounded at the top.
- v22: cutting in the crest with a small round (`Handling::cut_in`) scalloped it into a lumpy
  "cotton" edge. Reverted.
- v24: spruce cores widened (raggedly), so the sky shows only toward the bough ends and not
  between every pair of boughs at the trunk (the "fishbone"); rock tops entered through a
  halftone and a little darker.
- v25–v28: stronger billows in the nearer mist (a small gain). Rows of small inverted
  triangles showed under the wooded ridges, like fir tops reflected in water. Two causes: a
  ridge that sinks into the mist still had a serrated crest, so its mist paint cut dark teeth
  into the ridge behind; and the mist line inside a ridge was measured from the serrated crest,
  so it copied the teeth, upside down, into the mist. The fir tops now fade where a ridge
  sinks, the mist line is measured from the line the tops stand on, and the farther layers are
  carried down to the summit so no edge lies buried under a nearer range's pale mist.
- v29: a trodden path winding up from the bottom to the outcrop, a step lighter than the heath,
  to give the foreground something particular and lead to the figure. It read as a faint
  straight diagonal band, a beam or a ramp, not ground. Reverted.
- Final: a clean render from scratch (173 s); a resume from "ridges" reproduces it byte for
  byte.

## FRICTION

1. **Blank lines count in a checkpoint's fingerprint.** I tagged new helper functions
   `// ckpt: from summit` / `// ckpt: from figure`, but the blank line I left between two
   tagged regions (outside both) changed the hash of every earlier stage: "checkpoint ridges is
   stale". Workaround: `--stale-ok --ckpt` after convincing myself the only change was
   whitespace. The fingerprint could hash tokens, not lines.
2. **Helper functions count for every stage, wherever they sit.** Everything before a stage's
   block counts, and everything after `main` counts for all stages. So a painting's motif
   helpers stale the 100 s sky unless each is wrapped in a `ckpt: from` region. That works but
   is easy to forget; a helper used only by later stages should not have to be tagged by hand.
3. **`atmos::Ranges` gave me two ranges almost on top of each other** (5000 m and 5612 m, the
   second rising above the horizon) and no way to say "this one here". Its layers are spaced
   "unevenly in log distance" by seed. I drew my own crest profiles instead, which also let me
   make ranges sink into the mist on purpose.
4. **Default cracks read as a dark grid at 2400 px.** `Finish::aged` fits the crack opening from
   the island size (tens of µm), and at 0.18 mm per pixel every crack became a visible dark
   line over the pale sky, with ruled corner cracks at the top left. `dirt`, `grime` and
   `depth_um` barely changed it; only `width_um: Some(12.0)` did (the struct's doc says so:
   "To quiet a network, narrow it"). The preset is tuned for 3200 px or darker pictures.
5. **A pointed round at light pressure lays a hairline.** For spruce boughs I wanted a
   needle-clad band 2–5 units thick; the brush's own width barely showed. Workaround: build the
   bough mass as a mask (a band plus its hanging curtain) and hatch it, then use gestures only
   for the ends.
6. **No "behind" for masks.** To make each range stand in front of the one behind and let the
   mist lie between, every layer has to be painted down past the next one's crest, with
   hand-written `bottom` functions (max/min of other crests). One wrong bottom leaves a hard
   level edge that reads as a shore.
7. **Bare-ground flecks inside a lay-in.** A small pale scrape showed on the far massif. It was
   ground, not paint: the bristles plough paint off the weave's peaks. Only raising coverage
   from 3.2 to 4 removed it (as notes/strokes.md says). A painter would have touched the one
   fleck; the program has to pay for a denser pass over the whole range.
8. **A wet stipple aimed at a color field goes wrong over a blended passage.** The badger moves
   the paint away from the field the stipple aims at, so every touch is a speck of contrast.
   `color_over` (take the paint beneath, move it a little) is the cure; the default `color`
   aim is the trap.
9. **The sky is about 105 s of a 173 s render**, so every change to it costs a full render; every
   later stage resumes in 1–20 s. As the notebook says, the sky gets the fewest looks. I tested
   sky changes on `--crop ... --stop sky` windows (18 s) first.
10. **A brushed glaze fused with the badger draws worms.** `st.glaze(0.93)` over the whole
    canvas with `load_at` rising to the edges, then `st.blend()`, left dark squiggles like
    marbling across the pale sky and a dark line down one edge. `Canvas::glaze` (a film) did
    the job cleanly, but it is not a brush: I had to give up the hand in that pass.
11. **Touches into a wet dark dissolve.** Sky light on spruce boughs laid over the still-wet
    hatching was mixed away completely; I only found out by painting it pure white. There is
    no way to see what a pass laid except by exaggerating its color. A view that shows one
    pass's paint would have saved three renders. `c.wait` before the touches fixed it.
12. **Cut-in scallops a curved crest.** `Handling::cut_in(round)` on a mountain crest laid
    short round strokes that follow the outline as a string of bumps. The thin pale rim it
    was meant to cure (stroke edges over a lighter sky) stays; I accepted it as the backlit
    edge.
## Critique

What works. The distance. The ranges step back in value and warmth and stand in the mist; the
mist reads as mist, not water, because no range ends at a level line. The worn cone of the far
mountain gives the picture a place and a center, and the glow on its long flank tells you where
the sun will come up. The small dark figure on the highest rock against the brightest mist is
where the eye lands and then goes on to where he looks. The spruce group, unequal and
overlapping, reads as spruces at the full size: drooping boughs, curtains of twigs, dense near
the trunk. The veil darker toward the edges gathers the picture onto the glow without showing.
The sky is soft, with cloud banks that sit in it and no beads.

What doesn't, or not yet:
- The foreground is a large dark field. It is right for the light (contre-jour before sunrise),
  and the heath texture and skyline tufts keep it from being dead, but it holds nothing
  particular, and a quarter of the picture is given to it. Friedrich would have put something
  there (a path, a plant drawn from a study) that rewards looking close. My attempt at stones
  failed; I didn't find the thing.
- The spruces are still a little graphic, silhouettes more than trees with depth: their boughs
  are all in the picture plane, none come toward us. At viewing distance they pass.
- The rocks read as granite boulders, but their silhouettes are a little soft and rounded, and
  the big block under the figure is more a plinth than a tor.
- A faint pale rim follows parts of the far mountain's crest where its stroke edges thin over
  the lighter sky. I let it stand as the backlit edge; it isn't quite that.
- The sky's glow band is smooth enough, up close, that the notebook's charge ("close to a
  digital gradient at viewing distance") still partly applies. The unequal bands and the
  stipple help but don't cure it.
- The mist sea in the middle is broad and a little uniform; the ghost ridge helps, but the
  billows I mixed into the mist are too subtle to read as volume.
