# r14_p3: Evening on the Baltic shore

Program: `paintings/src/bin/r14_p3.rs`. Render: `out/r14_p3_full.png` (2400 px wide, aspect 1.4).

## The picture and why

The sun has gone down a little left of center. The afterglow lies along the rim of a calm
sea, and a thin young moon hangs above and to the right of the sun's place. A stony Rügen beach
runs across the foreground. On the right a big granite erratic lies at the water's edge, with a
lower stone behind it and two flat ones out in the shallows. On the left a crooked line of
fishermen's stakes walks out and away into the sea. A woman in a dark gown stands at the
water's edge between the stakes and the stone, her head just breaking the sea's rim, looking at
the place where the sun went down. A small galliot lies on the horizon on the right.

What it draws on in Friedrich (from notes/research/friedrich_materials.md and what is known of
his motifs, not from pictures):
- The Baltic coast of Rügen and Greifswald, erratic boulders on beaches, evening and moonrise
  over the sea, a lone figure seen from behind looking out: his most constant motifs.
- A low horizon with a large quiet sky, the glow gathered in one place, the land and the
  figure as dark shapes against it (contre-jour after sunset: no direct light, only the sky).
- One hard silhouette (the figure), everything else lost into the dusk.
- Strict, almost geometric placing: the stakes as a rhythm of verticals, the figure at the
  glow, the stone as a counterweight on the right.
- Smalt/cobalt, lead white, ochres, umber, black: the 1820 palette (`Style::friedrich()`).
- A dark glaze deepening toward the edges, after his advice to Carus [MET PDF p.35].

## Method, stage by stage

1. **sky**: a lean dead color in the sky's own tones, barely darker (so the warm ground can't
   show through thin blue); then the lay-in: long level strokes of thin paint from my own sky
   plan (a few piles by height above the rim, blended as light; the glow gathered over the
   sun's place and reaching higher there; thin level cloud streaks in the plan so they are
   laid in the wet sky); badger top to bottom, and once more over the upper sky; the streaks
   again with a small filbert in long level strokes into the wet; a stipple into the wet taken
   from the paint beneath and moved a little toward the plan; dry; a finer stipple barely
   lighter, denser toward the glow.
2. **sea**: level strokes colored by what the rippled water mirrors (the scene's `mirror`),
   mixed as light over a slate water body, a step darker and cooler than the sky; a broad soft
   glow path under the sun's place; badger; a few long straight glints in the path, into the
   wet; faint swell lines crowding toward the horizon.
3. **beach**: level strokes; far sand lighter (air), near sand darker, a mauve-gray brown; low
   wind ridges in the plan; a wrack line; a wet strip at the water's edge that gives back the
   low sky; then wind-ripple lines; dry; pebbles as flat dark dashes, few, in drifts.
4. **stones**: all stones are solids in the scene (one sun below the horizon), painted with
   `rocks::paint_solid` and my own color by plane: faces toward me in shadow, planes facing up
   take the cool sky, faces turned to the glow a little warmth.
   Then a dark glaze in the contacts and a few dark pebbles across the bottom of each outline.
5. **stakes**: dark silhouettes from the scene's bodies, cut at their waterlines; wobbling drags
   of varied darks up each post (timber); a thin light on the side turned to the glow and on
   the tops; their images mirrored about each waterline, in short level strokes broken into
   bars by a ripple field.
6. **woman**: `figures::woman`, standing, dark gown and shawl, dark hands; then my own
   gestures: a shawl end hanging free on one side, the head turned a little toward the glow,
   the hem dipping on the foot she stands on.
7. **moon**: one pointed stroke along the lit limb, turned toward the sun below the rim.
8. **ship**: hull, masts with a rigger, sails in short level strokes.
9. **gulls**: three, two wing flicks lifted off from a body touch.
10. **veil**: an even glaze of a neutral dark deepening away from the glow and toward the
    bottom edge.
11. finish: a thin aged varnish, a quieter craquelure (narrow opening, less grime), relief.

  beach, the erratic a loaf, the stakes huddled like a fence, the moon a smile, a loud dark
  crack net over the sky.

## FRICTION

Top five first (the ones that cost the most looks or hid the most), then the rest.

1. **The finish is where the color went, and nothing says so.** The aged varnish (0.4 coats of
   a yellow film) and my umber veil took about 30 levels of blue out of the upper sky: blue
   under yellow is gray. The sky stage matched my plan within a few levels; only a
   `--stop sky` render compared pixel by pixel with the whole found it, after a dozen renders
   of "why is the sky so dull". The finish runs on every render, so the eye adapts to it.
   Workaround: varnish 0.15 coats, veil a neutral dark. Wish: `Finish` reporting what it does
   to a few sample colors, or a `--no-finish` switch next to `--no-cracks`.
2. **The physical sky isn't a painter's dusk, and gradients between complements go gray.**
   `atmos::Sky` 4.5° after sunset gave a saturated daytime blue overhead and a glow of even
   strength all along the rim; its clouds came out as ochre blotches. I wrote my own plan of
   piles. Blending that plan's stops with `Mix::Pigment` sent yellow into lilac through a
   muddy green-gray; `Mix::Light` fixed it. Neither is a bug, but nothing warns a painter.
3. **Code above `main` stales every checkpoint, including code that paints nothing.** My
   scene builder (`build`, `shore`, `ground`), `water_body` and my `--plan`/`--probe`
   diagnostics sat above the first stage, so moving a stone, darkening the water or adding a
   probe made the 85–125 s sky stale. Fixed with `// ckpt: from sea` and `// ckpt: from veil`
   regions, but only after half a dozen needless whole renders; and a single
   `--resume X --stale-ok --ckpt` adopts only X, so adopting meant one run per stage. A tag
   for "no stage" (diagnostics) would help.
4. **Thin things in water: stub reflections, one water level.** `View::reflections` gave short
   pale segments under thin posts. I mirrored each stake myself about its waterline
   (`World::project`, a `Form` lookup per pixel, a ripple field to bar it). And `Water` has one
   level, so a tide pool on a beach above the sea had to be dug down to sea level, where its
   surface lay under the lip of the sand in front and couldn't be seen.
5. **The sky is 80% of every render.** Dead color, lay-in, two badgers, the streak pass and two
   stipples: 85 s at first, 125 s at the end; everything else together is 20 s. So the sky
   got the fewest looks and was changed in batches, as the notebook predicted. Each
   checkpoint is 313 MB at 2400 px, 3.4 GB per `--ckpt` run.

The rest:

6. **Missing notes.** README lists `notes/scene.md`, `notes/atmosphere.md`, `notes/tip.md`,
   `notes/surface.md`, `notes/drying.md`; none exist in this studio. I read the module docs in
   `scene.rs` and `atmos.rs` and the study programs instead.
7. **A `Held` brush can't change size.** Pebbles of many sizes needed a new `Held::new` per
   pebble (its `tool` field is public, but the bristles are built at `new`).
8. **Form samples don't know where they are.** `Form::mask(|s| ..)` hands me a `Sample`
   without its canvas position, so "the side of each stake turned toward the glow" needed a
   side table from part id to the stake's x.
9. **Small marks of dark paint on a figure dry much lighter than their masstone.** A shawl
   mixed to a dark red-brown came out bright orange-brown; I had to ask for a near-black to
   get a warm dark.
10. **Touches into a wet lay-in vanish.** Pebble touches into the wet beach fused completely;
    after drying they were round disks. Not wrong physics, but there is no quick way to see
    what a pass did except rendering it alone.
11. **No PIL/numpy.** To measure pixel values I made a `uv` venv in scratch; measuring was how
    I found that the pale stones and the gray sky were painted exactly as asked and my plan
    (or the finish) was wrong.

## Critique

What works. The sky is the picture's best passage: a blue-violet evening going down through
lilac and a pearly band to the gold rim, stippled so it has grain but no stroke direction,
with thin dark streaks lying across the glow and a few rose wisps higher up. The sea reads as
slate water at once, with its plane given by faint swell lines and a soft glow path. The
woman is the one hard shape, her head just over the sea's rim, and the eye goes to her and
then to the glow. The stakes, with their stumps and the one leaning, and their barred
reflections, are the most particular things in the picture. The erratic reads as split
granite with its broken back taking the sky. The ship and the gulls are small and quiet.

What doesn't. The composition is correct rather than inspired: stakes left, figure center,
stone right, like props set out on a stage; Friedrich's severity would have cut one of them.
The foreground's lower third is a dark, even field; the pebbles and ripples give it a surface
at full size, but at a distance it is empty rather than charged, like the dunes in the *Monk*.
The woman is better than the helper's pattern (a shawl end hanging free, her head turned a
little toward the glow) but still close to a long bottle with a knot of hair. The small stones in the foreground are generic lumps. The erratic's outline
is smooth where granite would be broken, and its left face takes a little too much light
for a stone with the glow behind it. The low streaks are still a little regular, like
ruled lines at the whole view. The craquelure is quiet but even, one net over the whole.
The upper sky is soft and a little heavy; a painter with another day would lay it once more,
cleaner, and let the blue sing.

Friedrich's side of the ledger: the palette (lead white, cobalt, ochres, earths, umber, black),
a dead color under a thin sky, stippling, contre-jour silhouettes, one figure from behind,
a Rügen beach with erratics, the darkened edges. What isn't his: I suspect his beach would be
emptier and his horizon lower, and he would not have put in both the ship and the gulls.
