# Wet control experiment (branch r6-wet, not merged)

Copied from branch `r6-wet` (`notes/wet/`, full write-up in `notes/wet.md`
§8 there) so Alice can view it on main.

**Control sheet** (`owner_control_sheet.jpg`, details `_open`, `_setting`):
three engines stacked (main; r6-wet; r6-wet + lift-off). Rows: A a light
accent into a wet dark, B a contour softened, C a flat's stroke end, D a
glaze-hand veil. Columns: drying stage (open, setting, tacky, dry) × load
(full, lean) × pressure (light, firm).

| test | main | r6-wet | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| accent into open paint, retained light (light / firm) | 0.62 / 0.65 | 0.82 / 0.94 | clean accents: pass |
| accent into setting paint | 0.68 / 0.58 | 0.99 / 0.96 | pass |
| veil over open sky, track showing ground | 13–41% | 0% | pass |
| flat along a wet contour, edge width at 1000 | 4–5 mm (any load) | 0.44 mm full, 1.3–1.8 mm lean | soft edges: partial |
| same at 3200 | 2.5 mm | up to 2.3 mm | about equal |

**Repaints** (`owner_repaint_*.jpg`): the panel's four losing studies,
each log adapted in the one passage that depended on paint sinking (same
number of chunks), main vs r6-wet + lift-off, whole over a 3200 crop.
Judge them blind first; the key is at the bottom.

The engineer's recommendation: don't make r6-wet the default yet (clean
accents and no exposed ground, but soft edges only from lean or blended
strokes); next, a real softening mechanism (a wet stroke's thin end
leveling sideways into the paint around it), or ship it as an opt-in
"wet control" setting now. The integrator's note: on the sky repaint the
r6-wet version looked the softer of the two to me, the opposite of the
engineer's read, so Alice's eye decides.
