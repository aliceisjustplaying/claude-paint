## 1. General impressions

A solitary figure stands above a still mountain lake, facing a pale evening glow beneath heavy clouds. The dark fir promontory holds the left side while the figure gives the open right side a small, human weight. It feels hushed and withdrawn rather than threatening. The strongest quality is the light held between the dark land masses: the yellow-gray sky and its reflection make the lake feel like an opening into distance. The composition works before any detail is noticed. (Source: `whole_1000.png`.)

To my eye, this approaches Friedrich most successfully through restraint, human smallness and the sense that looking into a landscape is itself the subject. Its biggest weakness is the separation between broad atmosphere and insistently described objects. Trees, grass and the figure sometimes seem placed on top of the light rather than existing inside it. The mountain and its reflection also close into a conspicuous, nearly lens-shaped unit, competing with the more mysterious space around them. I would preserve the subdued color and asymmetry but make fewer things equally explicit. (Source: `whole_1000.png`; `sheet.png`, all three crops.)

## 2. Paint or pixels?

Overall: painterly from a distance, noticeably digital in the crops. The convincing brushwork lies mainly inside the shapes; the mechanical impression comes mainly from their boundaries and repeated small marks. (Source: `whole_1000.png`; `sheet.png`.)

### Passages that read as paint

- **Mountain interior and foreground earth.** Broad, overlapping gray strokes turn across the mountain, while dark olive patches build the bank. Their uneven coverage and changes of direction suggest a brush laying one mixture over another. These are more convincing than a uniform surface texture would be. (Source: `sheet.png`, middle and right crops.)
- **Clouds and the warm sky below them.** Soft gray-violet passages mingle with ocher light without every cloud receiving an outline. The low clouds retain some lateral brush drag. This mixture of blended areas and visible strokes feels handled rather than merely blurred. (Source: `whole_1000.png`; `sheet.png`, left crop.)
- **Some fir boughs.** Broken green-gray touches over the dark trees suggest paint catching on an earlier layer. Locally, individual branch marks have useful irregularity, even where the overall trees become repetitive. (Source: `sheet.png`, left crop.)

### Digital or mechanical passages, most costly first

1. **The mountain/reflection boundary.** A remarkably continuous ridge encloses loosely brushed paint; below it, the reflected mountain has another long, clean boundary. That combination resembles a filled selection. Soften selected stretches of the distant ridge into the sky. In the reflection, carry a few broad horizontal water strokes across the boundary and lose whole sections of it—not just add fine ripple texture inside. (Source: `sheet.png`, middle crop.)

2. **The fir system.** Many trees repeat the same narrow, tapering silhouette and downward branch rhythm. Against the glow, tiny bright holes in the forest mass can read as perforations. Merge neighboring trees into larger dark groups, vary crown width and branch spacing more decisively and reserve articulated branches for a few trees. Paint back into the silhouette with the sky mixture to make fewer, larger openings. (Source: `sheet.png`, left crop.)

3. **The figure and walking stick.** The figure has a nearly continuous crisp perimeter, rounded head and tube-like arms; the stick is a very thin, steady line. It reads more as an icon than a painted body. Vary the contour with a few opaque brush touches, lose an edge into the gray water and distinguish one shoulder or coat plane with a muted value change. Give the stick a slight change in thickness rather than equal precision throughout. (Source: `sheet.png`, right crop.)

4. **Grass along the bank.** Numerous fine hooks and threads remain individually legible across the skyline and foreground. They resemble drawn wire over broader paint. Cover most with the bank mixture, then rebuild a handful of uneven clumps using a loaded brush. Leave substantial quiet gaps. (Source: `sheet.png`, right crop.)

5. **Light scuffs in the reflected mountain.** Several pale islands contain similarly fine, parallel broken tracks. They attract attention as surface artifacts rather than describing mist or water. Glaze some down and replace others with fewer, broader horizontal strokes of varied opacity. (Source: `sheet.png`, middle crop and upper right crop.)

6. **The moon.** The tiny crescent looks angular, almost like a bent bright staple. Repaint it as one small curved touch, then soften one tip with the surrounding sky. Keep its scale and avoid increasing its brightness. (Source: `whole_1000.png`.)
