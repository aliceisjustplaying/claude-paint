pair1: prefer Y (strength: slight)
why: Y organizes the upper-left light into fuller foliage masses, while X disperses it into small flecks. Y also lets the crown meet the sky less abruptly, though neither resolves the large dark lower half convincingly. Evidence: pair1_X.jpg, pair1_Y.jpg and their crops.
X defects: The right outline and interior sky holes have uniformly sharp, torn-paper edges, making the crown look cut out. Fine, nearly continuous stippling substitutes for distinct foliage planes. The lower crown becomes an opaque slab with little sense of overlapping branches.
Y defects: Rounded highlight clusters at upper left look like tightly packed knobs rather than varied leaf masses. The crop shows repeated diagonal grooves across the shadow foliage, unrelated to its growth. Soft rims around the sky holes feel applied uniformly rather than selectively lost in atmosphere.

pair2: prefer Y (strength: clear)
why: Y gives the rock clearer warm and cool planes and joins its shaded foot to the ground more convincingly. Its quieter foreground also avoids X’s distracting fringe of individual grass hairs. Evidence: pair2_X.jpg, pair2_Y.jpg and their crops.
X defects: The cast shadow has a hard polygonal outer boundary even far from the rock, reading as a separate flat shape. Thin grass strokes crowd the entire base and scatter across the foreground with little grouping. The bright face is covered in insistent tiny pits that compete with the rock’s larger form.
Y defects: Parallel ridges cover several rock planes without changing direction enough to explain their different orientations. The cast shadow has such dense raised texture that it resembles a dark mat laid on the grass. The light face’s bottom edge ends in a conspicuously regular row of short vertical marks.

pair3: prefer X (strength: clear)
why: X makes a more continuous evening atmosphere, with quieter warm cloud undersides and a less brutally separated horizon. Y’s simpler arrangement is useful, but its cloud marks and land edge are too conspicuous. Evidence: pair3_X.jpg, pair3_Y.jpg and their crops.
X defects: The two low cloud banks look like horizontal gray smears with blunt ends rather than varied cloud bodies. Pale strokes along the dark land’s upper edge suggest chunky nearby rocks, weakening the distant horizon. Small scattered warm sky marks lack a clear grouping.
Y defects: The land meets the sky in an uninterrupted, razor-clean edge that looks digitally masked. Orange cloud undersides form repeated rounded tabs, especially at lower right. The strongly mottled yellow sky reads as surface grain before it reads as luminous air.

pair4: prefer Y (strength: clear)
why: Y’s softened reflection and less rigid distant shore make the land sit within the scene rather than on top of it. The distinction between upright vegetation and downward-drawn reflected paint helps the water read. Evidence: pair4_X.jpg, pair4_Y.jpg and their crops.
X defects: The foreground shore and reflected outline are equally crisp, making a closed, pasted-on silhouette. The distant bank is a rigid double strip that flattens the space. Even granular foliage texture gives little indication of separate bushes or their direction of growth.
Y defects: Repeated upright loops along the vegetation crest look like a decorative fringe. The crop reveals conspicuous wiry marks around the shore tip and hanging into its reflection. Broad diagonal strokes and isolated peach dashes in the open water compete with its intended stillness.

pair5: prefer X (strength: strong)
why: X retains an intelligible network of winter twigs and open sky. Y’s pale scribbled masses obscure that structure and read more like fuzzy foliage than bare branching. Evidence: pair5_X.jpg, pair5_Y.jpg and their crops.
X defects: Fine angular twig forks recur too evenly across the crown, producing a diagrammatic mesh. Major branches remain almost uniformly dark and sharp, with little recession. Scattered grass hairs repeat the twig fussiness instead of establishing a simple ground mass.
Y defects: Dense gray hatching gathers in detached-looking patches around the crown, replacing branching with fuzz. Several dark limbs terminate abruptly inside those patches rather than tapering into readable twigs. The trunk ends at a small dark socket beside a smeared horizontal shadow, leaving the tree’s footing unconvincing.

Across the ten studies, the most costly recurring defect is insufficient edge hierarchy: crisp enclosed silhouettes detach foliage, rock shadows, horizons and reflections from their surroundings (especially pair1_X, pair2_X, pair3_Y and pair4_X). Second is repetitive surface activity that does not describe form: foliage grooves, rock ridges, grass hairs and twig hatching demand attention without clarifying the subject (pairs 1, 2 and 5 crops). Third is weak subdivision within large dark masses, especially the summer crowns and X’s reflected shore: darkness holds the silhouette but does too little to establish depth (pair1_X.jpg, pair1_Y.jpg and pair4_X.jpg).
