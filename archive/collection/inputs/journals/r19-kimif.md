- day 1, 09:00: Plan: an original twilight landscape in Friedrich's manner. Low horizon (~y 430), vast evening sky with the last glow low at left-center and a young crescent moon upper right. Three ranges of mountains dissolving into blue haze, a snow peak catching the last light; mist filling the valley; dark middle hills with a tiny distant church spire; a black foreground ridge with a gnarled stag-headed oak at left, two small firs at right, and two Rückenfiguren standing at the crest contemplating the moon. Canvas: fine linen 18x16, warm red-earth knife ground under a brushed lead-white top ground, per the Dresden findings. Method: pencil underdrawing, very thin layers, stipple and glaze for the sky gradations, crisp silhouette work for trees over set paint.
- day 1, 18:07: Sky base complete: gray-blue zenith, pearl middle, warm afterglow low at left-center, gray-violet stratus bands with faint warm rims. Learned: the glaze hand alone leaves open strokes; needs coverage 2+ with fill, then one blend. The glow went down too dense — had to drag it sideways into a band. Next: let the sky set overnight, then lay the mountain ranges from far to near in smalt/cobalt grays, keeping edges lost in haze.

- day 3, 16:29: day 2, late morning: Moon (crescent + earthshine + one star) is in; two comb spruces built at right (whorl skeleton, knitted needle mass, restated plates, faint rim); the two figures stand on the crest — man with hat and stick, hooded companion. Lesson: strokes dragged across setting pale mist lift it and smear; repair with pressed touches or clipped detail work at high coverage, never more strokes. Remaining: mute the too-yellow path, soften the speckled halo round the moon, knock back yellow flecks inside the firs, then final judgment.
- day 4, 16:29: day 4, midday: Finished. "Contemplating the Young Moon." This session: crescent moon + one star upper right; two comb spruces at right (whorl skeleton, knitted needle mass, restated plates, faint afterglow rim); the two figures on the crest — man with hat and stick, hooded companion, pressed dark against the mist; path muted to a worn trace; bottom corners sunk. Hard lessons: never patch a sky gradient with a rectangular mask (the pale slab) — repaint full-width tonal bands instead; never blend a band that crosses fresh dark silhouettes (the oak crown smeared; cost a day of repair); on tacky or setting paint, repair with pressed touches or clipped detail work, never dragged strokes; a glaze veil kills contrast it only meant to quiet. The smear halos around the crown were finally quieted by corridor-masked pearl stipple, and read now as dusk haze in the branches.
