**The Old Oak at Dusk**

The picture looks across a windless lagoon at the hour after sunset, from a dark bank in the foreground. A stag-headed old oak stands on the left, black against the light. It is the tallest thing in the picture, and its twigs thin out into fine, forked hairs.

The sky is painted in thin layers over a warm ground. It goes from a deep smalt-blue zenith through pale blue and lemon to apricot near the horizon. A few slender violet strata with gold undersides run toward the glow, and seven small birds and an evening star sit in it.

The still water repeats the sky, pale gold under the far shore and deepening to blue near the viewer. A ruined gable and four firs stand on a knoll across the water, with their reflection hanging beneath. To the left a low shore carries a village spire and poplars.

A single small figure with a staff, seen from behind, stands on the crest beside the oak. A broken row of fishing stakes runs out from the bank toward the ruin. The reeds and dry grass along the bank are drawn as dark silhouettes.

I left a few flaws in. The sky has a faint weave-dot speckle where blending lifted paint. There are pale flecks along the far shore's tree crowns. The trunk is a nearly flat dark mass with almost no bark detail, which suits the backlight.
