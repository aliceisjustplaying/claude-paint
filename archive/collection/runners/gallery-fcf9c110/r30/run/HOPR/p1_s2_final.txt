I've finished **Luncheonette, Four O'Clock**.

It's a red-brick corner building in low afternoon sun. The shadow of a building outside the picture cuts diagonally across the lower left of the front wall and the sidewalk. A counterman in a white coat stands in the dark doorway under the green LUNCHEONETTE sign. Above him and a little to the left, a woman rests her crossed forearms on the sill of the one open window, and neither knows the other is there. A lamppost, pale buildings farther off with a water tower, and a clear sky make the quiet right-hand side.

**What I did this sitting (painting clock day 198 to day 345):**
- **Sign:** I repainted the lettering. Each letter now has its own width and spacing, so the word reads evenly.
- **Shop windows:** I darkened the curtains from paper-white to cream, so the light looks like it's coming through glass. I added two coffee urns, a brass curtain rod and a cake stand under a glass dome, so the windows now read as a lunch room.
- **Figures:** I darkened a light sliver beside the counterman's arm. I painted the woman again with sloped shoulders, a sleeve and crossed forearms.
- **Windows above:** I closed the lower half of three windows so the pattern of open and shut panes is less regular.
- **Sidewalk:** I repainted the whole shaded part as a gradient, from deep at the wall to lighter toward the curb.
- **Sky:** I repainted the whole sky, which removed the faint rectangle behind the water tower.
- I signed it small in the street at the lower right.

**How I judged last time's open problems:**
- **Faint rectangle in the sky:** gone.
- **Uniform shaded sidewalk and street:** the sidewalk now has its gradient. I left the street strip as it is because it's a thin band.
- **Schematic shop windows:** improved by the objects. I've kept the underlying bands.
- **Lamppost shadow:** left as it is. The big shadow edge is cast by a roofline, not by an upright like the lamppost, so the two edges don't need to meet in perspective.
- **Flat, even surfaces:** I now accept these as the picture's manner.

What I'm still unsure of: the woman's forearms are a simple band. They read from a normal viewing distance but aren't really modelled.

**Practical lessons (also in the journal):**
- To fix a flat field, repaint the whole of it up to real edges, using a gradient between two or three piles if needed, and only over dry paint. This left no seams on the sky or the sidewalk, where the scumble had failed twice.
- When repainting around thin dark shapes, keep the paint about 0.8 units clear of them. Then go along any receding diagonal edge with a small brush, because the broad pass misses it.
- Pale paint over a dark needs the dark to be fully dry. Going the other way, one coat of the dark glass mix over dry paint is enough to trim a silhouette.
- Hand-lettering needs a width for each letter and adjusted gaps between them. Equal spacing looked ragged.
- In small details, earth and black mixes took about 10–20 days to dry. Fields rich in lead white took about 3–8 days (medium 0.08).

I'm happy with the picture. It feels quiet and sunlit, with two people alone at four o'clock.
