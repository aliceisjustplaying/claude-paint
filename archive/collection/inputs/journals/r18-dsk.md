- day 1, 09:01: Began a still life on a warm pale-lead ground: a dark bottle, a shallow bowl of lemons, a pear and a lemon on a table against a dim wall. Light from the upper left. Sketched the main shapes in pencil; a placed table edge at about y=455.
- day 1, 12:40: Ground, wall (dim warm grey-brown) and table (warm brown) laid in broadly and blended. First try at cast shadows came out as dark blobs; repainting the tabletop and will drag the shadows with a brush instead.
- day 3, 14:44: Learning the hard way: wet white under the bowl came up into every repaint of the tabletop, leaving a pale halo I could not cover. Letting the paint set for two days before painting over it.
- day 8, 18:25: Settling on a darker, warmer key: the wall and tabletop go deeper so the bottle, bowl and fruit carry the light. Repainting the wall in a dark umber-brown with a soft warm glow behind the bottle.
- day 11, 01:04: Resting the painting a few days so the highlights will sit on top of the dry paint instead of dissolving into it. The wet-in-wet passages read soft; the crisp accents need dry ground under them.
- day 32, 03:30: day 32: stepping back with fresh eyes. The whole reads as a still life now, but three faults: the table-edge line runs straight across the bottle like a seam; the pear is transparent and muddy, the table showing through it; and the wall has gone to vague smudges. Plan: repaint the bottle cleanly over the seam, repaint the pear as a solid body, unify the wall and table, then settle the last accents.
- day 43, 14:11: day 37-43: the finishing sessions. Rested the painting five days, then worked on dry ground: repainted the pear as a solid body (it had gone transparent where the table showed through), evened the wall (the broad strokes had banded), relaid the table and its cast shadows, and gave the bottle one clean vertical lay-in with a broad reflection and a crisp specular. Ended with the table edge drawn behind the bottle and small white speculars on the fruit. Resting it now so the last layers set.
- day 50, 15:48: day 50: finished. The dark bottle carries the light against the dim wall; the bowl of lemons and the pear hold the warm pool on the table, with the single lemon on the left closing the composition. Saved and closed the session.
- day 51, 08:24: day 51: returned and looked with fresh eyes. What I had called finished still read flat: the fruit were pale blobs under a regular stipple mesh, the bowl a white ring, the bottle a muddy silhouette, the table a field of blotches. Painted a second campaign. Repainted the fruit as opaque, rounded bodies (light from the upper left, a cored shadow, a warm bounce along the lower edge, small white speculars) and learnt that thin films of earth lighten over a pale base, so the shadows needed building in several full passes. Gave the white bowl an exterior shadow side and a deeper interior; repainted the bottle as clean glass with a soft left reflection and crisp speculars; unified the table, re-laid its cast shadows soft and short, deepened the wall and set a warm glow behind the bottle with a soft shadow on the wall. The picture is quieter and rounder now.
- day 61, 10:38: day 51, step back again: the fruit are the weak point. The lemons and pear read as pale cloudy blobs because the modelling was all soft wet gradients fused by blending; the forms are too light and too low in contrast. Also the bottle is a matte mass with accidental streaks, and my first attempt at a glass highlight over it (a broad low-coverage body pass) came out patchy and dashed along the rim. Plan a third campaign on dry ground: let everything set, then repaint the bottle dark and lay ONE clean soft window highlight with brush strokes, not a pass; rebuild the pear with a genuinely dark shadow; give the fruit a clear lit top. Wait for the paint to set first.
- day 93, 16:19: day 80-93, third campaign. Tired of the fruit reading as pale cloudy blobs, I found the cause: thin films of earth over a pale base lighten, and painting soft gradients on open paint fuses them to mush. The fix is dry ground and harder shapes: a mid body laid over the whole fruit, a shadow region with a curved terminator, a bright lit side, a warm reflected rim. Did that for the lemons and pear and they finally have volume. Repainted the bottle as clean dark glass: a broad soft window highlight laid wet into the fresh dark, a reflected warm edge on the right, a cool one on the left, a crisp specular. Evened the wall with two crossing broad passes (a single glaze-blend over dry ground just marbled it). Repainted the bowl's exterior with a light left and a shaded right, a warm bounce at the rim, and deepened the interior. Added warm reflected rims and small white speculars on all the fruit. The lemons still carry a hard dark crescent at the terminator and the lit passages are a little mottled; I softened the terminators with a mid ring. The picture is rounder and quieter now. Resting it before the last accents.
- day 112, 19:10: day 93-112, fourth campaign, on the fruit. Tried a nested-disc value ramp: it posterized into visible concentric rings, and blending the rings left wood-grain. Tried load_at to make a continuous light-to-shadow gradient: it came out a patchwork of blobs, because the load varies dip by dip. What works is a soft-edged mask with clip: lay the fruit in the mid shadow colour, then a soft (blurred) lit ellipse over the lit side. Clipped to a soft mask, the strokes fade out where the mask fades, so the lit side dissolves into the shadow with no seam, and a soft core band just outside the terminator, plus a warm reflected rim, finish the turn. Rebuilt all four fruit this way; they read round at last. Resting before the last speculars.
