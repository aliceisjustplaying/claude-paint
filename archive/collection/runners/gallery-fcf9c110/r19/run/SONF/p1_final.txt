**The Old Oak, Evening on the Lagoon**

A lone, stag-headed oak stands black on a dark bank at the left. Its fine twigs are drawn against a sky that grades from smalt blue at the top through pale lemon to apricot where the sun has just gone. A small cloaked figure stands on the crest beside the oak, seen from behind, looking out across a windless lagoon. The water carries the gold of the horizon and, lower down, a cool blue reflected from the upper sky. A row of fishing stakes recedes across it toward a far knoll where a ruined Gothic gable and four dark firs stand against the glow. Beyond them lie a low village shore with a spire and a pale, misty headland. Reeds break the bank line, seven birds cross the warm sky, and one evening star shows in the blue. Thin violet cloud strata with gold undersides converge on the horizon.

I painted nothing this session. I looked at the whole picture at three scales, in value and squint, mirrored, and in 1:1 tiles, and it holds together. The only faults left are a faintly scalloped hem at the trunk's foot and a few pale dashes above the far hill. I left them because my earlier repairs to that foot each made things worse. I called the painting complete and added a closing entry to the journal.
