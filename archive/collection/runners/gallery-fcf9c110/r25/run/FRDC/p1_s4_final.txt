I've finished **The Oak in Snow at Evening**.

One old oak stands on the central axis on top of a snow-covered rise, just after sunset. The sky runs in bands from cold smalt-cobalt overhead down to a warm Naples-yellow glow at the horizon. Beyond the snow there's only a band of forest in mist and a faint ridge.

**Changes this sitting (days 332–338)**
- **Darker branches:** I'd accepted the pale fine branches twice, believing their paths were never recorded. Last time I only searched the twig list (`SEG`). This time I looked through all the stored tables and found the paths and widths of the main branches in `GROUPS` and `CROWN`. Restating one branch first, in a new near-black mix, put it exactly on its old line without doubling it. So I restated:
  - the hand-drawn secondary limbs at half their recorded width;
  - every recorded branch path from its base until it thins to the finest twigs.

  The crown now has a dark framework that fades into the paler fine twigs, which suits the dusk.
- **Trunk:** one dark glaze removed a pale streak on the left side of the trunk, left by an old wipe.
- **Crow:** I added one small crow far off in the empty left sky, opposite the crescent moon.

**Flaws I'm keeping**
- **The pale specks in the fork:** they read as light through the branches.
- **The faint pale patch in the upper-left sky:** it passes for a thinning in the cloud.
- **The raised slab of paint in the snow** (only visible under raking light) **and a tiny violet fleck at the corner:** a frame would cover the fleck.

In each case a local patch would make things worse: paint dabbed over a dry, blended area dries paler and shows.

**New lessons for the handoff** (the earlier ones still apply)
- Before deciding a path wasn't recorded, check every stored table.
- Repainting a branch along its recorded path with a pointed brush, at the width it was drawn, puts the new stroke exactly on the old line.

My heart is happy with it. The oak finally has the dark structure it was missing, and the moon and the crow keep each other company across the sky.
