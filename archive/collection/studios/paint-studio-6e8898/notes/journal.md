- day 2, 06:10: Finished a first, and I think final, state of the self-portrait. Decisions made along the way:

  - No literal likeness to work from, so I built a face from what I know of
    head anatomy and portrait convention (Loomis thirds, terminator-line
    chiaroscuro) rather than inventing a fake specific person. Chose a bold,
    simplified, almost graphic light/dark split down the face instead of
    fully blended naturalism - it reads less like a photograph and more like
    a considered, constructed likeness, which felt honest for a painter with
    no mirror.
  - Warm umber/ochre ground, left bare in a few places as the flesh
    halftone - classic and it saved a lot of paint.
  - Big lesson of the session: unclipped broad/body strokes run well past
    their mask by their own stroke length - my first garment pass shot dark
    blue-grey strokes deep into the hair and background before I started
    using clip=true routinely. Cleaning that up (and a similarly
    overambitious background "glow" and a couple of cheek "stickers" from
    hard-edged ellipses used without softening first) cost a lot of chunks.
    Lesson banked: soften() before clip=true for any accent smaller than the
    broad masses, and clip=true almost always for anything near a hair/skin/
    background boundary.
  - Also learned that repeated wet-into-wet passes mostly remix rather than
    add hiding; a few times I had to wait() to let a layer set before a
    darkening or covering coat would actually hold.
  - Hair went through an accidental orange-copper phase before settling into
    a chestnut-auburn - kept it, it reads as a deliberate colour now, and
    gave the crown a soft warm sheen and a couple of flyaway strands and one
    loose lock over the near shoulder (which usefully also covers a patch of
    scar tissue from the garment mishap).
  - Background: dark warm-grey with a rough, textured, semi-abstract
    scumbled passage on the light side - unplanned (came from an
    overcorrection) but kept, it reads as atmosphere/movement on the side
    the gaze and light come from, and leaves the shadow side of the
    background plain and calm.

  Calling it done here. Title: "Half-Lit".
