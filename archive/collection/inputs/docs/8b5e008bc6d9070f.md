## BRIEF.md

# Paint a picture

Compose and paint one original landscape on a June day in the manner of Caspar
David Friedrich, at the easel of claude-paint, a simulator of oil paint on
linen. The subject and composition are yours. Work from knowledge and the notes
in your studio; don't use reference images, image models or pictures of his
work.

## Your studio
- The folder ~/src/a/paint-studio-cfa19c. Work only there, and in your scratch folder.
- You paint at the easel, in one session: open it, paint a chunk, look at
  the canvas, paint the next. The easel is `bin/easel` in your studio;
  notes/easel_guide.md has its commands (`open`, `do`, `look`, `note`, `save`,
  `close`).
- What is painted stays painted. There is no undo: paint over what you
  don't want.
- You mix your own paint on the palette, from the tubes, in the
  proportions you choose.
- Time passes on the painting's own clock: every mark takes the time a
  hand takes, and `wait(minutes)` lets it rest, as long as you like; paint
  dries as that time passes. Real time between commands doesn't count. The
  clock isn't a budget or a target.
- Scratch: run `~/.local/bin/agent-tmp studio-cfa19c`, then
  `export TMPDIR="<that folder>" TMP="<that folder>" TEMP="<that folder>"`;
  write temporary files only there.
- Wrap anything that might not finish in `timeout` (a process that hangs
  doesn't end on its own).

## The rules of the studio
- The easel is the only way to paint and to see the painting. Don't replay
  or copy the session yourself, open a second one, or edit or restore its
  files. (`bin/easel check`, which verifies the log against the canvas, is
  fine.)
- Look at the canvas with the easel's views. Don't read pixel values with
  other programs.
- Paint shapes and marks, not computed pictures: don't encode an image in
  masks, amounts or proportions.
- Shapes are drawn, not copied: don't make a mask or stroke by reflecting,
  flipping, rotating or translating another mask's or stroke's coordinates.
  A reflection in water is painted as its own shape, not as
  `mask:at(x, 2*h - y)`.

## What to read
notes/easel_guide.md; notes/studio_notes.md;
notes/research/friedrich_materials.md (his materials and method, sourced);
notes/research/trees.md (how trees are built) and
notes/research/oil_paint_physics.md as needed.

## Working
- You make every artistic decision.
- Friedrich's pictures are full of small, particular details; don't stop at broad
  passages.
- Look at your painting often, whole and close up.
- Keep a working journal with `bin/easel note "..."` as you go: your own
  working notes. Add to it; don't rewrite earlier entries.
- Develop the painting until you judge it complete. Then save it
  (`bin/easel save`) and close the session.

## When you're done
Reply with the paths of the saved painting and its log, the painting's
title if you give it one, and if you like a few sentences about the
picture.

- day 1, 09:00: Plan. A June morning on the flat meadows of a Pomeranian river, after the manner of Friedrich's Greifswald meadows, but my own arrangement. Canvas 70 x 50 cm, fine linen, warm red-ochre knife ground under a pale roller-applied lead-white top ground. Low horizon at about y=438: the sky takes most of the picture, deep cobalt/smalt at the top paling to a warm cream at the horizon, with a low band of summer cloud. On the horizon, right of centre, the silhouette of a Hanseatic town: a tall-spired hall church, two other towers, roofs; windmills far left and right. Middle ground: a silver river winding towards the town, pollard willows along it, haymakers and small hay cocks, horses grazing. Foreground: a slight grassy rise at left, a lone oak in full June leaf, and two small figures seen from behind looking out toward the town. Order: pencil underdrawing, thin underpainting, sky, distance, meadows, tree, then the small particulars last.
- day 1, 09:02: Mishap: chunk 2 was not my drawing. - day 2, 07:03: Day 1. Sky laid in five bands, deep cobalt-smalt at top down to a warm lead-white-ochre-vermilion cream at the horizon, blended with the badger while wet. A cumulus bank built from lit ellipsoids behind where the town will stand. It is too pale against the cream: the cloud barely separates from the sky. Plan: a second sky layer to cut the blue around the cloud tops, with deeper grey-violet in the shadows. Thin olive underpainting on the land. The broad strokes overran the horizon, so the distant land band has to cover that seam.
- day 3, 12:12: Day 3. Deeper second sky layer cut around the cloud: the top is now a good cobalt, paling to cream at about y=280. I remodelled the cloud as a grey-violet bank (smalt, red earth, umber, white) with its lit tops at upper left: against the luminous horizon it reads better as a darker mass than a white one. The far land is a blue-grey band, and the town silhouette is on it in one flat aerial tone. Problems: the town is the same value and hue as the cloud behind it, the little house gables read as sawteeth, and there's a stray lens-shaped smudge at the cloud's upper left. Waiting for it all to set before adding darker shadow sides and lit edges.
- day 4, 08:33: Day 4. The warm white rims on the billow tops read as snow on a mountain ridge. Blending them downward softened the cloud into a whitish, streaky mass, but the blender also dragged white across the tacky towers, which are now half lost. Lesson: clip blends away from anything else that is not dry. Once this is dry I will glaze thin grey-violet shadows back into the lower right of each billow and restate the town completely.
- day 5, 22:05: Day 5. Meadows laid in three bands, blue-green in the distance to a fuller ochre-Prussian green nearer, and the river put in as a pale sky reflection. The river reads as a road: its width is too even and it runs as one straight diagonal. Next session I'll push the banks into meanders with meadow paint, add a dark line of bank reflection along its upper edge, and put willows along it. The glazed cloud shadows went on as heavy worms, so I badger-blended them into a soft grey-violet mass. It needs its billow tops found again.
- day 8, 11:53: Day 8. Billow heads on the cloud from an ellipsoid form lit from the upper left. The silhouette now reads as a cumulus, but the lower row came out as cotton balls. A diagonal badger pass pulled them into the grey. It is streaky close up and reads acceptably whole; I'll calm it with a thin grey-violet veil later. Foreground rise laid: lit yellow-green crest band, mid green slope, dark umber-Prussian foot. Next is the oak on the rise: limbs first, then foliage clumps stippled dark, then half-tone, then light, leaving sky holes.
- day 10, 21:58: Day 10. Oak: bark armature from a skeleton, then 25 foliage clumps from rough ellipsoids: a dark Prussian-umber mass, a mid-green stipple on the lit side, small yellow-green touches in the lights. It read as broccoli, every ball lit alike, so I darkened the whole right side and the undersides with a stipple driven by one big crown ellipsoid. The crown now has a single light. - day 17, 13:33: Day 17. Oak limbs restated in dark bark with lit edges. River repainted with a gradation, pale cream at the far end to blue nearer, with soft willow reflections brushed down wet and badgered. Willow trunks greyed. Two post mills on the horizon. The two figures stand on the crest seen from behind: a man in a dark coat and cap, a woman in a wine-red dress with a pale shawl. They came out well.
- day 29, 11:07: Day 20-ish. Sky patches: the brushy flicks at the oak's rim were covered with sky, but the fresh lead-white mixes read far paler than the aged sky, and my first wet-on-wet corrections only mixed in more white. After drying, restating with much deeper mixes (white 4 : cobalt 1.4 : smalt 1.1) matched. They now read as faint wisps around the crown. Cloud: the rocky-strata look was too much, so I repainted the whole body as one form, a warm grey mid-tone with shadow to the lower right and cream on the upper-left heads, badgered wet. It is calm and soft now, perhaps a touch too pale. The towers were eroded at their edges, so the town is next, restated crisp with proper spires. The meadow was repainted under the figures and horses with a depth gradation, and cast shadows added.
- day 47, 20:01: Sky trouble. My patches around the crown never matched: fresh paint over light or dark underlayers reads differently each time. I tried to make them part of long horizontal wisps. The first pass came out as white cartoon clouds, and knocking them back wet with blue turned them into flat dark lenses with hard rims. Lesson: in a sky, don't correct locally; repaint the whole passage. Letting it dry, then relaying the upper sky from top to y~340 in graded bands across the full width, cut around the oak and the cumulus, and badgered while wet.
- day 68, 06:22: The sky repaint went badly wrong. Broad strokes and a 40-wide badger without clip ran straight across the masks, carving the oak's upper crown into separate balls and nearly erasing the cumulus. Recovery: I deepened the upper sky while wet with clipped strokes and a clipped blend, then rebuilt the cumulus on its form (clipped, with its old halo ring painted as the cloud's own soft rim). The town was restated twice because soft edges ate the spires; poly(pts, true) SMOOTHS the corners, so the new town is drawn with plain polygons: a hall church with a needle spire, a smaller spired church, a capped tower, gabled houses. The upper crown was repainted in the original sequence (dark mass, fringe, half-tone, lights, crown shadow), and limbs now show through the gaps. Rules from now on: clip=true on every blend near anything else, and no edge='soft' next to thin shapes.
