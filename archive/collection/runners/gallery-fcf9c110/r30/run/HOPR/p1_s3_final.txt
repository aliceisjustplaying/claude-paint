I've finished **Luncheonette, Four O'Clock**.

It's a red-brick corner building in low four-o'clock sun. The shadow of a building outside the picture cuts diagonally across the lower left of the front wall and the sidewalk. A counterman in a white coat stands in the dark doorway under the green LUNCHEONETTE sign. Above him and a little to the left, a woman rests her folded arms on the sill of the one open window, and neither knows the other is there. A lamppost, pale buildings farther off with a water tower, and a clear sky make the quiet right side. Seen in grey values and squinting, the picture still works: the white coat in the dark doorway is where the eye goes first.

This short sitting (painting clock day 345 to 377) was small repairs:
- **The counterman's hem:** grey smudges had spread where his trousers meet the coat. I repainted the bottom edge of the coat with the original paint mixes. The sunlit half now matches the rest of the coat. The shaded half came out a little lighter than the grey above it, and I've left it because it looks like a turned-up hem.
- **The woman's forearms:** this was the one thing left open last time. I added a single thin line of shadow where one forearm crosses the other, which shows the crossing and separates her arms from her blouse. She now clearly reads as leaning on the sill. She's very small, so more modelling would make her look fussy.
- **Left as they are:**
  - A few tiny pale specks in the dark shopfronts of the distant buildings, which you can't see at viewing distance.
  - The shaded brick, which is only a little darker than the sunlit brick and separates from it mainly by colour. The shadow on the sidewalk and the lit shop windows carry that shadow instead, and repainting the brick around all the window frames and the sign would risk more than it would gain.

**Practical lessons (also in the journal):**
- Small touch-ups over dry paint blend in with one coat if I use the exact original mix and stop at a real edge. A mix that's only close shows as a slight step in lightness.
- The earlier lessons still hold:
  - To fix a flat area, repaint the whole of it up to real edges.
  - Pale paint goes over a dark only when the dark is fully dry.
  - Thin broken layers of paint look like spots on these flat surfaces.
  - Hand-lettering needs a width for each letter and adjusted gaps between them.
  - The drying times are as noted in the journal.

I'm happy with it. It's a quiet, sunlit corner with two people alone at four o'clock.
