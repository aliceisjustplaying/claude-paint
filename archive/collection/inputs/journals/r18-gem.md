- day 1, 09:00: Setting up the easel. Planning a still life on linen: a glazed earthenware pitcher, a ripe lemon with a cut half showing translucent pulp, a folded linen napkin, and a walnut, arranged on an aged oak table against a quiet chiaroscuro background.
- day 1, 09:00: Prepared linen canvas (500mm wide, 16x14 plain weave). Applied a two-layer ground: a leveling lead white and raw umber base, followed by a warm ochre imprimatura brushed across the weave.
- day 1, 09:02: Laid in the drawing with natural black chalk and HB pencil on the toned ground: established the horizon of the table, the stance and proportions of the earthenware jug, the placement of the whole lemon and cut half, the drape of the linen napkin on the left, and the walnut on the right.
- day 1, 09:29: Blocking in the oak table: laying horizontal strokes along the grain of the wood. Reserving the silhouettes of the lemon and white napkin so the ground remains luminous for their brilliant lights, while allowing the shadows to fuse wet-into-wet.
- day 3, 11:46: Allowed the underpainting of the background wall, table surface, and cast shadows to dry fully over two days. The paint is now touch-dry, ready for building the glazed earthenware jug, the lemon forms, and the fine linen napkin without dragging or muddying into the underlayers.
- day 3, 12:41: Modeled the glazed earthenware pitcher: laid in rich red earth and ochre body tone, sculpted the core shadow on the right with raw umber and bone black, glazed warm reflected light along the right contour, and struck a crisp impasto highlight in lead white on the left shoulder and rim. Anchored the vessel with a deep contact shadow on the tabletop.
- day 3, 13:14: Beginning the lemons: mixing a rich palette of chrome yellow, yellow ochre, green earth, raw umber, and lead white. Sculpting the whole lemon with dimpled peel texture and the cut half with glistening translucent pulp segments and pale pith.
- day 3, 13:56: Painting the draped linen napkin on the left and the walnut on the right. Mixing lead white with touches of raw umber, bone black, and cobalt blue for the cool, sculptural shadows of the linen folds, and warm impasto lead white for the sunlit crests. The walnut is modeled with earthy umber, ochre, and rugged shell texture.
- day 3, 14:38: Resolving the background wall into a unified, deep chiaroscuro space. Moving away from the active diagonal texture toward a quiet, velvety olive-umber atmospheric tone that lets the luminous foreground objects hold full stage.
- day 3, 15:24: Refining the tabletop and linen napkin: carving clean, tailored contours around the cloth, establishing crisp sculptural folds with warm cream highlights and cool shadow troughs, unifying the oak tabletop along its horizontal grain, and striking the illuminated chamfer along the table's front edge.
- day 5, 16:51: Allowed the unified table planes and glazes to dry fully over two days. The canvas is now touch-dry across all passages. Now, with dry underlayers beneath, we can build the definitive foreground forms with rich body color, crisp impasto lights, and exquisite textural details: the succulent cut lemon with radiating segments and spiraling peel, the crisp linen folds, the rugged walnut, and the specular ceramic glazes on the earthenware jug.
- day 5, 18:02: Bringing the earthenware jug to full sculptural power: laying in rich saturated terracotta and amber-olive glazes, deepening the core shadow with raw umber and bone black, and striking brilliant impasto specular highlights along the turned ceramic shoulder and rim.
- day 5, 19:56: Fusing and refining the forms: blending the turned ceramic body of the earthenware jug into a seamless sculptural curvature, connecting the spiraling lemon peel directly to the succulent cut fruit, and tailoring the folded linen napkin with crisp, starched edges and luminous drapery planes.
- day 5, 20:19: Adding a botanical lemon leaf with its woody stem between the lemon and the folded napkin: mixing green earth, yellow ochre, raw umber, and a touch of Prussian blue. Striking final crisp specular accents on the ceramic rim and table edge to complete the composition.
- day 15, 20:22: Final review of the completed canvas. Stepping back from the easel: the quiet chiaroscuro atmosphere envelops the scene with serene dignity. The luminous textures of the cut lemon pulp and curling rind, the crisp folds of the draped linen napkin, the fresh botanical leaf, the rugged walnut, and the deep terracotta resonance of the earthenware pitcher achieve a harmonious balance of form, light, and tactile presence. The painting is complete.
