**Still Life with Clay Pitcher, Quince, and Knife**

This picture explores the quiet, contemplative gravity, rigorous formal geometry, and intense tactile presence of a classic Spanish *bodegón* in the lineage of Juan Sánchez Cotán, Francisco de Zurbarán, and Luis Meléndez. 

Lit by a single source of raking north light from the upper left, the composition is anchored on the left by the grounded vertical volume of a rustic earthenware pitcher (*cántaro*), its swelling terracotta belly turned on the potter's wheel, modeled from warm red earth, yellow ochre, and raw umber, crowned with a flared lip, a sturdy pulled loop handle, and a luscious impasto catchlight skimming the glaze of its shoulder. To the right, resting firmly on the luminous limestone table slab, a ripe quince (*membrillo*) glows in warm cadmium and yellow ochre, turning through a knobby organic form into a deep amber-umber core shadow and soft table bounce, topped by an arching woody stem and grounded by an occlusion contact shadow. In the right foreground, an old forged steel knife lies diagonally across the limestone plane, its sharpened cutting edge and brass bolster catching brilliant specular glints, while its turned-wood handle projects forward over the front stone ledge into the viewer's space, casting a dramatic vertical shadow down the shaded face of the masonry block to bridge the picture plane into our room. The silent, deep chiaroscuro background unites all forms in atmosphere, light, and timeless repose.
