# Paint a landscape

Compose and paint one original landscape in the manner of Caspar David
Friedrich with claude-paint, a physical oil-paint simulator in which a
painting is a Rust program. The subject, season, hour and composition are
yours to choose. Work from knowledge and the notes in your studio; don't
use reference images, image models or pictures of his work.

## Your studio
- The folder ~/src/a/paint-r15-p1. Work only there.
- Your painting: paintings/src/bin/r15_p1.rs. Everything renders at
  2400 px wide:
  - the whole painting: `cargo paint r15_p1 -- --full --width 2400`
    (-> out/r15_p1_full.png)
  - a window at that resolution:
    `cargo paint r15_p1 -- --full --width 2400 --crop x0,y0,x1,y1`
  - save a checkpoint after every stage: add `--ckpt`; start again from a
    stage: add `--resume <stage>` (with the same `--full --width 2400`,
    and the same `--crop` when cropping).
  README.md has the rest.
- The engine (crates/paint) stays as it is. Record any limitation of the
  tools you meet and the workaround you use.
- Scratch: run `~/.local/bin/agent-tmp r15_p1` once and export
  TMPDIR=TMP=TEMP to it; write temporary files only there.
- If linking fails with "You have not agreed to the Xcode license
  agreements", set DEVELOPER_DIR=/Library/Developer/CommandLineTools for
  cargo.
- View PNGs via `scripts/peek SRC OUT.jpg [H W Y X]` (renders are large).
- Use whole renders to judge the picture and crops to inspect details;
  checkpoints save repeating unchanged stages. A `timeout` around a render
  is a safeguard against a hung process.

## What to read
README.md; notes/guide.md (the engine, as a painter uses it);
notes/research/friedrich_materials.md (his materials and method, sourced);
notes/research/oil_paint_physics.md as needed; the engine's module docs in
crates/paint/src.

## Principles
- The engine provides physics and tools (brushes, paint, canvas, optics,
  masks). You make every artistic decision.
- Presets like Style::broad() are a place to start; choose stroke
  direction, curvature, order, stippling, glazing and every color like a
  painter would.
- Step back and look at your painting often, whole and in crops, the way a
  painter does at the easel.
- Develop the painting until you judge it complete.

## What to leave (keep it saved as you work)
1. paintings/src/bin/r15_p1.rs and the final render out/r15_p1_full.png.
2. notes/r15_p1.md: the composition and your artistic reasons; your
   working method stage by stage; the tool limitations you met and your
   workarounds; your critique of the result.
3. notes/craft_r15_p1.md, a materials-and-tools record: for each
   observation, the operation, the effect you saw in the paint and your
   explanation or uncertainty, described in terms of paint, brush, surface,
   geometry and computation.
4. Final reply: the render paths and the tool limitations you met.
