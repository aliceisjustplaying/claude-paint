# r15_p2: Oak in the snow at dusk

Program: `paintings/src/bin/r15_p2.rs`. Render: `out/r15_p2_full.png`
(`cargo paint r15_p2 -- --full --width 2400`, 2400 × 1714 px, about 150 s
from scratch). Canvas: `Style::friedrich()` (440 mm linen, 15 × 13
threads/cm, two knifed earth grounds), with the top ground changed to a
patchy warm whitish brushed layer (#dccfb6, 55 µm, stiff 0.4). Palette
`friedrich_1820` (lead white, pale smalt, cobalt, yellow ochre, chrome
yellow, red earth, vermilion, raw umber, bone black). Aspect 1.4.

## Composition and reasons

A winter evening just after sunset. A single old oak stands on a snowy
rise at the left: short massive trunk, gnarled limbs with sharp elbows,
broken stubs, and a stag-headed dead leader. The rise falls away to the
right into a wide snow plain. On the horizon lies a low violet-grey band of
woods, with a Gothic church (tower, steep spire, high nave, lower choir)
set against the brightest part of the afterglow. A trodden path meanders
from the lower edge to a lone walker in a greatcoat and hat, going toward
the church. An old fence line of leaning posts, capped with snow, runs
away across the plain on the right. Four crows sit in the oak and two more
fly toward the church. The evening star hangs over the glow.

- **Why these motifs.** The dead oak, the church in the distance, the
  figure seen from behind, dusk and snow are Friedrich's own vocabulary. I
  put them in my own arrangement: no dolmen and no ruin. The tree is not
  in the middle but a little left of centre. The path is a single walker's
  track, not a road.
- **Structure.** The horizon sits low, 73 % down, so the sky takes most of
  the picture, as in his winter and moon pictures. Two diagonals cross.
  The rise falls from the upper left to the right; the path climbs from
  the lower centre to the church at the right. The fence repeats the
  second diagonal and gives the plain its depth by perspective spacing.
  The oak's spread (about 60 % of the width) makes a dark lace against the
  lightest part of the sky, and the church is the only other dark vertical.
- **Light.** The sun has set just behind the woods, a little right of the
  church. Nothing casts a shadow, so everything is modelled by the sky. The
  zenith is a cool grey-blue and the horizon apricot. The crest of the
  rise catches the whole sky and is lightest. The slope facing the viewer
  is in blue shade. The far snow gives back the glow and is warm. The
  near snow sinks into violet. The oak is a contre-jour silhouette with
  only a cool sky-lit rim on the upper left of its wood.
- **Meaning, lightly.** An old dead tree and a living walker are joined by
  the path to the church under the evening star. I kept it quiet: small
  figure, no dramatic light.

## Working method, stage by stage

1. **Ground** (`st.prepare`, 36 s). Two knifed earth layers, then a
   brushed whitish top ground whose bristle striations stay.
2. **drawing.** HB and 2H `hand_line`s: the rise's crest, the woods and the
   far snow line (ruled), the church (ruled), the trunk's sides and the
   path. The drawing was fixed with `fix_drawing`.
3. **sky** (72 s).
   - Broad filbert, lean (medium 0.3), coverage 4, strokes 160–340 units
     nearly level, swept top to bottom, aimed at a gradient field. The
     field is grey-blue at the top, neutral in the middle, cream then
     apricot at the horizon, warmer around the glow (x = 720) and cooler
     to the left.
   - Six stratus bands laid wet into the wet sky: filbert 6, clipped to
     soft band masks, a lavender-grey top and a rose-gold underside.
   - Stipple into the wet sky (cluster 0, dips every 8, coverage 1.2).
   - Then two crossing level badger passes.
4. **glow.** A transparent #eaa66a glaze, up to 0.32 coat, deepest near
   the horizon and centred on the glow.
5. **distance.**
   - Woods: short upright touches of a round 3.2 into a band mask. The
     band has a noisy top edge (copses), stays off the rise, and ends on
     an uneven far snow line.
   - Church: the sable, clipped.
   - Mist stippled into the wet woods near their foot, then a light
     badger pass.
6. **snow** (23 s).
   - Broad lean filbert, clipped, following the rise's slope on the left
     and level on the plain, swept top to bottom. The colour field is a
     gradient: warm far snow, blue-lavender near.
   - The crest restated with a slightly lighter body stroke along the
     slope.
   - Two level badger passes.
7. **path** (after `dry`). Three clipped passes of a small round along
   the path: a slightly darker floor, a faint lighter left wall (facing
   the glow) and a bluer right wall. Then footprints: short alternating
   touches whose spacing and size shrink with distance.
8. **oak.**
   - The canvas is dried first.
   - Limbs of 2.6 units or more: filbert drags along each limb, 1–9 side
     by side across it, clipped to the union mask of all limbs.
   - A unifying pass of dark bark along the wood's grain over the whole
     limb mask.
9. **twigs.** Every branch under 2.6 units is one stroke of a pointed
   round (point 0.7). Pressure goes from the branch's root width down to
   0.7 × its tip width, with a long release, so each twig tapers to a
   point. There are 4,819 of them, and the pass takes 1.2 s.
10. **bark.**
    - Fissures: thin darker strokes along the grain.
    - Ridges: fewer lighter broken ones.
    - A cool grey sky-lit rim on the upper-left edge of every limb,
      stroked along the grain and clipped to a rim mask.
    - A small knot-hole low on the trunk.
11. **snow on the oak.** Where a limb (not the trunk) runs within ±38° of
    level for at least three points, a pointed round lays a thin line of
    bluish snow along its upper side. A quarter of these runs are
    skipped.
12. **foot.** A drift banked round the trunk's foot, higher on the left:
    two opaque passes of lean body colour, clipped, and no blender.
13. **posts.** Thirteen fence posts spaced evenly on the ground, which is
    uneven on the canvas: 1/(y − horizon) steps evenly. Some are missing
    in the distance. Each post gets a dark stroke, a cool left edge, a
    snow cap and banked snow at its foot.
14. **grass.**
    - 167 tufts in patches: on the rise, along the path and the fence,
      and six big clumps at the lower edge.
    - Each tuft has a small blue hollow in the snow and 4–22 fine pointed
      blades that arch or break over.
    - Some tufts have a seed stalk with a dark head.
15. **figure.**
    - The walker: sable, clipped to a polygon silhouette, with a faint
      cool rim on the left edge.
    - Crows: four perched on level limbs and two flying as an "M".
16. **veil.**
    - The evening star: a semi-opaque halo glaze and one touch of the
      sable.
    - A transparent blue-violet glaze deepening the near snow (up to 0.22
      coat).
    - A thin warm-grey glaze growing toward the corners, after Carus's
      note of Friedrich's advice (up to 0.22 coat).
17. **finish.** Varnish 0.22 ± 0.06 coat. Faint cracks: 4 µm wide, dirt
    0.12, veil 0.15, hierarchy 0.5, patchy 0.6.

### How the picture got there (the main corrections)

- **Clouds.** First render: the thin stratus bands were dotted dashes, like
  Morse code. A 6-wide filbert at light pressure only touched the weave's
  crowns. Wider bands, more pressure and load, and stippling and blending
  after them made them soft.
- **Sky lace.** Stippling after the badger left pale lace. Stippling
  before the badger fused the touches into faint cirrus wisps, which I
  kept.
- **Dark band under the rise.** The woods mask ran across the whole width,
  so the woods were painted under the rise, and the lean snow did not
  hide them. Fix: mask them off the rise.
- **Snow over the woods.** Unclipped snow strokes ran up over the woods
  (by design, `hug`). Fix: `clip(true)` on every snow pass.
- **Pale oak.** The first oak was painted into the still-wet snow and
  lifted it, so the trunk came out pale. Fix: `c.dry()` before the tree.
- **Snow that read as fog.** Three tries:
  1. A drift height field in perspective, plus a strong badger: a sea of
     cloud.
  2. Crisp drift ridges: ruled lines, like ripples on a lake.
  3. Final: a smooth laid gradient, two badger passes, no stipple.
     Vegetation, the path, posts and the drift at the tree's foot make it
     ground.
- **Tree geometry.**
  - First: too small, curly twigs, a dead leader.
  - Then: loops in the heavy limbs.
  - Then: a generic deciduous tree.
  - Oak elbows (a sharp kink answered a step later), heavy wood that
    remembers its direction, and a stag-headed leader made it an oak. I
    chose seed 13 of eight candidates, previewed as SVG line drawings.
- **Limb seams.** Gaps in the limbs: `Shape::ribbon` through a sharp elbow
  twists and its fill cancels. Pale streaks at junctions came from
  lit-side strokes crossing other limbs and from crossing strokes lifting
  each other's wet paint. Fixes: per-segment quads plus joint discs, a
  separate rim mask, and the unifying pass.
- **Trunk foot.** The trunk first ended in a flat cut with a smeared
  "reflection" below it: the badger dragged dark bark into the drift. The
  ribbon's round end cap also showed as a grey ball once the unifying
  pass painted it. Fixes: shorten the trunk, cut the limb mask at the
  snow line, and paint the drift opaque without a blender.
- **Grass.** First it was an even confetti of orange "V" sprouts. Then
  fewer, clustered, greyer tufts with fine blades, seed heads and a blue
  hollow at each base.

## Tool limitations met, and workarounds

1. **Top-level helpers make every checkpoint stale.** Functions above
   `main` (and edits to `use` lines) count for every stage. Workarounds:
   - The tree generator sits between `// ckpt: from oak` and
     `// ckpt: end`.
   - I adopted a checkpoint with `--stale-ok` only when the change could
     not affect it (an added import).
   - Geometry that the drawing stage uses (the path) forced full reruns;
     I took them (about 150 s each).
2. **`Shape::ribbon` self-intersects at sharp turns,** so its fill cancels
   and leaves holes in a limb. Workaround: one ribbon part per segment
   plus a disc at every joint (`Shape::add` parts never cancel).
3. **A clip mask can't tell one limb from another.** Offset strokes of a
   thin limb painted lit colour across the thick limb it joins.
   Workaround: paint all wood dark, then the lit edge on a derived rim
   mask (inside the limbs, outside them 2 units up-left).
4. **No per-pixel direction field for handlings from geometry.** I built
   my own 2-unit grid holding the direction of the thickest limb over
   each cell, and passed it to `angle`.
5. **`c.drag` strokes have no look-and-fill.** Crossing strokes into wet
   paint thinned it and left sky showing at the junctions. Workaround: a
   unifying `c.work` pass over the whole limb mask.
6. **Stroke-scale unevenness in a smooth lean passage.** Light blotches at
   the loaded starts of broad strokes stayed in the snow. Two badger
   passes reduce them but don't remove them. Stipple in the snow made it
   worse (pale lace), so I dropped it there.
7. **Round end caps of ribbons** extend half a width past the end point.
   Workaround: multiply the limb mask by a cut-off at the snow line.
8. **Previewing geometry costs a render.** Workaround: an `OAK_SVG`
   environment variable writes the tree as an SVG and exits, and
   `OAK_SEED` picks a candidate. `rsvg-convert` rasterises it; ImageMagick
   could not read that SVG. Both are left in the program; neither is set
   for the final render.
9. **`magick montage` failed** (no fontconfig). I used `+append`/`-append`
   instead.

## Critique

What works:
- At a distance the picture holds: a strong dark oak against a luminous
  gradient, and a clear reading of depth. The lace of the crown against
  the glow is the best passage, and its twigs taper convincingly.
- The rise's silhouette, the woods and church in haze, the diminishing
  fence posts and path, the tiny walker, the crows and the star all
  read.
- The palette is restrained (earths, cobalt, lead white) and the colour
  harmony is close to his dusk pictures: cool zenith, apricot horizon,
  violet snow.

What doesn't:
- **The snow.** It is the weakest part: smooth, almost airbrushed, with
  faint stroke blotches. It has none of the fine, pooled, stippled
  surface of his snow, and the modelling is mostly a gradient.
- **The oak.**
  - Generated, it is a little too regular in its fineness: every twig is
    equally crisp and spiky, with no aerial softening in the far parts of
    the crown and no clusters of dead twig ends.
  - The heavy limbs still look slightly tubular.
  - The drift at its foot is a bit cottony, with a light top edge wider
    than the trunk.
- **The sky.** The cirrus mottling in the upper sky is livelier than
  Friedrich usually allows. It is beautiful at 1:1, but a stiller sky
  would be more his.
- **The figure** is a flat silhouette. At 30 units that is honest to his
  practice, but it reads a little like a cut-out.
- **Balance.** The right half above the horizon is empty except for the
  two crows and the star. I meant that emptiness; it is right for him,
  but the picture's weight sits left.
