# Charcoal: vine (willow) and compressed

Research notes for the easel's `charcoal()` (paint's `charcoal.rs`, and the carbon beds in `dry.rs`). Compiled 2026-11-15 for gap 11 of the Cassatt support plan (notes/cassatt_support.md on branch `notes/cassatt-materials`). Cassatt's preparatory drawings for her prints are "compressed charcoal and graphite over some charcoal lines" (Met, *Maternal Caress* drawing; cassatt_materials.md §4).

**Tags:** **[M]** measured, cited. **[P]** practitioner or museum description, cited, no number. **[D]** derived here from measured inputs (the arithmetic is in `charcoal_optics.py`, this folder: `uv run notes/research/charcoal_optics.py`). **[E]** estimate, with the reason no measurement or physics sets it.

**Bottom line**
- Both kinds are carbon in the tooth, held by nothing but the paper's texture: vine has no binder, and compressed charcoal's gum is glassy at room humidity, so it bridges nothing to the sheet. In the tooth charcoal behaves like a black pastel: loose particles that the finger, the eraser, a puff and a dry brush move, and that fixative binds. So the easel models it with the pastel stick (`pastel.rs`) and gives the stick one new dial, how readily the eraser takes what it lays (`lift`).
- **Color [D].** Carbon absorbs within 0.1 µm, so all a bed of charcoal returns is what its facets reflect (about 9 % against air). Mie in air, from the measured complex index of cellulose char (Jäger et al. 1998), gives vine R∞ 0.021, neutral, whatever the particles' size above 1 µm. That reflection is the bed's first surface, so no 4 % veil goes over it: vine is seen at L* 16, not 29.5 (estimates-audit-2 C1). Compressed charcoal is half fine compact (lamp black packed with gum, pores finer than light: an effective medium, R∞ 0.010) and half ground-charcoal splinters (as vine's), mixed by Kubelka–Munk: R∞ 0.016.
- **Fixative [D].** The resin wets a share of the pores and halves what the facets reflect there, but carbon's K/S changes only ×0.96–1.5 (a pigment of n ≈ 2's ×5). The share wetted is the light spray's resin (1.5 µm, from Krylon's SDS and its coverage) over the pores of the loose charcoal under it: a thin line is soaked, 20 µm of it 0.14. Fully wetted, vine goes L* 16.1 → 13.6. What makes a fixed drawing look deeper is also the wetting of a bloom of loose fine dust (greyer, R∞ 0.03) and of the paper between the marks, neither of which the engine models.
- **Lift [D].** A kneaded eraser, soft enough to wrap a particle, out-pulls the paper's van der Waals hold ×11 on a 3 µm vine splinter. Char fired at 500 °C and cellulose are hydrophilic, and at 50 % RH water condenses at their contact: a bridge at a 0.1 µm asperity adds 90 nN, and the splinter is taken 0.85. A particle below the paper's asperity scale (R ≲ 0.3 µm) holds as a smooth sphere, ×1.3 harder than the eraser pulls, so dry it is taken 0.42 of the time, and with the same water bridge 0.10. Compressed, half fines and half crumbs, with the water on both as on vine: 0.48. Pastel's 0.55 doesn't follow from this; that is an engine-wide question.
- **Grip [D]: none.** The dial is gone: gum arabic's Tg is 46 °C at 50 % RH, 21 K above a warm room, and a dry binder doesn't flow at the stick's contact pressure. Grip would return only in a room above about 73 % RH.
- **Deposit [M/D].** Vine's hardness is wood charcoal's, 3 MPa. The stick is 80 % pores, so a worn volume lays a fifth of itself in 3 µm splinters, which cover as 14 µm (pastel's deposit covers as 6). Compressed's grains cover as 3.3 µm.
- **Muddying [D].** The engine mixes the loose layer's reflectances by volume. Kubelka–Munk mixing (K and S by volume) makes pastel over loose charcoal 1.5–2.5× muddier at a few percent of charcoal. The engine's 0.2370 vs 0.2351 understates it; see §6.

## 1. What it is

- **Vine and willow charcoal** [P]. These are twigs of willow or vine "heated at a high temperature in an enclosed vessel without oxygen"; the stick has no binding agent, its "minute, splinter-like particles" crumble onto the support, "the medium is easily manipulated", it "requires paper with some texture" to hold, and "to prevent smudging many artists apply a fixative" (Met, Drawing: Charcoal). "The sticks don't contain any binder, so they will be dusty and brittle in texture" (Fine Art Tutorials). Vine "comes in shades of gray"; willow is "darker in color than vine charcoal" (Wikipedia, Charcoal (art)). The easel treats willow as vine (`kind="willow"` is accepted as vine).
- **Compressed charcoal** [P]. "Held together by a gum or wax binder"; "intensity of the shade is determined by hardness": softer grades have less binder and are blacker (Wikipedia; Fine Art Tutorials). One historical recipe is burnt birch, clay, lamp black and a little ultramarine (Wikipedia), so it can be bluish ("blue-black" PITT, Fine Art Tutorials). It is "usually harder in texture than willow and vine charcoal" (Fine Art Tutorials).
- **Stick sizes** [M, retail]. Vine and willow come as "long, thin sticks" in several thicknesses, up to "tree sticks"; compressed as sticks and blocks (Fine Art Tutorials). Winsor & Newton Artists' Willow is 14 cm long, Ø 5, 8 and 10 mm (thin, medium, thick: Artlounge's listing; W&N's own pages give no sizes). General's compressed is 3″ × ¼″ × ¼″ (76 × 6.35 × 6.35 mm; Blick, Utrecht; its jumbo stick is 3 × ½ × ¼ in). Easel defaults: vine round Ø8 mm and 140 mm long (2–16 mm allowed); compressed square 6.35 mm and 76 mm long (3–15 mm).

## 2. Optics

- **Charcoal powder reflects under 5 % in the visible**, flat, rising into the near infrared [M] (cometary-dust analogue study, arXiv 2110.05832: "low reflectivity, which remains below 5% in the visible domain"). Charcoal reflectance under oil (%Ro, 0.06–4 %) is a different, specular quantity from polished sections, and isn't used.
- **Carbon's index [M].** Cellulose pyrolysed at 400, 600, 800 and 1000 °C, complex index from reflectance spectra (Jäger, Mutschke & Henning 1998, *A&A* 332:291; tables from the Jena OCDB, `data/carbon/cel400.lnk` etc.). At 550 nm: cel400 1.52 + 0.47i, cel600 1.22 + 0.78i. Their densities (1.44, 1.67 g/cm³) are char's own, so the index already holds its sub-micrometre pores. Charcoal is fired to "around 500 °C" (FAO 1987, *Simple technologies for charcoal making*, ch. 4; 450–500 °C balances friability against fixed carbon). The script takes the mean of cel400 and cel600: 1.43 + 0.61i (610 nm), 1.35 + 0.63i (550), 1.21 + 0.63i (460). Lamp black (soot): 1.95 + 0.79i (Bond & Bergstrom 2006, *Aerosol Sci. Tech.* 40:27).
- **Vine's R∞ [D].** The dry-pigment recipe (dry_pigment_optics.md §2.1, its Mie code): lognormal sizes, transport scattering, K = 2σa, S = 0.75σs(1−g), R∞ by Kubelka–Munk, per channel. Carbon's absorption length (λ/4πk ≈ 0.07 µm) is far below any splinter, so each particle is opaque and returns only its facet reflection: (n−1)²+k² over (n+1)²+k², 0.087 at normal incidence against air. R∞ is therefore almost independent of size: 0.022 at 1 µm, **0.021 at 3 µm**, 0.022 at 20 µm. The splinters are taken as charred cell wall, median 3 µm [E: willow fibre walls are a few µm thick; the result barely depends on it]. Fired at 400 °C instead, 0.018; at 600 °C, 0.028 (more graphitic, more reflective).
- **No veil over it [D].** The engine shows a matte surface with a 4 % first-surface veil (`canvas::haze`): the share of light an oil film's smooth surface reflects, scattered back by a matte one. A bed of particles has no such surface. Mie's scattering of each opaque particle already holds its facets' reflection, and KM's R∞ is everything the bed returns, so a veil over it counts the facets twice (it would put vine at Y 0.060, L* 29.5, above the measured powder's 5 %). The engine leaves the veil off charcoal, loose or fixed, wherever it lies open (`Cell::bare_carbon`, `Canvas::unveiled`): vine is seen at Y 0.021, L* 16.1, compressed at L* 12.9. A light workable spray leaves no film (below), so fixed charcoal is a bed too. Paint or wet paint over it brings its own surface back.
  - *The same double count is in every pastel*: chalk and pigment beds are particle beds too. The difference is about 4 % of the light, ΔL* under 2 for a pale pastel but large for the darks. That is engine-wide and is left to its own change; here the veil comes off charcoal only, which needs the cell to know its carbon (`Cell::carbon`, by volume, loose and bound; checkpoint layout 5).
- **Compressed's R∞ [D, half of it E].** Charcoal powder and lamp black pressed with a binder ("burned birch, clay, lamp black", Wikipedia). Gum binder 5 % of the solids by volume [M range: organic binders 2–8 % by weight in pressed charcoal, US 5,221,290; 2.5 % CMC in a black chalk, US 2021/0292589; gum and char densities are alike]. Solids 60 % of the compact [E: a pressed powder compact is 50–70 %; no measurement of a charcoal stick found].
  - *Where the carbon is soot* (tens of nm) the compact's pores are finer than light, so a crumb is an effective medium (Bruggeman) of carbon, gum (n 1.52, dry_pigment_optics.md §1) and air: 1.22 + 0.34i at 550 nm. Its facets reflect less: R∞ 0.010. Sensitivity: solids 50–70 % gives 0.008–0.012; binder 2–10 %, 0.010–0.0097; the soot's own index in it, 0.013–0.017.
  - *Where it is ground charcoal* of 1–10 µm, the crumbs are a bed of opaque splinters, as vine's: 0.021.
  - *The stick's soot share is unmeasured* (the General's, cr630 and ESPO SDSs give only "carbon"). SEM puts carbon-black particles about 7× smaller than charcoal's. Half of each, mixed by Kubelka–Munk (K and S by volume): R∞ = **0.016**, L* 12.9 (estimates-audit-2 C5, bounds 0.010–0.021). The blue tint ("blue-black" PITT) isn't in the physics: carbon is neutral, and the ultramarine some recipes add is unmeasured, so the old (0.022, 0.022, 0.026) is gone. Clay (kaolin) taken into the effective medium darkens it (0.008 at 20 %), but real clay platelets of 0.2–2 µm scatter as particles and grey it ("more binder, harder and lighter", German Wikipedia *Zeichenkohle*). The effective medium can't show that, so the stick is gum-bound.
- **Grey versus black.** Vine reads grey on paper mostly because its marks are lighter and broken, so paper shows between them [P: "aren't as dark as compressed charcoal"; vine "comes in shades of gray"]. A loose dust of fine char (0.1–0.5 µm) is also greyer than the splinters, R∞ 0.025–0.035 (fine soot: 0.04–0.056). That is the "bloom" a loose drawing has. Not modeled: one R∞ per stick, and the fines' share of a mark is unmeasured.
- **Under fixative [D].** "The fixative fills the air gaps between particles", making "light grey values appear slightly darker"; heavy spraying darkens more [P] (Fine Art Tutorials, fixatives guide; ArtParts Fine Arts; AIC Paper Conservation Catalog chs. 3 and 23, "saturation changes").
  - *The resin.* Krylon 1301 has been found to resemble poly(n-butyl methacrylate) (AIC Conservation Wiki, BPG Adhesives): n_D 1.483, 1.06–1.07 g/cm³ (Scientific Polymer Products; Polysciences). PMMA is 1.4905 and Paraloid B-72 1.487, so n **1.49**, ρ **1.07**.
  - *How much a light spray lays.* Krylon Workable Fixatif's SDS (2017, rev. 5) lists, by weight, acetone 46.5 %, toluene 20 %, propane 12.75 %, butane 12.25 % and ethyl 3-ethoxypropionate 3.25 %. The 5.25 % left is the resin (and any additive), 5.4 % by volume of the liquid. An 11 oz (312 g) can covers 15–20 ft² a coat (retail listings: Hobby Lobby; Amazon and Walmart give "up to 20"). That is 8–11 µm of resin a coat. About half reaches the sheet, and a light spray is about a third of a coat [both E]: **1.5 µm** (1.4–1.8) (`charcoal::LIGHT_SPRAY_UM`).
  - *Share filled.* That resin fills the pores (porosity 0.55, the audit's for a loose bed) of the loose charcoal under it: 0.27 of 10 µm, 0.14 of 20 µm, 0.09 of 30 µm, all of a thin line under 3 µm (`charcoal::wet_share`; estimates-audit-2 C11). Pastel keeps its 0.15 (the easel's light spray), since changing it changes every pastel; for an average 20 µm layer the two agree.
  - *Darkening.* Mie with the pores filled by n 1.49: the facets reflect 0.042–0.061 against resin, against 0.082–0.088 against air. Carbon's S falls ×1.59, 1.37, 1.04 (R, G, B); its K barely moves; K/S scales ×1.52, 1.30, 0.96 (the mean of vine and compressed, `charcoal::WET_KS`). Vine R∞ (G) goes 0.0211 → 0.0205 (wet 0.15), 0.0185 (0.6), 0.0167 (1.0): L* 16.1 → 13.6 at full wetting.
  - *The engine* binds pastel by its rule (K/S × 1/(1 − w + w/5), for particles of n ≈ 2). For carbon that would be far too much: at full wetting ×5, L* 3.9. So the loose layer's carbon share (`Cell::carbon`) is darkened by carbon's own K/S scaling over its own wetted share, and the rest by pastel's rule (`Cell::bind_resin`). Pastel's own rule, which is right only near n ≈ 1.7, is an engine-wide fault left to its own change.
  - *So for a near-black a small absolute change is right.* Resin only roughly halves its facets' reflection. The deeper black and the lost grey of a fixed charcoal drawing are the fine-dust bloom and the paper wetting. A light spray leaves no film: the resin's menisci in the pores face every way, so the bed stays matte and unveiled. A gloss would need a continuous film, and a workable fixative is made not to leave one.

## 3. Adhesion: what holds it

- **Vine: only the tooth** [P]. The particles are "relatively large" and there is no binder (Met). "Large, loosely adhering particles" sit on the surface and are "extremely prone to smudging and lifting" (artist guides, search summary).
- **Compressed: the binder holds the crumbs, not the sheet [D].** The binder "creates adhesion beyond mechanical trapping" (Fine Art Tutorials) [P], but a dry binder can only bridge contacts it was wet at, those inside the stick. Gum arabic's glass transition is 62 °C at aw 0.33 and 42.6 °C at aw 0.54, measured for the pure gum, not only the juice mix, and it is sticky only near Tg [M: Righetto & Netto 2005, *Int. J. Food Prop.* 8:337]. At the studio's 50 % RH (`charcoal::STUDIO_RH`, a gum at equilibrium has aw = RH) its Tg is 46 °C, 21 K above a warm room: glassy. The stick's contact pressure is its hardness, about 5 MPa, while binder bonds in pressed powders form at the tens to hundreds of MPa of tablet compaction [P: pharmaceutics practice, no single source here]. So the deposit is all loose, as Fine Art Tutorials has it: "just like natural charcoal, compressed charcoal is smudgeable and dusty". The `grip` dial (a share bound as it lands) is removed. A wax binder, which flows under pressure, or a damp room where gum's Tg falls to room temperature would bring it back, with their own physics. Straight on from the measured points (92 K per unit aw), Tg reaches 25 °C at aw 0.73 and 20 °C at 0.78: a room above about 73 % RH, Paris's outdoor air (annual 76 %) but not a heated studio [E: extrapolated]. The easel's charcoal is taken at 50 % RH. #322 adds the one studio-humidity setting, and the hook to read it is `charcoal::STUDIO_RH`.
- **Particle size** [P, uncertain]. The Met calls vine's particles "relatively large" and "splinter-like". One unsourced blog gives vine fragments "from several hundred microns down to fine dust", and compressed charcoal as fine particles packed densely. A DTIC report on carbon-loaded paper found coarser carbon particles "came out of the paper very easily" [M, qualitative]. Size sets the lift (§4). R∞ barely depends on it (§2). The deposit's coverage law keeps pastel's particle thickness (6 µm).

## 4. Erasing

- **Order** [P]. Vine is the easiest dry medium to erase: "easy to erase", "soft, liftable properties" (Fine Art Tutorials); it lifts "off easily by dusting or erasing" (Wikipedia). Compressed is "more difficult to erase" (Fine Art Tutorials).
- **Physics [D].** A particle goes with the eraser if the eraser pulls harder than the paper holds. Hamaker constants (10⁻²⁰ J):
  - cellulose 5.8 (Bergström et al. 1999);
  - carbon 6.0: carbon fibres 5.2–6.2 and activated carbon 5.0–7.8 (Maurer, Mersmann & Peukert 2001, *Chem. Eng. Sci.*, and the values they cite); graphite is 24 ± 13 (Gallego-Fuente et al. 2026), but char is no graphite;
  - a kneaded eraser, polyisobutylene with vegetable or mineral oil and CaCO₃ and TiO₂ fillers (General's MSDS; Derwent SDS 2020; ESPO SDS), as a hydrocarbon polymer, 6.5 [E within 6–7].

  The forces:
  - *The eraser.* It is soft (E ~0.1 MPa [E]; W/E ≈ 0.6 µm, above the particles' roughness), so it wraps a particle: JKR pull-off 1.5πWR, with W = A/12πD₀² = 63 mJ/m² (D₀ 0.165 nm).
  - *The paper, coarse particles.* A rigid, rough particle above the asperity scale holds 10–150 nN whatever its size [M: irregular 40–65 µm grains 50–150 nN, Nagaashi et al. 2021; 20 µm spheres on rough glass, Moutinho et al.; pastel_brushing.md §1]. Take 39 nN, a decade wide (lognormal, ±1σ spanning ×10).
  - *The paper, fine particles.* Below the asperity scale (0.14–0.5 µm effective contact radius, Nagaashi) a particle sits on one smooth asperity: DMT, A·R/6D₀².
  - *Water.* Char fired at 500 °C is hydrophilic: water soaks into it before a contact angle can be read ("Pyrolysis temperature effects on biochar–water interactions", *Soil Systems* 2019, 3:27; Kinney et al. 2012, *Biomass Bioenergy*: least hydrophobic at 400–600 °C). Cellulose is too. At 50 % RH the Kelvin diameter of a meniscus is 1.5 nm, so water condenses at each contact, and a bridge at an asperity of radius a holds 4πγa more (cos θ ≈ 1): 90 nN at a = 0.1 µm (0.05–0.2). The eraser's putty is a hydrocarbon, hydrophobic, so it gets no bridge.

  | R | eraser | paper (vdW + water) | taken | dry |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.1–0.2 µm | 29–57 nN | 36–72 + 90 nN | 0.10–0.18 | 0.42 |
  | 0.5 µm | 143 nN | 39 + 90 nN | 0.54 | 0.87 |
  | 1.5 µm | 430 nN | 39 + 90 nN | 0.85 | 0.98 |
  | 3 µm | 0.86 µN | 39 + 90 nN | 0.95 | 1.00 |
- **Easel values.** The kneaded eraser's take per pass is `strength × lift × reach` (graphite.rs `erase`).
  - Vine **lift 0.85** [D]: splinters of R 1.5 µm, with the water bridge at a 0.1 µm asperity (0.72–0.92 over 0.2–0.05 µm; dry, it would be 0.98).
  - Compressed **lift 0.48** [D in f]: its deposit a share f of fines and the rest crumbs held as vine's splinters, both with the water bridge vine has at 50 % RH: fines taken 0.10, crumbs 0.85, so 0.10 f + 0.85 (1 − f), with f = 0.5 for a half-soot stick. The bounds are 0.10 (all fines, all soot) to 0.85 (all splinters). f is unmeasured; an SEM of a compressed mark would set it. The audit's 0.70 (estimates-audit-2 C4) is the same mix without the water bridge, which contradicts vine's own lift, so it isn't used.
  - The practitioners' order (vine easiest, then graphite, then compressed, Fine Art Tutorials) is the engine's: one pass takes 0.50 of a vine line, 0.49 of a 6B graphite one (lift 0.9) and 0.39 of compressed (`pastel::tests::charcoal`). Vine's margin over graphite is narrow. By lift alone (0.85 against 0.9) graphite would erase first; the rest is how the two marks lie in the tooth (the eraser's `reach`, and graphite's own coverage law), which this change doesn't examine.
  - The old 0.35 is below anything adhesion allows.
  - **Pastel's 0.55** (`PASTEL_LIFT`, unchanged so old logs replay) doesn't follow from dry adhesion, which takes its 1–20 µm chalk and pigment 0.87–1. With water, a 0.5 µm particle is taken 0.54 and a 1.5 µm one 0.85, so 0.55 is not far outside the physics. Computing it is an engine-wide change.
  - Charcoal that a finger, a blade, a dry brush or a puff moves is laid down again at its own lift: what the tool carries keeps the cells' lift, weighted by their carbon (`pastel::Carried`). Lift is a property of the particles, not of the gesture, and the eraser's `reach` term already takes the pressing into the hollows (estimates-audit-2 C10). Pastel moved this way is laid at its 0.55, as before.
- **Graphite** keeps its 0.9 (graphite.rs). Black chalk keeps its 0.72.

## 5. Smearing and dust

- The finger, a stump or a chamois blends charcoal (Wikipedia). In the easel the finger picks up 22 % of the loose deposit per pass (pastel_blending.md §3.1, measured for a dry residue). Both charcoals lie loose (§3), so the finger moves both far (test: share of the line's darkness smeared below it, vine 0.30, compressed 0.52). Compressed's dense mark holds more powder for its darkness, so it carries further. Graphite isn't moved by the finger at all in the easel (its smearing isn't modeled).
- A puff and a tap take loose crumbs (pastel_removal_air_blade.md). Vine is "dusty" (Fine Art Tutorials), and the conservation catalog calls charcoal's "extreme friability" the problem with treating it (PCC ch. 3).

## 6. Over and under other media

- **Paint over charcoal** seals it, as with every drawing (graphite.rs).
- **Pastel over unfixed charcoal** [P]. Charcoal dust muddies pastel laid over it. In the easel, pastel laid on loose charcoal mixes with it in the tooth's loose layer (`Cell::add_pastel`), so it comes out greyer; over fixed charcoal it lies on the bound crust and keeps its color.
- **Is the muddying right? [D]: too weak.** `add_pastel` averages the loose layer's reflectances by volume. Particles mixed in a powder mix by Kubelka–Munk instead: K and S add by volume, and carbon's K/S is ~23 against a yellow pastel's ~0.13 (in green). The script's example is a yellow pastel (0.75, 0.6, 0.1) whose S is chalk filler's in air (0.55 µm⁻¹) into vine charcoal:

  | charcoal by volume | the engine's mixing | Kubelka–Munk |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2 % | (0.735, 0.588, 0.098) | (0.704, 0.578, 0.100) |
  | 5 % | (0.714, 0.571, 0.096) | (0.649, 0.549, 0.099) |
  | 10 % | (0.677, 0.542, 0.092) | (0.578, 0.504, 0.098) |

  The loss from the pastel's own color is 1.5–2.5× larger by KM in red and green. In blue, where the yellow already absorbs, it is about none. The test's small margin (0.2370 over loose against 0.2351 over fixed) comes from this and from the thin charcoal line's small share of the volume. Fixing it means KM mixing in `add_pastel`, which every pastel shares. Behind a switch it is its own change, not this one's.
- **Partial fixing.** "A drawing may be only partially fixed, the lower layer being fixed before the last application of charcoal" (PCC ch. 3). The easel does this with `fix(mask)` or a fix between passes.

## 7. Deposit

The pastel stick's Archard law lays K·F·s/σp of the stick's volume.

- **Vine's hardness [M]: 3 MPa.** Wood charcoal's dynamic hardness (a Brinell-like portable tester) falls from about 29 MPa in the wood to about 3 MPa charred at 450 °C, 3.5 at 600 and 4.6 at 750 (de Abreu Neto et al. 2020, *SN Appl. Sci.* 2:1020, nine eucalypts of mean density 0.79 g/cm³, §3.3; the companion *Energy & Fuels* 2018 study gives 3.05 MPa at 450 °C). Charcoal is fired at 450–500 °C (FAO 1987). Willow (0.35–0.45 g/cm³) is lighter than these woods and cellular solids soften with density (Gibson–Ashby, ρ^1.5), so willow's could be lower. The studies found charcoal's hardness correlates less with the wood's density than the wood's does, so 3 MPa is taken (range 2–4).
- **Compressed σp 5 MPa, K 0.07; vine K 0.05 [D: bench].** Pressed charcoal briquettes crush at 0.13–3.8 MPa compressive (mangrove briquettes; cotton-stalk 2.2 MPa), and indentation sits about 3–20× above compressive strength for a frictional compact: σp bounds 2–10 MPa. K bounds 0.01–0.1 for friable compacts. Graphite pencil on paper measures K ≈ 10⁻³–10⁻² (Bertin et al. 2025, "The physics of pencil drawing"), so a cellular char or a gum-bound compact, which crumble by fracture, sit above it. What the bench needs is K/σp: draw a known force and length and weigh the stick.
- **What a worn volume covers [D].** The tooth counts a pastel's deposit as the volume it wore, covering as particles 6 µm thick (`PARTICLE_UM`). Willow charcoal is mostly pores. Its walls' true density is 1.44 g/cm³ (willow char, cited in Cutter & McGinnes, "A note on density change patterns in charred wood", *Wood and Fiber Science*), and the stick's apparent density is 0.27–0.35 (Cerne, chars at 400–750 °C; ρ_char ≈ 0.68 ρ_wood applied to willow's 0.35–0.45). So a worn volume is 0.2 solid, and it lays as splinters a cell wall (3 µm) thick. Plates cover their own area per their thickness, so a stick volume covers as 3/(1 − ε) = **14 µm** (12–16) (`charcoal::VINE_COVER_UM`). With the hardness, a vine stroke covers ×0.57 of what it did at σp 4 and 6 µm: the "vine reads grey" practitioners describe. Compressed (60 % solids) gives up 3 µm grains, which cover 1.5/d of their volume: d/(1.5 × 0.6) = **3.3 µm**.
  - *In the engine* a stick lays PARTICLE_UM/cover of its worn volume into the tooth (`pastel::Grains::lies`): the volume that covers there by pastel's law. Everything that works on the tooth's volume afterwards (the finger, a puff, the pores' filling, the fixative's pores) sees that. Vine's 0.43 of its worn volume is its 0.21 of solid at a packing of about one half, like a loose bed's, so it is close to what lies there. Compressed's 1.8 is less so: grains at 0.33 packing.
- The stick sizes are in §1.

## Estimates left, and why

| Constant | Value | Why it is an estimate |
|---|---|---|
| firing temperature | 500 °C | FAO's commercial charcoal; no art maker states its kiln temperature. Published Raman of drawing charcoals (Daly et al. 2018) could place it (estimates-audit-2 C6) |
