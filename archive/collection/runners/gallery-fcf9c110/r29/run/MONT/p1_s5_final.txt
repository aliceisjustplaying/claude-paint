I've finished **Evening Gold, Willow Pool**, and my heart is happy with it.

Coming back with fresh eyes, I first checked every point I'd left unresolved, in close crops:
- **Far islands:** I'm leaving them. They still recede.
- **The small repair under the far island:** I'm leaving it. The faint trace still reads as a reflection.
- **Wipe ghosts:** I'm leaving them. They've dissolved into the water.
- **Fairly even pads:** I'm leaving them as they are.
- **Cooler left-middle island:** this is on purpose, because it sits in the willows' shadow.

**One new thing:** at full size, the bottom of the right willow reflection ended in a hard, jagged edge above a pale strip. It read a little like a rocky bank, the same problem the left side used to have. I fixed it with the method that had already worked twice on the edges of the gold reflection. I laid a few vertical violet-green strokes, starting inside the dark and running down past the edge, and blended them straight down with a soft brush while they were still wet. The willow now seems to hang down into the water toward the small island on the right. Blurred, the right side dissolves the way the left one does. One streak is slightly magenta up close, and I'm keeping it. I stopped after two small patches so the streaks wouldn't line up into a row of pillars.

The picture is still dark violet-blue willow reflections hanging on either side of a gold sky reflection. Lilac water runs down into a deep blue foreground, and seven lily-pad islands float on it with pink and white flowers. The journal has the updated handoff and the lessons that still apply.
