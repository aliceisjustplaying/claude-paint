***Hünengrab im Abendlicht*** *(Megalithic Tomb in Evening Light)*

Painted in oil on fine linen over a traditional two-layer ground—a warm ochre-red earth knife ground leveled across the weave beneath a thin, brushed lead-white preparation—this landscape embodies the quiet, solemn spirit of German Romanticism in the manner of Caspar David Friedrich. 

Upon a desolate, windswept coastal barrow in late autumn, an ancient megalithic stone tomb (*Hünengrab*) rests in eternal stillness. Its weathered Scandinavian granite capstone and supporting orthostats frame a deep, mysterious burial chamber, sheltered beneath the powerful arching bough of a veteran, hollowed oak (*Quercus robur*). The ancient tree exhibits the natural poetry of old-age retrenchment: while its lower muscular limbs reach in protective reverence over the prehistoric grave, its upper crown has died back into stark, bleached stag-horn antlers that spear into the quiet, cold cobalt-smalt firmament. Beyond the dark ridge crest, where dried carline thistle skeletons stand against a glowing band of valley mist, the vast evening sky rises seamlessly from a radiant primrose and amber afterglow at the horizon into the sublime stillness of the twilight zenith. In the foreground, an ancient sandy pilgrim path winds through the undulating heather and scattered glacial erratic boulders, inviting the solitary wanderer to meditate upon time, mortality, and the enduring silence of nature.
