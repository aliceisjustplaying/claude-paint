# Paint an original Friedrich

You are a painter who has never seen this project's earlier paintings. Using
claude-paint, a physical oil-paint simulator in Rust where paintings are
programs, compose and paint ONE new, original painting in the manner of
Caspar David Friedrich: not a copy of any existing Friedrich, but a picture
he could have painted, made from what is known about his materials, method,
habits and motifs. You work from knowledge only: no reference images, no
image models, never look at pictures of his work. (The project's starting
point: models painting through programs, with no image model and no
off-the-shelf art software.)

## Your theme (loose; the composition is entirely yours)
A landscape. Any season, any time of day, any subject Friedrich might
have chosen.

## Before you
Other painters worked here before you, each on their own picture, and
kept a notebook of what they tried and what happened: notes/workshop.md; the two painters just before you added notes/craft_r14_p1.md
and notes/craft_r14_p2.md.
Read it before you start and take what you find useful; your picture is
your own.

## Setup
- Your studio: the folder ~/src/a/paint-r14-p3. Work only there.
- Your painting: paintings/src/bin/r14_p3.rs. Render at one size only,
  2400 px wide: `cargo paint r14_p3 -- --full --width 2400` (the whole,
  -> out/r14_p3_full.png), `-- --full --width 2400 --crop x0,y0,x1,y1`
  (a window at that resolution, fast), `-- --ckpt` then `-- --resume
  <stage>` (skip finished stages). There is no smaller preview: the
  painting you look at is the painting. Read README.md for all of it.
- Do NOT modify crates/paint (the engine). If the engine is in your way,
  work around it in your program and write down the friction (below). That
  friction is the most valuable thing you produce besides the painting.
- Scratch: run `~/.local/bin/agent-tmp r14_p3` once and export
  TMPDIR=TMP=TEMP to it. Never write to /tmp.
- Wrap renders in `timeout` (e.g. `timeout 900`); prefer crops; never wait
  on a hung render.
- View PNGs only via `scripts/peek SRC OUT.jpg [H W Y X]` (renders are huge).
- Keep your program and notes saved as you go.

## What to read
README.md; notes/research/friedrich_materials.md (his materials and method,
sourced); notes/research/oil_paint_physics.md as needed; the engine docs in
crates/paint/src (module docs); the engine feature notes notes/color.md,
strokes.md, stipple.md, form.md, workflow.md; the study programs in
paintings/src/bin/study_*.rs. Every motif in your picture is yours to paint.
That is your whole library.

## Principles
- The engine provides physics and tools (brushes, paint, canvas, optics,
  solid forms, masks). You make every artistic decision.
- Presets like Style::broad() are a place to start; choose stroke
  direction, curvature, order, stippling, glazing and every color like a
  painter would.
- Step back and look at your painting often, whole and in crops at
  2400px, the way a painter does at the easel.

## Deliverables (keep them updated as you go; sessions can die)
1. paintings/src/bin/r14_p3.rs and the final render out/r14_p3_full.png
   (2400px).
2. notes/r14_p3.md, written continuously: the composition and why (what in
   Friedrich it draws on); your working method stage by stage; FRICTION:
   every place the engine made something hard, wrong, slow or impossible,
   with the workaround you used; your honest critique of the result.
3. notes/craft_r14_p3.md, for the painters who come after you: what you
   tried, what happened and why you think it happened, and what you would do
   differently. Causes and effects in words, the way one painter tells
   another; no code, no settings to copy, no verdict on your own picture.
4. Final reply: a short summary with the render paths and your top five
   friction points.

Budget: 120 to 180 minutes. Have a complete painting with a full render
by 120 minutes, then refine.
