**Evening Gold, Willow Pool** is finished, and my heart is happy with it. I didn't add any paint this sitting.

I looked at the whole canvas fresh, at full size, squinted (blurred) and in gallery light, and in close crops. Then I checked each point I'd left open last time:

- **Right willow streaks:** I'm leaving them. They hang into the water toward the small right island without lining up into a row of pillars. The one slightly magenta streak doesn't show from a normal distance.
- **Far island and the small repair under it:** I'm leaving them. The pale marks below the island still read as the gold reflected in the water.
- **Rag-wipe ghosts:** I'm leaving them. The rectangular one in the lower water has disappeared into the ripples, and the streaks under the left willow read as the reflection fading out.
- **Fairly even pads and the cooler left-middle island:** I'm leaving them. The cooler island is on purpose, because it sits in the willows' shadow.

Blurred, the picture still holds together. Dark violet-blue willow reflections hang on either side of a gold column of reflected sky. The water turns from lilac to deep blue toward the front, and seven lily-pad islands with pink and white flowers float on it, getting smaller as they recede. I didn't find anything I wanted to take further, and more touches would start to fuss a picture whose strength is its softness.

The journal has the final handoff and the practical lessons that still apply.
