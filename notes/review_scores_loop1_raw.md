## Q1

```
scores: friedrich=5 paint=5 drawing=5 light=6 detail=5 total=26
worst: Snow at the base of the big boulder (and the small rock) is a row of five evenly spaced, identical white puffs, like cotton balls set along a shelf rather than drifted snow; one mechanical motif where the eye lands first (CRAFT).
next: Wood at upper left is a flat black-green mass with no trunks, gaps, or air, capped by a comb of near-identical fir silhouettes stepping down the slope in a regular rhythm (CRAFT).
next: Sky and snow field carry an even, horizontal wood-grain striation that runs the same everywhere; it reads as a surface filter, not as a ground texture showing through thin paint (TOOL, probably).
notes: The motif is sound: a glacial erratic, a lone spruce, a broken birch stump and a dark fir wood under a pale winter sky, all Friedrich vocabulary. The single spruce is the best thing here: drooping tiered branches with snow on their upper edges, drawn by someone who has looked at a spruce. The boulder has a real plane change and lichen, and the upturned dry-grass tufts are close to his "fine upturning" strokes. But the picture is spread evenly with no emotional center, and the repeated devices (snow puffs, fir silhouettes, grass clumps at equal intervals) give it away as assembled. The low cast shadows are consistent with light from the left, and the long diagonal shadow band carries the eye well, but the snow field has almost no value modulation toward the distance. Break the regularity, open the wood so it has depth, and let the snow meet the rock the way snow does.
```

## Q3

```
scores: friedrich=4 paint=4 drawing=3 light=5 detail=4 total=20
worst: The boulder, the staff-bearing figure and the tree trunk each have a pale halo outline, so they sit on the hillside like cut-out stickers; the figure, meant to be the center of the picture, is a flat dark shape with no contact with the ground (CRAFT, possibly TOOL edge artifact).
next: The tree's foliage is a set of flat dark-green blobs with lighter speckle, the "imaginary substance called foliage" Carus mocked; the branching is rubbery and doesn't say what species it is (CRAFT).
next: Middle-ground hedges are rows of identical dashed scribbles and the scattered bushes are uniform dark lumps sprinkled evenly across the plain; nothing is observed (CRAFT).
notes: Friedrich did paint wide views over a river plain with a spire and a figure. But this reads as sunny pastoral, with puffy cumulus in a blue sky and bright even greens, more a picture-book or animation background than his stilled, emptied light. The aerial perspective is the best part: the plain pales and cools toward a hazy blue horizon, and the spire and red roofs sit convincingly far off. The foreground grass has many upturned blades but they are all the same length and spacing. Structure is missing everywhere: the tree, the rock (a loaf with a dark end) and the figure are signs, not things. Draw one tree from life before painting another.
```

## Q4

```
scores: friedrich=7 paint=5 drawing=5 light=7 detail=3 total=27
worst: The lower third (wet sand and shallows) is an empty brown-gray fog sprinkled with evenly scattered dark specks; the tide line is unreadable and there are no specific stones, weed, shells or footprints, so the foreground Friedrich would have filled with observed things is simply absent (CRAFT).
next: The craquelure is an even, fine net of the same density over sky, sea and sand, visible even at 1000px; real cracks follow paint thickness, corners and stretcher, so this reads as an overlay (TOOL).
next: The hanging net, the one focal object, is a grid with vertical brown dashes over it and doesn't drape or sag from the rope; the poles are uniform sticks, the right one with a toy hook (CRAFT).
notes: This is the only one of the five that thinks like Friedrich: a low horizontal horizon, a sky taking up most of the canvas and graded from slate-blue to lemon, a thin crescent moon, a distant sail, gulls, net poles and a small figure with her back turned. The restraint and silence are right, and the light is one light: dusk after sunset, with dim reflections of the figure and poles in wet sand. The sky is the best-painted passage, softly stippled with thin violet cloud bars. What fails is what he was known for, which is minute specificity. The beach has nothing on it you could name, the rock at left is a lumpy mass without fracture planes and the figure is a schematic silhouette. Fill the foreground with a dozen things you've actually looked at and get rid of the uniform crackle.
```

The foreground grass is richer and more varied in value than in Q3 and is its best passage, but it's still a uniform field of blades.
