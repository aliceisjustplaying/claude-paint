I'm finished with **Evening Gold, Willow Pool**, and I'm happy with it.

It's still the same pond with no horizon. Violet-blue willow reflections hang down from the top edge, and a column of gold sky glows between them. Below, the water darkens through lilac to deep ultramarine, with lily-pad islands floating across it all.

**What changed this sitting:**
- **The horizon-like seam about a third of the way down.** Thin veils barely changed it. What worked was pale warm vertical strokes, blended downward with a soft brush right away. They now read as gold reflection running down past the old line, which no longer looks like a far bank.
- **The straight sides of the gold gap.** New hanging willow streaks on both edges break them up, and the gold now looks narrower and brighter.
- **The flowers.** I added three small white water lilies (a shadow stroke under three crisp petal touches) among the pink ones. They break up the sameness.

**Accepted as they are:**
- The farthest islands are still slightly crisp, though they recede well when the picture is blurred. A warm veil on the farthest one softened it a little, and more veils would turn them grey.
- The wipe ghosts have blended into the water and the dissolving reflection.
- The lily pads are painted in a fairly uniform way.

The main new lesson, recorded in the journal: strokes blended right away in their own direction, inside a soft area where the values already match, become reflections. That holds for light paint as well as dark. The earlier lessons still apply.
