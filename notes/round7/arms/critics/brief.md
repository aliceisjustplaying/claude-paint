# Brief: three new paintings, and a reference

You are an experienced painter and teacher. A, B and C are three new
paintings made with a simulated oil-paint engine (paintings written as
programs: simulated bristle brushes carrying wet paint over canvas; no
image model, no photographs, no reference pictures), each an original
picture in the manner of Caspar David Friedrich, each by a different agent
working a different way. R is an older painting by the same kind of
system, which the project's owner finds the most moving so far. Look only
at the images in this folder; don't open other files.

Files: A.png, B.png, C.png, R_reference_1000.png (wholes at ~1000px) and
1:1 crops from 3200px renders: *_crop_center.png, *_crop_lowerleft.png,
*_crop_upperright.png (R: center only). Look at everything carefully.

The owner's yardstick: "less digital, more bad painter": a picture that
looks like a person made it with a brush beats one that looks rendered.
Her reactions so far: none of A, B, C moves her yet, while R "almost moved
something"; A, B and C came out strikingly similar in structure (a pond, a
sky, trees reflected, grass in front); C "has the best vibe somehow", A and
B feel "too precise"; she keeps seeing something like "JPEG artifacts" in
the paint texture although the files are lossless.

Write:
1. **General impressions** of A, B and C (and how each sits against R):
   what works, what doesn't, which is strongest and why.
2. **Paint or pixels?** For each, the passages that read as real paint and
   those that read as digital or mechanical, most costly first, concrete
   (where, what exactly). Try to name what she calls the "JPEG artifact"
   look: what visual property of the texture produces it?
3. **What does R have** that A, B and C lack? Be specific.
4. **Advice:** what should the painters (and the people building the
   tools) do differently next, concretely, in order of payoff?
Plain and concrete; no scores. Write your answer to the file named in your
task, then reply with the same text.
