- day 1, 09:00: Plan. A June day on the meadows by the Bodden outside a small Pomeranian town, my own invention in Friedrich's way. Horizon low at y~468 so the sky takes two thirds. On the horizon a small town silhouette (x 560-820): a tall tower with baroque cap and lantern, a broad squat tower, a slender spire, nave roofs, trees. A windmill on a slight rise at left, another far right. A thin channel of the Ryck catching the sky light across the middle. Middle distance: a row of pollard willows along a ditch, grazing horses. Foreground: a path curving in, two figures seen from behind standing looking toward the town, a split-rail fence, June flowers in the grass. Ground: warm red/ochre knifed lower layer, lead-white brushed top. Method: pencil drawing, thin underpainting, sky graded by stippling and blending, land in thin layers, details last.
- day 3, 12:03: Sky first layer came out in stepped bands (lead white swamps the smalt; the stipple took hours and the paint set before blending). Let it dry two days as an underlayer. Second sky layer: eight graded piles in narrow overlapping bands, top down, blended in overlapping sections as I go while wet.
- day 5, 22:53: Land laid in six graded piles, far bluish-grey to lit yellow-green middle to dark olive foreground, then badger passes crossing. The near-vertical pass dragged streaks, which read a little like grass; fine as an underlayer. The river channel left out for its own paint. Waiting for the land to set before the far details.
- day 8, 17:46: Day 8: town silhouette in two coats (the first, with umber and white, read pink; the second with smalt and a little bone black set it grey-blue). Post mill on the left rise with lattice sails drawn by rigger. River reads as a hard white bar; needs banks, a darker far side and quieter value. Next: left rise, willow row along the ditch, cloud-shadow bands across the meadow.
- day 12, 15:47: Day 12: the meadow second layer in six zone piles came out as hard stripes; crossing badger passes turned them to mottle, then two wide horizontal passes calmed it into a soft green field, darker at the foot. Lost the cloud-shadow banding; I will restate it on dry paint with thin glazes and grass hatching. The far strip under the rise is still too pale and ends in a hard line at ~485: must be joined to the meadow with a transitional blue-green band.
- day 15, 20:35: Day 15: pollard willows along the ditch, ten receding. First crowns read as pale broccoli with spokes: the dark stipple too white and thin, the fanned rods too light and regular. The ditch is a ruled stripe. Next: when dry, restate each crown with denser, darker stipple of varied shapes (some lean, some lopsided, one younger and narrower), lit clusters on the upper left only; break the ditch with bank grass; darken trunks.
- day 21, 16:01: Day 21: willows restated, now too dense and dark: round lollipops. Plan to lighten them to silvery grey-green, loosen edges with rods, and haze the far ones. Path laid: too pale, hard-edged, blunt at the far end. Will glaze it down, add two ruts with a grass strip between, and let the meadow grass overgrow its edges and swallow its far end.
- day 27, 13:46: Day 27: path glaze too orange and saturated, ruts ruled like rails: the path reads as a horn. Once dry I repaint it opaque in a muted grey sand, then grass grows over both edges and a central strip, and the far end dissolves into the meadow.
- day 31, 18:47: Day 31: two horses in the middle distance right of the path, a grazing bay and a standing grey facing it. The grey began as a llama (neck too thin and upright); thickened neck, chest and barrel. Cast shadows came out as blue puddles; the grass will break them.
- day 35, 21:04: Day 35: meadow hatched as grass in bands (lit yellow-green bands alternating with cool cloud-shadow bands), foreground darker and longer blades. The rise re-hatched greener; a vertical seam at x~383 where its earlier mask ended, softened by a horizontal badger while open. Farmstead with red roof and trees by the mill. Left to dry two days before willow toning, figures, fence and foreground detail.
- day 38, 02:40: Day 38: two figures from behind on the path at y~560, a man in a dark greatcoat and cap, a woman in a red-brown dress with a pale shawl, looking over the meadow to the town. The shawl came up pink from the wet dress under it; the man's coat reads navy and flat. Willow trunks came out pink (umber+white). All to restate once set.
- day 41, 02:03: Day 41: repainted willow trunks from pink to grey-olive; the shadow and fissure darks dissolved into the still-open paint beneath. Waiting for the trunks and figures to dry before the darks. The crowns are too much like round broccoli; will turn them into brooms of upright rods with silvery leaf.
- day 44, 03:24: Day 44: fanned rods on the willows overdid it: they read as palms or grass fountains. Next: bury most of the rods under clustered leaf stipple in the broom-shaped envelope, dense at the core, broken at the edge, so only a few rods show at the rim.
- day 48, 17:00: Day 48, a hard look at scale. The figures (100 units tall, feet at y 561, heads on the horizon) set the eye height at about 1.75 m, so near y 580 a metre is ~69 units. By that measure my pollard willows are 1.5-2 m high: shrubs, not willows. The horses are toys too, and the far cattle are mice. Rather than live with it, I paint the whole willow row out with meadow and put in a new row of full-sized pollards along a ditch from the left edge (y~548) to near the river (x~405, y~499), heights ~2.85*(y-468): the nearest ~230 units, rising well into the sky as a dark repoussoir on the left, with a gap and a stump where one has been felled, so the mill shows through. Horses repainted smaller and farther, at y~505.
- day 51, 18:26: Day 51: new pollard row painted at true scale on the left: five trunks (nearest 219 units) with heads of rods, then crowns stippled dark underside, grey-green middle, silver top-left. They read as round trees more than pollards but they hold the left side and frame the mill. Town silhouette given a cooler, darker blue coat. River toned: lower edge darker, reflections of the towers, glints. Next: horses at true scale (s~19 at y~503), a fence on the right, foreground flowers and seed heads.
- day 51, 21:20: Day 51 evening: fence of split posts and two rails on the right, laid out in perspective (eye 1.75 m, focal 1000 units) from the right edge down to the river bank; the horses stand in the pasture beyond it. Found that the river toning had run over the figures at shoulder height (the RIVER mask predates them) — repainted the shawl cream (no more pink) and the coat band; the coat needs another coat when dry.
- day 54, 04:35: Day 54: tree shadows under the pollards went on far too dark (blue-black hedges); I tried to soften them with a badger while wet and it smeared them into streaky bars across the meadow. Lesson again: no unclipped blending near fresh dark. Letting it all dry, then repaint the meadow in front of the pollards with lit grass hatch and put back only a light, cool shadow. Also: the crowns and trunks are thin enough that the horizon line and the river show through them; they need another opaque coat.
- day 61, 21:37: Day 61: coat restated near-black; foreground given tall grass stems, seed heads, buttercups, daisies, sorrel, clover. Whole view: the left meadow repaint is a smooth flat slab ending in a hard edge at y~592; it needs grass hatching and a shadow band like the right. The pollards are too pale and round to hold the left as Friedrich's dark trees would: darken trunks, add darker underside masses to crowns and cover the horizon line showing through them.
- day 69, 23:27: Day 69: pollard crowns re-stippled: dark underside, grey-green mass, silver-lit top left. Covered the horizon line that showed through them; the band behind the trunks repainted as flat hazy meadow. They now hold the left as a dark mass against the pale sky. Next, after drying: a few straight young shoots breaking the crown tops so they read as pollards, then the sky's upper blue and the meadow's hard seam at y~513 on the left.
- day 72, 01:14: Day 72: birds (a loose group of three over the town side, two singles, a lark high), path edges shaded, a worn grass strip down its middle, pebbles, figures' cast shadows falling right across the path, a walking stick. The path is still the palest thing in the lower half and pulls the eye away from the figures; once dry, a cool thin glaze over its near part.
- day 76, 21:18: Day 76: cow parsley umbels on dark stems at both lower corners and one by the path, lit blades over the dark foreground, a thin cool glaze on the foot of the path and the lowest grass. Fixed the horses' relation to the fence: they stand behind it at ~50 m, so the near rails now cross in front of their legs, and grass breaks the blue shadow slabs under them. I judge it done: a quiet June forenoon, the two looking from the path to the towers across the Ryck meadows. Things I would do differently: plan the scale of trees and animals from the eye height before painting anything, and never blend near fresh darks.
- day 90, 02:54: Day 90: back at the easel after judging it done, and I didn't agree with myself. The path ran into a blunt square end at the bottom edge: widened it to leave the canvas at the lower edge, darkened its foot with a glaze, and let tufts and encroaching grass break both edges and grow in the worn middle. The pollards read as mushrooms: pale smooth stems under flat dark caps with a band of the rise showing through at crown level. Repainted the trunks grey-olive with fissures, lit ridges on the left and grass at the foot. Opened a gap of sky and rise under each crown, drew bunches of dark rods springing from knobbly heads, then hung leafy lobes back down over the rods so the canopy's underside is ragged. They now read as pollard willows.
- day 105, 13:16: Day 105: the town's foot was a bare row of equal walls sitting on a ruled line. Added hazy trees at both ends, a row of low houses with lit west gables and red-grey roofs in front of the churches, a far post mill and a low far shore on the horizon. A first haze glaze over the town went on as ragged blobs; painted over it with the horizon sky and a fresh coat of the town grey, then restated the lit faces, dark nave roofs and windows. Now it's a town with a skirt of houses and trees rather than a cut-out. I judge it finished: the pollards, the path's foot and the town were the three things my earlier 'done' had left unresolved.
- day 107, 23:43: Day 107: back at the easel a last time. Gave the black horse a cool sheen of sky light on crest, back and rump (the first rim ran round the whole head as an outline; took it back off the head and throat with black), and the bay a warm lit back. Added one particular: a white stork stalking in the meadow between the first and second pollards, black wing, long red legs and bill down to the grass, feet sunk in blades.
- day 107, 23:43: Judged complete and saved: 'Morning on the Ryck Meadows'.
- day 107, 23:47: Day 107, late: back once more for small things. Gave the man's greatcoat a half-belt with two buttons, a back vent and lit fold ridges on the skirt; the woman's dress folds falling from the waist and spreading at the hem, and two soft folds in the shawl running to its point. The dark mark round the black horse's head I leave: it reads as a halter. Saved and closed: 'Morning on the Ryck Meadows'.
