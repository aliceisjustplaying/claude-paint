- day 1, 09:00: Plan: an estuary at evening, a Thames-style sailing barge with a tan sail dark against a low luminous sky. Horizon about y=440 (sky two thirds). Sun just set left of centre (x~600), glow yellow-gold there, cooling to grey-blue and smalt above, with a bank of darker cloud across the upper sky. Far shore a thin cool strip with a low clump of trees on the right. Water takes the sky, a gleam path below the glow, the barge reflection broken in ripples. Foreground: a mud spit lower left with a few mooring posts. Warm buff imprimatura (white, ochre, a little red earth) over knifed lead white, 600mm x 400mm.
- day 2, 07:38: Sky laid in four bands wet, blended; cloud bank of mauve grey (white, smalt, red earth, umber) worked in and blended soft; gold under-rim went on as a zipper of dots, rescued by blending into the edge. Water laid in three bands gold to mauve to grey-blue, blended level. Water is too close in value to the sky; will glaze it darker once the far shore is in. Resting overnight before the dark shapes.
- day 5, 11:32: Far shore in; the tree clump first came out as smooth humps like mountains, restated as lobed crowns in a darker cool mix. Barge blocked in: tan spritsail, topsail, foresail, mizzen, black hull. The sails read as flat orange slabs, too hot and too light for contre-jour. Next: darken them toward umber, model the cloth with vertical folds, light the leech with the glow through the canvas, add sprit, mast, rigging.
- day 8, 21:29: Mistakes today: the second water pass ran its unclipped strokes over the hull and buried it; then an unclipped blend over the reflection dragged the foresail tack into the sky as a fuzzy blob and smeared pink bands sideways across the water. Value study shows the picture all light-to-mid, with no dark anchor except the hull. Plan: let it dry, restate the hull crisp, repaint the whole water darker (deep blue-grey in the foreground), draw the reflection fresh into the wet water with clipped hatching, no loose blending. Then the mud spit dark in the lower left for a foreground anchor.
- day 13, 08:23: Water glazed dark in the foreground - the glaze came far stronger than the earlier sail glazes, a near-black streaky band with a hard top edge at y~520. Worked mauve horizontal strokes and ripple lights into it and blended; now a calm gold band near the horizon over a ruffled dark band, which reads like wind on the water and I will keep. Mud spit laid in dark brown, edge beaded and messy, needs restating. Squinting, the design holds: gold horizon band, barge silhouette, dark foreground.
- day 14, 20:35: Leech glow went on as hot opaque orange patches; blending the whole mainsail to soften it spread the orange across the sail and dragged the new sprit and mast into streaks. Lesson again: blend only a narrow zone, never the whole shape after accents. Will let it dry and restate the mainsail in a deep russet body, then put the backlit glow on as a thin narrow scumble along the leech and peak, unblended.
- day 20, 08:12: Repainted the whole water in one pass (gold band, mauve, dark grey-blue) for coherence, then drew the barge reflection freshly into the wet as horizontal hatched dashes over shapes of the inverted sails, ragged ends, not blended - it finally reads as a reflection. Spit redrawn flatter; its found edge came out serrated so I turned the spikes into marsh grass with rigger tufts. Mud still a dull flat brown mass - next: vary it, wet sheen, stakes.
- day 22, 00:21: Bad hour. A scumble for wet sheen on the mud went on as opaque cream blobs (a lead-white-heavy pile at load 0.25 is not a scumble here). Blending to fuse them spread the cream over the whole bank: now a pale grey mound. Must let it dry and repaint the mud in opaque dark body, then leave it alone. Keep sheen, if any, to a few thin long strokes of a darker, lower-white mix.
- day 26, 07:17: Added a windmill on the far shore and a distant sail right of centre, small and cool, to answer the barge across the empty right half. Long thin ripple strokes across the water hid the two hard seams left by earlier clipped blending; the water reads as a calm estuary with ruffles. Puddles on the mud too bright and cut-out: to dull. Mast reflection too continuous: to break.
- day 27, 13:46: Last session: darkened the hull reflection under the keel, dulled the puddles with a mud glaze, broke the mast reflection with ripple dashes, restated the stakes with a glint of glow on their tops, added four gulls and two poplar spires on the far clump. Glazed the lower-right corner of the water and the top of the sky to hold the eye in; the top glaze came streaky and I left it as windblown cloud after one soft blend. Value check: gold horizon band, dark barge, dark spit and a darker foreground all hold. Stopping here. More work would only muddy it.
- day 27, 14:53: Back with fresh eyes. The mainsail read as a flat cut-out, so I put a narrow broken band of warm light (red earth, vermilion, chrome yellow, white) inside the leech and along the head of the topsail. It's clipped to a 2-unit band and not blended, so it reads as backlight coming through the cloth edge. The band also caught the foot of the sail as a pale hatched patch, which I covered with two flat strokes of the deep sail mix. Everything else I left alone. The picture is finished.
- day 30, 14:58: Back once more. The two puddles on the mud still read as cream cut-outs, the brightest things in the foreground and the wrong colour. After three days' drying I glazed them clipped with a thin cool mauve (smalt, red earth, umber, a little white) so they take the same sky as the estuary, then drew one faint gold hairline along the far edge of each for the horizon glow. They now belong to the water and no longer pull the eye away from the barge. Nothing else touched. Finished.
- day 30, 14:58: day 30: Back one last time and looked at it whole, close up on the barge and the spit, and in value and squint. The masses hold: gold horizon, dark sail and hull, dark spit lower left, and the reflection broken in the water. The puddles now sit at the water's value. The sails are flat, but they read as contre-jour silhouettes and I prefer that to working them again. No paint added. Checked the log against the canvas, saved and closed. Title: Evening Tide, Barge off the Marsh.
