# Proposed improvements to the painter instructions

Prioritize complete foreground protection, an inspection checkpoint after blending and faithful transfer of laid trials. These address repeated failures across A, B and C; the current brief already requires campaign inspection and comparable trials (Brief:35–39; ranked evidence below).

## 3. Proposed text

These are proposed edits, not changes to the supplied prompts. The wording uses diagnostic checks rather than numerical recipes. Rankings 12–15 are mostly existing advice or lower-priority additions; the new material concentrates on rankings 1–11. Thin-tint, interlocking-hatch and fluid-relief recipes remain test candidates below rather than new default repairs.

### Techniques: “Plan before you paint” — replace the sketch-session bullet, lines 10–13

> - **Try it on scratch.** Use `scratch: true` to test a mix or stroke. Recreate the passage's underpaint, treatment and drying state, then transfer the successful recipe with the same handling. Scratch has its own tools and clock; the painting still needs its own drying check.

Basis: rank 3; Brief:39 and Guide:698–715 already support the instruction. This replaces developer-only setup advice, not a new sketch workflow.

### Techniques: “The palette” — add after “Steer a mix by eye”

> - **Match the painted passage.** For a repair, start with its recipe and handling from the log. Compare a laid trial with the present surroundings: after later layers or a different deposit, the old recipe may no longer match. The board or a held pile cannot settle the match.

Basis: ranks 3 and 8; Guide:97–101,652. C's successful log retrieval is retained alongside A's substrate warning.

### Techniques: “Lay-in: lean, quick, thin” — add after the first bullet

> - **Coverage is stroke density.** If an old color shows through, inspect the gaps, pigment hiding and film thickness. Shorten starving strokes or reload more often. `fill=true` closes gaps with dabs; trial their texture before using them across a quiet plane.

Basis: rank 5; Guide:164–168,230–232,321,324,331 and Studio:46–48,75–77. This does not prescribe thicker paint for every lay-in.

### Techniques: add “Blending” immediately before “Glazing and scumbling (over dry paint)”

> - **Blend for the transition you need.** Keep the modeled lights and darks distinct; trial narrow transition work before blending a small form's whole body. Keep shapes you want to preserve outside the blend mask, even when they are dry: wet paint can move onto them.
> - **Look after one pass.** Inspect before blending again. If darks turn pale, texture disappears or the weave shows through, stop and reconsider the film instead of blending the loss wider.
> - **Treat a recurring seam as a passage problem.** If local blends or patches keep leaving a ring or box, trial a different method across the connected transition. A new graded layer up to natural boundaries may work; protect its neighbors and compare the whole result before extending it.

Basis: ranks 2 and 4; Guide:314,340–347 and Studio:124–136. A/B/C support the failure diagnosis; C's successful local alternatives prevent a blanket whole-field rule.

### Techniques: “Keeping control” — replace “Clip,” lines 183–185

> - **Protect the shapes that are there now.** Finish broad backgrounds before fine marks when possible. For a later pass, clip away every current branch, grass tip, figure and other mark to preserve, or plan to restate it. Reapply object exclusions after growing or blurring a background mask, then check the final mask. Soft or lost edge handling is not protection.

Basis: rank 1; Guide:327,340–358,382–396. This is final-mask verification, not the unverified historical rule that subtraction before blur is always safe.

### Techniques: “Keeping control” — add after “Clip”

> - **Check the final mask.** Before a costly pass, sample `m:at(x, y)` inside and outside the target, including beyond each end of a band. Check the mask after all operations. `rect(x, y, w, h)` takes width and height, not the opposite corner.

Basis: rank 6; Guide:382–401. No claim is made about undocumented extrapolation beyond curve endpoints.

### Techniques: “Keeping control” — replace “Masks from earlier chunks,” lines 186–188

> - **Keep what a later sitting needs.** Globals persist between chunks; locals do not. Retain needed paths, widths, recipes and protection masks. Before redrawing by eye, check the saved paths. In the journal, name unresolved passages, drying dependencies and marks to preserve; recheck their state on return. Keep large temporary masks local.

Basis: rank 11; Guide:668–674,695–696. Paths helped exact restatement in C; notes preserve continuity but do not guarantee better decisions.

### Techniques: “Marks” — add after “Marks follow the form”

> - **Diagnose the pattern.** If marks become beads, lozenges or pickets, compare their width, length, spacing and direction with the form before repeating them. Restore the large shape and light-to-shadow relationship before adding more texture; a continuous accent may need one dragged stroke rather than a row of touches.

Basis: rank 9; Studio:78–83,119–120. Subject-specific successful marks remain examples, not prescriptions.

### Techniques: “Glazing and scumbling (over dry paint)” — replace the glaze/scumble bullets, lines 170–177

> - **Glaze** with transparent paint over a dry passage. Added medium makes paint more fluid and transparent, but its amount and the `glaze` hand do not guarantee a useful veil. Trial the laid color, value and reach; a white-rich veil may lighten and hide instead of tinting. Over impasto a glaze can pool in valleys and leave ridges showing.
> - **Scumble** with light opaque paint dragged thin over a dry darker passage. Trial its breakup: low load alone does not ensure broken color, and an opaque spot may cover more than intended. Give an overall glaze a visible purpose and a successful trial before spreading it over passages that already work.

Basis: rank 10; Guide:97–101,340–347 and Studio:32–35,49–53. The existing dry-support bullet, lines 178–179, stays.

### Techniques: “Time” — replace the first bullet, lines 197–200

> - Time passes only as you paint or `wait`. Drying depends on pigment, film thickness and oil; added medium slows it, and an absorbent ground can quicken setting where it draws oil from thin paint. Check `drying(x, y)` along the next stroke or wipe and at nearby edges. Use open paint for intended fusion; wait for dry support when a glaze or covering correction needs clean separation.

Basis: rank 7; Guide:67–70,97–112,601–615. This corrects an internal documentation conflict; the journals supply no absorbent-ground experiment. Exact oil and absorbency effects remain verification questions below.

### Techniques: “The whole picture, every time” — replace “The full value range,” lines 47–49

> - **The intended value relationships.** Check `mode: "value,squint"`: do the large lights, middles and darks produce the effect you want? Give the light enough contrast to read; choose the range for this picture before adding more detail.

Basis: rank 12. This qualifies an existing absolute to fit the brief's freedom of subject and manner (Brief:3–7,37,41–43); it is an editorial interpretation, not a new engine finding.

### Brief — two sentences total

Insert after the first sentence of the trial paragraph, line 39:

> When a trial works, transfer its actual recipe and handling, then inspect the result on the painting.

Insert after the existing normal-color finish inspection, lines 51–52:

> Also check gallery light where the painted surface matters; use relief light to understand ridges before deciding whether they need repair.

Basis: ranks 3 and 14; r31-smoke:29–31,52–58,61–66; paint-studio-d95ea6:50,87,93; Techniques:150–153. The first sentence reinforces an existing Guide:711 instruction; neither sentence requires another repair or sitting.
