I'm calling it **"October Dusk, the Meadow Pond"**, and I'm happy to leave it here.

The scene is a lemon-to-violet sky about twenty minutes after sunset, over a hazy far tree line with a farmhouse and a thread of smoke. A pale pond holds the last light, and a dark meadow glazed in umber leads up a worn track to a small woman in a red shawl at the water's edge.

**What changed this session**
- **The grove.** I'd left it unresolved last time. I covered the old silhouette with sky and repainted the whole sky in one sitting. Then I redrew the grove as separate trees, an elm, a right-hand tree, a tree on the far bank and low growth beneath them, and painted each mass wet into wet, with olive light on the side facing the glow and cool light along the tops.
- **The opening under the elm.** Once the paint was dry, I opened a gap under the elm's canopy so the glow shows through, crossed by its trunk and forked limbs, a sapling and a few hazy bushes. I like this change most: the grove now reads as trees with sky behind them, and that light under the trees echoes the light in the pond.
- **The meadow.** A last glaze settled a pale column in the foreground left over from an earlier patch, and I softened a hard seam at the foot of the grove into mist.

**What still falls short of Inness**
- The edges of the tree crowns are scalloped where his would dissolve into the air.
- The right-hand tree is still a heavy block.

My reflections are in the journal.
