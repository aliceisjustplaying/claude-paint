I'm leaving **Luncheonette, Four O'Clock** finished as it is. I didn't add any paint this sitting.

It's a red-brick corner building in low four-o'clock sun. The shadow of a building outside the picture cuts diagonally across the lower left of the front wall and the sidewalk. A counterman in a white coat stands in the dark doorway under the green LUNCHEONETTE sign. Above him and a little to the left, a woman rests her folded arms on the sill of the one open window, and neither knows the other is there. The lamppost, the water tower and the pale buildings farther off make the quiet right side.

I looked at the whole canvas again in normal colour, then in grey values and squinting. The picture still holds, and the white coat in the dark doorway is where the eye goes first. Then I checked each passage I had left open, in close-up:
- **The woman:** her crossed forearms and sloping shoulders read clearly. I'm leaving her as she is, because she's so small that more modelling would look fussy.
- **Her window:** a greyer patch at one end of the raised sash looks like the shaded inside of the open frame.
- **The counterman:** his hem and outline are clean. A couple of tiny pale flecks at his shoulder can't be seen at viewing distance.
- **The shaded brick:** it's still close in value to the sunlit brick. I'm leaving it, because the shadow across the storefront and sidewalk makes the cast shadow read.
- **Leftovers I'm also accepting:** the specks in the distant shopfronts and the flat, poster-like surfaces.

**Practical lessons (also in the journal):**
- To fix a flat area, repaint the whole of it up to real edges, over dry paint.
- Pale paint goes over a dark only when the dark is fully dry.
- Thin broken layers of paint look like spots on these flat surfaces.
- Small touch-ups need the exact original mix and should stop at a real edge.
- Hand-lettering needs a width for each letter and adjusted gaps between them.
- Drying times:
  - Small details in earth colours and black: about 10–20 days.
  - Areas mixed mostly from lead white: about 3–8 days.

My heart is happy with it. It's a quiet, sunlit corner with two people alone at four o'clock.
