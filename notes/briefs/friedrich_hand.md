# Friedrich's hand: read before painting

One page on how he worked, in order, and where the irregularity in his
surfaces comes from. Principles, not recipes. Sources and details:
`notes/research/friedrich_process.md` (keys in brackets refer to it).

## How he worked, in order

1. **Studies from nature, carried home.** Pencil studies of single trees,
   rocks and ranges, with notes on them: "in the distance," a ground line,
   the horizon, ranges numbered front to back for their aerial perspective
   later [KH]. The picture itself was invented whole in his head [NG p.49],
   and its parts were taken from the studies almost unchanged, enlarged
   and traced onto the canvas [MOST-abs; KÖR fig.8].
2. **A bare room, one light from above.** Nothing on the walls but rulers;
   the paints kept in the next room [NG; WP-KERSTING-EN]. Kersting shows
   him seated with his arm on a maulstick, or standing, leaning on a chair
   back: close, small, deliberate work.
3. **A bought ground, tone chosen for the light.** Warm under white, or
   reddish ocher as the mid-tone of a moonlit picture. Its brush striations
   and even a scratch stayed visible; he let them [KÖR p.284].
4. **Draw everything.** Faint chalk or pencil, then the whole picture "properly
   and definitely" in pen and ink, grass and leaves included; straights
   ruled; symmetry guides rubbed off before paint [NG pp.49, 58]. No part
   is drawn less carefully because it matters less [CATS p.132].
5. **A thin brown lay-in, like a sepia wash.** Values first, in one color
   [NG p.49; KÖR fig.1].
6. **One or two thin layers of color over it**, glazes over the light
   ground; body color only in snow, lights and last touches [NG pp.51, 56].
   He darkened with blue [MÄD]. Greens in up to four layers of different
   greens [MÄD].
7. **Each passage in its own hand.** Skies, mist and far hills stippled, so
   thin that the paint gathers in the ground's pits and no stroke shows
   [NG p.56; CATS p.127]. Firs in short hatching. Near snow slightly
   pastose. Ice dry-brushed. Ground left bare in places [HH].
8. **Leave things out.** He drew ships, nets, a well and a walking stick,
   and painted none of them [CATS; NG; WP-ZM]. He seldom added what wasn't
   drawn [ART].
9. **In states, over months.** The Monk's sky was seen gray and heavy,
   then pure and calm, then with a moon and morning star; the final sky was
   painted just before shipping. The whole picture had first been laid in
   dark blue [WP-Monk; SMB-blog]. Stop, let it dry, look, repaint what is wrong.
10. **Particulars last, in opaque paint:** grass blade by blade in fine
    upturned or straight strokes over the finished ground, gulls placed on
    the finished sky, the moon in white [NG p.56; CATS p.130; WP-ZM].

## Where the irregularity comes from

Not from noise added at the end. It is layered in the matter:

- **The ground:** knife waves under brush striations under thin paint;
  a patchy white; a scratch.
- **The drawing:** two passes, lines broken where the pen skipped over the
  ground, beads of ink where it lifted, ghosts of guides rubbed away,
  lines that "shimmer through" [NG p.58; CATS p.130].
- **The paint:** thin paint pooled into dots; coarse smalt splinters as
  blue specks; a few pigments mixed by hand, never the same twice;
  thickness that changes from passage to passage.
- **The edges:** at a boundary two colors may **not even touch**; the ground
  shows between them, with dots of the drawing left in the gap [HH].
- **The changes:** a repainted sky, a turned figure, overpainted ships.
- **Time:** cracks, yellowed and crazed varnish, faded smalt, abrasion from
  old cleanings [SMB-blog; HH].

## Principles

- **Precise in drawing, uneven in matter.** Copies fail by being even, fast
  and vague: the Dortmund copy has no underdrawing, "a more even paint
  surface" and "rapidly applied" passages [NG p.50]. Our renders fail the
  same way.
- **A few mixed piles, never a formula.** His color came from a spare
  palette and the knife. A smooth color function across the sky is the
  opposite of his method (Alice: "maybe a little too perfect").
- **Detail is not graded by importance, but by distance.** Near things are
  drawn blade by blade; far things are "single delicate brushstrokes" the
  eye assembles [HH].
- **Lost edges by stipple and pooling, found edges by drawing and the last
  opaque touch.** Decide each one; don't soften everything.
- **Look at two distances.** Close up his pictures read as drawing; from
  across the room, as light [WP-Tetschen]. Check both before you stop.
