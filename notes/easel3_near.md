# easel3_near: a sandstone block at the edge of a spruce wood

(Working notes, written as I go. Session log: `paintings/lua/easel3_near.lua`.)

## The picture and why

A close view of a bedded sandstone block (Elbsandstein type) standing at the
edge of a spruce wood on a still late-autumn morning. Behind it, a pale
morning sky over a misty far wood; at left, the dark spruces of the wood's
edge; on the block's top, a young birch has rooted in a crack. At the foot:
dead bracken, grasses, fallen birch leaves and small stones.

What it draws on in Friedrich: his close studies of rocks and trees (the
Elbsandstein and Harz rock studies, the spruce thickets, tree studies drawn
with the place and date noted), the habit of setting a near, dark, detailed
motif against a pale, strokeless sky, and the symbolic pairing of the old
rock with the young tree that grows out of it. Palette: the post-1820
palette with greens (lead white, pale smalt, cobalt, ochre, red earth,
umber, bone black, chrome yellow, Prussian blue, green earth).

## Stages

1. Canvas (friedrich style, 1820 greens palette, aspect 1.3).
2. Sky: a gradient from cool gray-blue to warm cream near the horizon, broad
   strokes and a blend, then (after a day) a thin stipple veil over it to
   hide the brushing, as Friedrich stippled his skies.
3. Far wood: a low band of spruce spires, hatched, hazed blue-gray; the
   forest floor as warm brown tone. First try had an even crest (a hedge);
   redone with uneven spires.
4. The rock: a form of three stacked blocks (beds), turned, roughened, cut.
   First tries: a single block (read as a hay bale), then pillowy rounded
   beds painted over paint that was still open (see friction). Third try:
   harder rounds, painted after `dry()`.
5. Spruces at the wood's edge (tree{habit="spruce"}), limbs then hatched
   needles, clipped behind the rock.
6. The rock's local color: sandstone from shadow gray to pale ochre, rain
   streaks and algae, strokes down the shadow planes and along the beds on
   the lit faces.
