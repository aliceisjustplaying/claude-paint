**The Oak in Snow at Evening**

The painting is finished. One old oak stands on the central axis at the crest of a snow-covered rise, just after sunset. The sky is painted in bands, from cold smalt-cobalt overhead through a pale green-gray down to a warm Naples-yellow glow over the horizon. Beyond the edge of the snow there's only a band of forest in valley mist and a faint ridge to the right.

**This sitting (days 318–332)**
- **Moon:** I added a thin waxing crescent high in the cold blue on the right, with its lit side toward the set sun. It's the one thing I'd been missing. It gives the empty right side a small second light that answers the oak without competing with it, and makes the stillness read as evening.
- **Fine branches:** I'm leaving them paler than a true silhouette. Nothing has changed since my last attempt: the paths of the first, paler branches still aren't recorded, and I couldn't recover them from the random generator.
- **Faint pale patch in the upper-left sky:** left as it is. It still passes for a thinning in the cloud.
- **Trunk:** left as it is. The spread of the roots holds.
- **Snow:**
  - A dark violet blot in the lower-right corner was left from last sitting's glaze. My cover dab dried pale and showed, so I wiped it off. A tiny fleck remains at the very edge, where a frame would hide it.
  - Under raking light, earlier snow work around the base of the trunk shows as a raised rectangular slab. My attempt to feather its edge read as a new pale patch, so I wiped that off too. In normal light the slab is nearly invisible, and I'm leaving it.

**New lessons** (the earlier ones in the journal still apply)
- Patches on dry snow behave like patches on the sky: an opaque dab over a dry, blended area dries paler and shows. Wipe it off at once.
- Holding a pile up to the canvas shows the paint thick, which looks lighter than the same paint thinned in a blended area. Pick a mix that looks a shade too dark on the knife.
- Check the gallery and raking-light views before adding anything. Old raised paint shows there even when it doesn't in normal color.

My heart is happy with it: a solitary oak rooted in the snow, the last warm light behind it and a thin moon rising in the cold.
