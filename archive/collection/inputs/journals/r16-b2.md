- day 1, 09:00: Plan. Subject: a summer evening after sunset on the flat meadows by the Ryck outside Greifswald, the town silhouette (three church towers, roofs, windmills) dark against a luminous western sky. Low horizon (~0.62 H), large sky graded from smalt blue at top through pale greenish yellow to warm apricot at the horizon, a few thin rose-grey cloud bars, a young crescent moon and the evening star. Land: a strip of river/Bodden water catching the sky, flat meadows with haycocks and a few grazing horses, a ditch with pollarded willows, a fence; foreground dark meadow with a path, grasses and small flowers, two small figures seen from behind looking toward the town. Method after the notes: warm red/ochre knifed ground under a brushed lead-white top, pencil underdrawing, thin brown underpainting of the land, sky in wet-in-wet bands blended level, then thin layers and details last.
- day 1, 10:45: Sky laid in five bands (smalt+white+a trace of black at top, pale smalt, lemon white with chrome yellow, apricot white with ochre+vermilion at the horizon), blended level with long ruler passes while open. The zenith came out paler and greyer than I want; I will deepen it later with a graded smalt glaze once dry. Two dark smears at y~160 will become thin cloud bars. Land underpainted thin in umber/red earth/ochre (too orange but it is only the warm under-layer), cool grey strip for the distant plain.
- day 2, 13:34: Zenith deepened with a graded smalt glaze (medium 0.72, load falling with height), blended level: now a proper deep twilight blue, weave showing through in the glaze like pooled paint. Warm horizon glow re-laid over the dry lemon band in two bands, strongest near x=600 where the sun went down. It left a faint step at y~370; thin cloud bars will go across there.
- day 5, 02:30: Cloud bars laid as noisy ribbons in violet-grey and blended soft with a badger; upper wisps took on a rose tint over the blue, which I like for after-sunset. Town silhouette (St Jacobi spire, St Nikolai with its lantern cupola, the squat St Marien, three naves, gabled roofs) and two post mills painted as masks, but the mixture came out far too pale, almost sky value. Letting it set, then restating darker and cooler over it; the pale layer beneath should keep it airy.
- day 6, 01:52: Land laid in four bands darkening toward the viewer (green earth, ochre, umber, a trace of Prussian; lead white in the far band for air), blended level. Reads as a flat misty plain, which is right for the Greifswald meadows; the banding is a bit regular and the far strip under the town is too white. Waiting for the town to dry before restating it darker.
- day 8, 16:58: Mistake: a mown hay field came out as a bright yellow rounded blob like a pond. Painted it back with the meadow green and a toned-down straw strip with straight field edges, then blended with a rect mask, which left rectangular seams at x=280 and y=570 (the notes warned me). All of this is under-layer; the meadows will get a second, more particular layer once dry. Town restated with trees at its base and post-mill sails, but still too pale; next time a much darker mix and a better-drawn silhouette (stepped gables, the Dom helmet as a bell, not a ball).
- day 13, 10:46: Town repainted in a much darker smalt/umber/black mix over the dry pale one: stepped Backstein gables along a wall-like row of houses, buttressed naves with stepped east gables, the Dom helmet as a bell with lantern and needle. Painted the ghost of the old round cupola out with a sky-matched cream. Mills now have proper lattice sails as solid quads. Far plain restated blue-grey-green with two strips of Bodden water catching the glow, thin hedgerow lines and distant tree clumps built from many small crowns. The far plain is still too grey and pale against a too-yellow middle ground; the land needs a second, darker and cooler layer.
- day 16, 00:09: Second layer on the meadows, cooler and darker toward the viewer, a muted straw strip for the mown field, one level blend over the whole land below 482 (a curve, not a rect). Coherent now but soft and smudgy; the pale far band I will make into evening mist lying over the Bodden meadows, feathered into the grass, rather than fight it. Then the particulars: ditch and pollard willows at left, path and two figures, haycocks, horses, a fence.
- day 20, 01:50: The mist over the far meadows worked after a fashion: a graded white glaze whose rect edge I had to break with short vertical badger passes; it now reads as vapour hanging over reeds. The ditch and path went badly: the ditch is a hard wire with dark outlines, and the path first came out a flat bright yellow wedge, then (restated darker, reshaped as a winding track) still too light, a rounded tongue, with yellow showing through where I painted it out. Letting it dry before repainting the foreground properly; grass and reeds will break the ditch.
- day 26, 02:21: Path glaze went on over tacky paint and grabbed into blotches like bark; the painted-out wedge left a dark trapezoid. Decision: once dry, repaint the whole foreground below ~590 as one continuous coat, then lay a narrower, quieter footpath only slightly lighter than the grass, and build the grass over its edges.
- day 35, 04:25: Moon attempt: a crescent at (742,250) in the cream part of the sky had almost no contrast. Painted it out with a cream mix that dried lighter than the sky, leaving a pale hazy disk; the evening star touch vanished too. Will correct with a thin, slightly cooler veil when dry and put the crescent higher, where the sky has turned blue enough for a pale moon to read.
- day 38, 04:34: Turned the accident into the subject: the pale disk became the earthshine of a young crescent (as in the Two Men Contemplating the Moon), with the lit crescent in near-pure lead white on the side toward the set sun. Quiet, but it reads up close. Evening star restated as a small white touch.
- day 46, 09:27: Meadows restated as perspective field strips (grey-green far, olive, two straw strips of mown hay, darker greens nearer), clipped to wavy edges: graphic, flag-like, but in the spirit of the Greifswald meadows; details will break them. Then the four pollard willows along the ditch: radial hatching over crowns built from ellipses along each rod. Came out as one merged grey hedge-mass, too light and too big. Plan once dry: open a few sky gaps between crowns, darken with a deeper green (no white) leaving the grey as silver at the edges, then trunks, bare rod bases under the heads, and spiky rod tips above the foliage.
- day 51, 22:22: Looking at the whole, the merged willow mass reads convincingly as a distant grove standing in the evening mist on the left, so I am keeping it as that rather than as near pollards. Painted out the crown spill below it and removed the wire-like ditch entirely with the strip colours; both passages disappeared cleanly. Next, once dry: deepen the grove, ground it with trunks and mist at its foot, then the near particulars.
- day 56, 00:15: Deepened the grove with radial hatching in a dark green (no white) kept inside a shrunk mask, and set small trunks at its foot. Two faults: the hatching again ran below the grove foot (I forgot to cut the crown masks at the ground line), and the untouched 4-unit grey rim now reads as a halo round every crown. Once dry: repaint the strips below the foot, break the rim with dark feathery touches on the shadow sides, keep silver only on the upper sky-facing edges.
- day 60, 05:43: Particulars in the middle ground: thirteen haycocks in two perspective rows on the mown strips (straw body, a lighter crest toward the sky, shade on the side facing us), two horses in a paddock at right (a bay grazing, a grey with its head up), a silvered post-and-rail fence along the brow of the near meadow with an open gate where the path passes. Sizes set from an eye about 8 m above the plain (a figure at y=620 about 37 units). Grey horse far too white for dusk and the cocks sit on too-dark contact shadows; both to correct when dry.
- day 64, 07:06: Foreground grass: ~5000 tapering blades with a pointed round, lengths and widths growing toward the bottom edge; the light sky-caught blades came out too bright in a crop (like frost) but at arms length the meadow finally has texture. Whole view now: grey horse and haycocks pop too much, the strips are still too flag-like. Next: the two figures on the path, then glazes to quiet the middle ground, mist at the foot of the grove, birds, foreground plants.
- day 68, 07:57: Two figures on the path, seen from behind, looking toward the town: a man in a dark greatcoat and top hat, a woman in a wine-red dress with a pale shawl and bonnet, ~37-40 units tall at the path. The shawl picked up some red from the wet dress, which reads as a warm light. A small flock of eight rooks flying home toward the grove in the pale sky.
- day 72, 10:15: Quieting the middle ground: thin umber/smalt glaze on the grey horse (it dried dappled, a dapple-grey now), hatched stubble and windrows over the mown strips in two greened straws, and a shade glaze on the lower halves of the haycocks, blended upward with a small badger so each cock darkens from a skylit crest to its foot.
- day 76, 11:07: Evening mist drawn across the foot of the grove: a thin scumble of white with smalt, heavily badgered sideways until the trunks stand in haze and the crowns float. A hard vertical seam where the mist ended was feathered away.
- day 80, 14:25: Mistake: a feathered glaze across the strip seams spilled into the grey horse and laid dark scallops along the y=555 seam. Rescued while wet by badgering the bands sideways - the seams are genuinely softer now, the meadow reads less like flags - but the grey horse is streaked and must be repainted, and there is a smudge on the path.
- day 89, 15:08: Repainted the grey horse, then - impatient, chained a glaze onto it while still tacky. It grabbed into streaks; the horse is now a muted dun with a pale rim along its back, which honestly suits the hour better than the chalky white. Leaving it.
- day 95, 21:03: A bad hour at the town. A darkening glaze on the lower town spilled over the roofs like stacked logs; badgering fixed the texture but left marks in the sky. Cutting them back with a ring of apricot sky colour made a glowing halo around the whole silhouette, and badgering that halo (unclipped) chewed the tower edges and blurred St Jacobis spire. No undo: will let it all dry, restate the whole silhouette crisply from the town mask, then correct the sky around it zone by zone - cream above, rose in the cloud bars, apricot near the horizon.
- day 108, 05:31: Restated the town from its mask in the old smalt-umber-white: crisp spires again, the halo reduced to a faint afterglow hugging the roofs, which a backlit town at dusk would have anyway. Small sky patches over the last spill marks first showed as pale rectangles (rect masks again!) - feathered their edges with a badger while wet and they have melted into the glow.
- day 134, 06:28: Repair at the groves east end, where a sky patch had cut into the crowns: first attempt picked up wet sky paint and came out as grey smooth ovals; after drying, rebuilt the crowns from ~200 small leaf-cluster dabs in deep green, then broke them with a few grey-green hatch touches like the rest of the grove. The trees now overlap the first houses of the town again, which ties grove and town together.
- day 144, 06:28: Finished on day 144. Standing back: the quiet deep sky and the long flat bands are the Friedrich part; the particulars (the couple on the path, rooks, mills, haycocks, the horses, the gate, dock and thistle) are there to be found at close range. Faults I am leaving: the far water still reads a little like a lake of ice, the foreground blades are slightly too bright for the hour, the meadow seams are softened but not gone. Saving.
