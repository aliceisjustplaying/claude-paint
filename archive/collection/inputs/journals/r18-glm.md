- day 1, 09:00: Session 1. 40x32 linen, warm pale knife+brush ground. Subject chosen: estuary at dusk from an old mooring — a dark broken treeline, amber light-path on falling water, a furled-sail yacht moored near a dark grassy spit, a few birds. Palette: cobalt+bone+ochre+vermilion+earths+lead. Plan: warm wash to set the evening key, sky built in bands and blended, clouds wet-in-wet, then distant shore, water, light-path, boat, foreground and birds.
- day 1, 09:16: First toning pass failed: glaze hand laid separate loud strokes, ground showing between. Letting it set and will cover with a fuller opaque tone (lead+ochre+umber+black, low medium).
- day 1, 17:01: Tone established after two misfires: dense lead/umber/black/ochre broad pass x2, blended. Warm mid grey-brown ground. Waited 6h before covering the failed glaze; will wait again for this layer to set before sky.
- day 7, 14:30: Accident: a full-sky blend dragged a pale veil over the whole canvas, bleaching the darks. Deciding to work with it: a misty silver dusk. Re-establishing treeline, headland, bank, path, boat and top sky over the veil.
- day 8, 05:24: Back for the final session. Judgement at the look: light-path and masses are good. Fixes needed: (1) mist where headland meets water, (2) the amber cloud's left edge is notched with square steps, (3) the moored yacht's hull is lost against the bank, (4) drag streaks on the left water, (5) foreground spit flat. Build up mist first.
- day 8, 12:15: The mist wedge turned into a pale scumble cascade down to the water - the load was too heavy and it runs straight down the middle of the canvas like a waterfall. The old stepped amber edge is gone at least. Scrapping the wedge idea: I will knock the cascade back with the dusk grey sky tone, keeping a soft amber haze only near the tree tops.
- day 8, 12:56: The grey overpaint went grey-violet-mauve - the umber/cobalt mix is too cool. The middle sky is now a big washed grey fog where the amber cloud was. Lesson of the session so far: every big soft pass I make turns to grey sludge; the amber cloud needs a patient opaque rebuild in amber tones, not grey knocks. Sitting down to rebuild it deliberately: warm ground colour first, then lit tops.
- day 8, 17:26: Treeline repay failed: laid wet into the wet amber haze, came up chalky grey - the dark picked up the pale underneath (note 28 in reverse). Waiting for the whole middle to set before trying again; then I will lay the wood more solidly in two firmer passes, less medium.
- day 16, 15:23: The treeline is repaid and strong, but the misty shore beneath has gone cold slate and the bank-strip overpaint reads as a second grey band. Also the whole middle is drifting too dark: what the picture wants is the far shore warm-dun with its dark clumps, water between it and the bank pale grey, then the bank dark. Warmth and lightness must come back into that strip. Plan: glaze warm dun (ochre+umber+lead) over the strip, then repaint the far shore line of trees ON that.
- day 16, 16:24: The streaks refuse to die: floated grey over them and blended for 19 minutes and they show as a grey wash-curtain that stops above the boat. This water has eaten hours. New tactic: STOP fighting the streak field. Let the lower-middle water be a broad grey plane - I will paint one luminous horizontal dusk-light band across the whole width (a 'cold glinting band' like a second reflection of the bright sky), bright, wet, right across, to break the vertical streaks and give the eye horizontal water. Rough scumble drops at coverage 0.8 in grey-blue, then a lighter grey band across to reset the field, then I'll judge again.
- day 16, 17:50: Boat hull fought back all session but now reads as a small dark vessel at anchor with a red flag - its reflection will be an amber smear beneath.  Sending the anchor chain now and cleaning up the last of the boat's grey halo with soft cool grey so it sits IN the water rather than on it.
- day 17, 05:24: The boat area has eaten eight attempts and gets worse each time - last look shows a brown potato with blue puddles. Painting over a zone repeatedly on the sim leaves ghost edges and every 'final' boat on unset ground picks the bed up. Hard decision now: I am DONE trying to make this boat. One last bath over the whole zone across all its ghosts, one LAST simple dark hull laid on set ground in a single pass with zero repair, and if it still fails I paint the boat out forever and let the light-path carry the picture alone. No more fiddling in between.
- day 22, 15:46: The boat is a brown potato again. My poly stayed in a 130x30 unit box and the hatch fill simply didn't build the hull I saw in my mind. I have decided, after all this: the boat comes OUT. The picture is a dusk estuary with a light-path - the boat is not its soul. I will bathe the zone once more in the cool grey and finish the water there; a dark moored shape was nice to want but the painting doesn't need it. Letting go.
- day 23, 02:54: Done. The last look shows a coherent dusk: warm haze sky with a cream horizon band, one dark treeline across with mist and rooted tufts, marked water carrying the amber reflection down to the foreground, the dark bank sweeping in from the right, the moored mast and grasses at the edge, birds high. The session was a long series of rescues - the boat was surrendered, the varnish turned to broken colour and had to be repainted into - but the picture survives its accidents and has a mood of its own: a hushed, silver-and-amber evening. Naming it 'The Last Light on the Flats'. Saving.
