# Brief: compare five pairs of small painting studies

You are an experienced painting teacher. The images in this folder are
five pairs of small oil studies made with a simulated oil-paint engine
(paintings written as programs; no image model). In each pair, X and Y
share the same subject, canvas, palette and underlying geometry; only the
way the paint was applied differs. You don't know who made them or how;
don't guess. Look only at the images in this folder; don't open other files.

Pairs (each: a 1000px view of the whole and one crop from a 3200px render,
same window for X and Y):
1. pair1: a broadleaf tree crown in summer leaf
2. pair2: a rock on grass in daylight
3. pair3: an evening sky over a dark horizon
4. pair4: a shore and its reflection in still water
5. pair5: a bare winter tree

Files: pairN_X.jpg, pairN_X_crop.jpg, pairN_Y.jpg, pairN_Y_crop.jpg.
Look at every image carefully, whole and crop, before writing.

The one question that matters: **does it look good as a painting?** Not
"is it like a particular artist". Judge as a painter would: masses and
values, edges (found, soft, lost), how objects meet their surroundings,
economy of marks, how the paint surface reads (brushwork, texture, anything
mechanical, repetitive or digital-looking).

For each pair write:
```
pairN: prefer X|Y|neither (strength: slight|clear|strong)
why: <one or two sentences>
X defects: <the two or three most costly, concrete: what, where, why it reads wrong>
Y defects: <same>
```
Then one closing paragraph: across all ten studies, which defects recur
(the same problem in several studies), in order of how much they cost.

Write your answer to the file named in your task, then reply with the same
text. Be concrete and plain; no scores.
