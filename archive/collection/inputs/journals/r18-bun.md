  Studio notes from this campaign, for next time:
  - coverage= as a function is NOT supported in this build (it errors "converting Lua function to f32"). Gradients have to be built from stacked soft masks at different coverages, or from stipple.
  - work() with a hard-edged rect/glow mask leaves a visible rectangle. Always feather well outside the damaged area.
  - An option table with only string keys has no array part, so table.unpack(t) returns NOTHING and every option is silently dropped. Write the options out, or keep an array part.
  - raw umber is a LIGHT earth. A "dark" pile of raw umber + lead white is mid-value; you need bone black in it to get a dark.
  - Big passes get starved: a filbert 20 with 40-110 unit strokes at coverage 2.4 over 250k units lays a thin veil. filbert 12, length 24-64, dips={4,1.0,0.1} and fill=true gives a solid coat.
  - Stipple is the right tool for soft tone: a dark pile over a lighter passage lays smooth and pattern-free. A pile much LIGHTER than what is under it reads as orange spray. Same lesson as the fill=true leopard spots.
  - blend(m) leaves a hard disc if the mask is a shrunk copy of the shape - use (m:shrink(3)):soften(3).
  - blend() over a lean layer lifts the lead-white ground through in pale blobs. It needs a thick opaque base under it first, and even then only twice.
  - Never blend a whole group of touching objects: it drags one fruit's colour onto the next.
  - Surgical fix-ups with masks that cross other objects do more harm than the flaw. Repaint the object instead.

  What the repainting taught me, all of it the hard way:
  - COVERAGE IS NOT OPACITY. A pass at coverage 2-3 is a veil. To bury a strong old colour (saturated vermilion) I had to go to coverage 8-10 in two passes. The same pile over a pale cloth comes out much lighter and more saturated than the same pile over a dark ground, because the film is thin - so a pile has to be mixed against the ground it will sit on, not judged on the palette.
  - The recipe that works for a solid coat: hand="broad" (or a big filbert), coverage 3-4.5, fill=true, pressure 1, dips={1,1.0,0.0} so every stroke gets a full load. With dips={3,...} the load runs out along the stroke and I get starved streaks. Then ONE blend over the opaque coat to fuse the strokes, and no more blending after that.
  - THIN FILMS BLOOM. Any small, thin or low-coverage application of a lead-white or vermilion pile over a dark ground glows like a lamp - I made three separate glowing highlights and had to paint them out. For a highlight on a dark form either lay the fruit's own light colour more thickly, or use a lead-white-free earth. fill=true over a small mask beads the paint into cottage cheese; a single touch is the only clean way to lay a small light spot.
  - A sphere reads from lay-in, terminator, core shadow and a quiet reflected light. It does not need a specular highlight, and it certainly does not need a hard edge-light along the silhouette - that read as a drawn outline and had to be painted out.
  - Colour: raw umber is a LIGHT earth, so "umber + black" is still mid-value, and yellow ochre + red earth is a pumpkin, not earthenware. Earthenware wants raw umber + red earth + a little lead white and nothing bright.
  - Paint order matters absolutely. I laid cloth passes that painted straight over the fruit and the bowl and had to repaint the group. All cloth work now goes through free = cloth - bowl - fruit, and the fruit are painted last.
  - `below(600)` is an error - below() wants a curve or a point list, not a number. That one cost me three failed chunks.
  - A contact shadow must be a pool just under the object and offset away from the light, not a ring around its base; a ring reads as dirt. Same for a ring-shaped highlight.
  - Long thin accent strokes (a filbert 6 with an explicit stroke and clip) are the one thing that stays crisp - I finished with fold lines on the cloth and a thin light on the bowl's rim laid that way.
  - A glaze of a warm dark over the group and a cool dark over the room tied the whole picture together and gave the darks their depth.
