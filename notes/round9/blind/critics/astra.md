(・_・) Reviewed all four whole paintings and all twelve crops. Ranking: **N, L, M, K**. Image filenames below are the evidence for these visual judgments.

## 1. General impressions

**1. N — strongest emotional picture:** the small person beside the cross, under the enormous damaged tree, makes the empty snow feel like a place of mourning rather than unused space. The dark ceiling of sky and low yellow light support that feeling. The tree’s sideways spread gives it presence, though its twig network becomes diagrammatic on inspection. This could move someone before they notice how it was made. (`N.png`)

**2. L — most convincingly brushed overall:** the gray-pink sky and broad, overlapping snow strokes make a gentle winter atmosphere, but the ruin, winding stream, tree, evergreens and fence compete to explain the scene. The traveler’s approach to the ruin could be affecting; the evenly snaking stream makes the journey too decorative. It feels more like an illustrated destination than N’s unresolved encounter. (`L.png`)

**3. M — convincing loneliness, less convincing paint:** the long canal, abandoned boat and immense pale sky offer quiet isolation, but the orderly shrinking trees and smoothly modeled snow make the landscape feel constructed. The boat is an emotionally useful detail: something left behind, not merely another winter symbol. The stiff figure and tidy perspective weaken that possibility. (`M.png`, `M_crop_lowerleft.png`)

**4. K — striking silhouettes, weakest human touch:** the looming pollarded willows and yellow horizon have menace, but their crowns look like bundles of straight rods and their trunks break into distracting fragments at ground level. The distant walker could supply loneliness; the oversized mechanical trees keep drawing attention away from that feeling. (`K.png`, `K_crop_center.png`)

## 2. Paint or pixels?

Problems are listed most costly first. These are judgments of appearance, not claims about implementation.

### N
- **Mechanical:** the big tree resolves into sharp, repeated angular forks, with many equally explicit twig endings. Its trunk is a nearly uninterrupted dark cutout. (`N_crop_center.png`, `N_crop_lowerleft.png`)
- **Mechanical:** a conspicuous crack network covers both smooth sky and snow; it supplies an antique surface without supplying brush decisions. The isolated horizon blobs also resemble a row of dabs rather than distant land. (`N_crop_upperright.png`, `N_crop_lowerleft.png`)
- **Paint-like:** the cream paint banked around the tree’s foot has dragged, overlapping edges. The snow caps on the foreground rocks read as a few useful loaded strokes. Preserve that economy. (`N_crop_lowerleft.png`, `N.png`)

### L
- **Mechanical:** the main tree’s continuous bark stripes and neatly piped snow follow its branches too faithfully. The trunk ends abruptly instead of entering the snow. (`L_crop_lowerleft.png`)
- **Mechanical:** the ruin’s openings are crisply cut from a rather uniform gray wall; the birds are bold little emblems. The moon’s stippled halo looks locally applied rather than part of the surrounding sky. (`L_crop_center.png`, `L_crop_upperright.png`)
- **Paint-like:** broad diagonal snow strokes overlap without describing every drift. The sky has directional dragging and broken coverage. The ruin’s lower edge dissolves into rubbed, mottled paint: a better passage than its roofline. L has the strongest evidence of a hand working across a surface. (`L_crop_lowerleft.png`, `L_crop_upperright.png`, `L_crop_center.png`)

### M
- **Mechanical:** the canal’s long, clean banks and repeated diminishing trees make perspective feel like a layout exercise. Small grasses accumulate as separate upright tokens along the edge. (`M.png`, `M_crop_lowerleft.png`)
- **Mechanical:** the sky and snow are smoothly blended under an oversized crack network. The cracks become the most articulated thing in otherwise quiet passages. (`M_crop_center.png`, `M_crop_upperright.png`)
- **Paint-like:** the boat’s pale top and dark hull have useful dragged ends. Short cream strokes across the canal suggest paint laid down rather than an effect applied everywhere. Those local passages need more influence over the whole. (`M_crop_lowerleft.png`)

### K
- **Mechanical:** the willow shoots are long, straight wedges with blunt ends, packed into radial fans. Differences in length do not disguise the repeated construction. (`K_crop_center.png`, `K_crop_upperright.png`)
- **Mechanical:** blue snow shadows contain dense, nearly parallel ridges; beside them, the trunk base disintegrates into horizontal flecks. Both attract attention as surface artifacts rather than snow or bark. The crack network adds another competing layer. (`K_crop_lowerleft.png`, `K.png`)
- **Paint-like:** broad off-white snow swipes overlap visibly, and the irregular gray horizon contains some convincing blunt brush placements. Keep these larger marks; reduce the fine activity around them. (`K_crop_lowerleft.png`)

## 3. Advice, highest payoff first

1. **Make the picture work without the antique skin.** Next versions should omit visible cracking entirely. Compare the bare paintings before restoring anything. K, M and N especially let cracks carry surface interest where brushwork is quiet. Tool builders: keep aging separate and off by default. (`K_crop_upperright.png`, `M_crop_upperright.png`, `N_crop_upperright.png`)

2. **Repaint the dominant tree, not every twig.** K needs fewer shoots, crooked growth, grouped dark masses and interruptions. N needs fewer equally legible forks. L needs broken bark stripes and snow that does not trace every limb. M needs individually shaped crowns rather than a procession of similar units. Tool builders: prioritize strokes that change width, exhaust their paint and merge into existing wet masses over adding more fine lines. (`K_crop_center.png`, `N_crop_center.png`, `L_crop_lowerleft.png`, `M.png`)

3. **Let snow erase parts of things.** Brush pale paint across sections of trunk bases, banks and distant silhouettes. Keep only a few sharp edges near the emotional center. L’s softened ruin base already demonstrates the payoff; M’s continuous canal banks demonstrate the need. Tools should make painting back over a shape easy, not merely roughening its outline. (`L_crop_center.png`, `M_crop_lowerleft.png`)

4. **Protect the emotional subject through subtraction.** N: keep the person, cross and tree relationship; simplify competing twigs. L: remove some foreground grass and fence activity, and make the stream’s bends less regular. M: preserve the boat and vacancy, but interrupt the tree procession. K: subordinate the branch fans so the walker can matter. (`N.png`, `L.png`, `M.png`, `K.png`)

5. **Test brush behavior on small passages before another full landscape.** Use one trunk entering snow, one gray-blue shadow and one pale sky patch. Seek L’s overlapping snow strokes and N’s dragged snow mound, not K’s repeated shadow ridges. Judge at both whole-picture size and crop size. The target is a few imperfect, purposeful strokes—not uniform roughness everywhere. (`L_crop_lowerleft.png`, `N_crop_lowerleft.png`, `K_crop_lowerleft.png`)
