- day 1, 09:00: Plan. A winter evening after sunset: a Huenengrab (dolmen) under snow on a low knoll, an old stag-headed oak to its left, a second, broken oak to its right, the flat country beyond sunk in mist with a ghostly church ruin and firs on the far horizon. Sky from grey-violet overhead down to a pale lemon-rose band at the horizon, a thin crescent moon, a few crows. A small figure with a staff stands looking at the stones; footprints lead to him through the snow. Canvas 70 cm, aspect 1.4, fine linen, warm red-ochre knife ground under a brushed lead-white top ground, as his were. Method: pencil underdrawing, thin underpainting, one or two thin layers, stippled gradations, details last.
- day 2, 14:55: Day 1-2: first sky layer came out far too pale; lead white swamps everything at these proportions (swatch tests at the bottom edge, to be painted over: smalt 4 : white 1 is only a mid slate). Second sky layer laid over the dry first: grey-violet smalt zenith, lilac-grey, a peach band of vermilion and ochre, lemon of chrome yellow at the horizon, blended wet; the zenith stippled and blended again to soften the mottling. It reads as a clear cold after-sunset sky with haze. Land underlayer still far too pale: it must come down in value so the horizon glow is the lightest thing.
- day 3, 15:58: Day 3: land second layer: misty blue-grey distant plain, cream-rose crest of the knoll catching the afterglow, cooler grey foreground, blended along the slope. Into the wet distance: a low fir wood on the far left horizon (first firs came out like bottles; restated with wider drooping-tier silhouettes) and a Gothic church ruin looming paler behind them, its lancets open to the lemon sky. Now letting the land set before the oaks and the stones go over it.
- day 6, 19:07: Day 6: the two oaks. Built each from hand-placed trunk and main boughs (a crooked leader dying back at the top, a snapped bough on the right tree, dead stag-horn limbs), with side branches grown along them by the area rule and zigzag sympodial deflection, then a pass of short crooked twigs clustered along every thin branch and at every tip, which gives the bushy oak crown edge. Blunt ribbon ends extended into tapering shoots. First dark pass over the wet brown only lifted it: the silhouettes are still a flat mid brown and must go much darker and cooler when set. The dolmen came out like a cake with a lumpy top; it will be restated under a snow cap.
- day 9, 12:50: Day 9: trunks and big limbs darkened with a full-loaded pass of umber, bone black and smalt; now a flat dark silhouette, needs cool skylight on the tops of limbs, bark and snow. Foreground restated darker and cooler, then long drift crests laid in: a light warm ridge with a cool shadowed slope under it, blended along. They read soft, almost cloudy; grasses, stones and footprints should fix them to the ground.
- day 22, 17:31: Days 19-22: twigs restroked with a bone-black-rich mixture: thin films of the umber mixture came out tan, lighter than the zenith, wrong for trees against an evening sky. Trunks glazed to one dark silhouette. Crescent moon low in the west above the glow, horns turned away from the set sun; a crow on the dead leader, a few more in the air. Footprints first painted as pale pills (the lit rim on the wrong edge: the far wall of a hole faces us and is in shadow), restated as a trodden trough. It still reads too light and too straight. Grass tufts too yellow and too few for dusk.
- day 40, 23:50: Days 25-40: the dolmen has been a struggle. The snow cap came out as a symmetrical frosted lid with regular scallops; rock bumps painted into it read as dark helmets; snow put over them while they were wet went grey. Waited for everything to dry and laid a new cap opaque: an asymmetric top, higher toward the left, a lit sky edge, an irregular lower line, thinning at the rounded ends where the rock shoulders show. Kerbstones half-sunk in the snow with caps, a leaning fallen slab at the right. Figure: a man in a long dark coat and cap with a staff, back turned, at the end of his trail, looking at the stones.
- day 56, 02:41: Day 56: lesson learned again and again: dark over wet light goes grey and streaky (the roots, the rock in the openings). Now waiting three or four days before crossing any wet passage. Openings under the capstone now show the misty plain and the snow behind, so it reads as stones standing on legs rather than a hut, though still too regular. Snow drifted against the foot of the dolmen and the oak roots. A big erratic boulder bottom right and a broken stump bottom left, to give the empty foreground some weight; both need their snow. The drift under the big oak left a pale oval that must be knocked back.
- day 73, 03:43: Day 73: squinting showed the land as light as the glow and nothing holding the bottom edge. Glazed the foreground with smalt, a little umber and red earth in much oil, blended the upper edge out. It went down in cool swirls that read as wind-scoured snow. Snow laid along the tops of the near-horizontal limbs. Still to do: the stump top (a crown of zigzags), the drifts at the stump and boulder too white for the darker ground, the step in the trail, muted and more numerous grasses, the dolmen modelling.
- day 83, 06:04: Day 83: granite mottling on the dolmen came out as a leopard skin: far too light and too even for stones seen against the glow. Will let it dry and glaze it back down with transparent black and umber until only a whisper of grain is left. (Also: a noise stretch takes plain numbers, not named ones.)
- day 114, 08:56: Day 114: a bad week at the dolmen. The transparent glaze meant to quiet the granite ran past its mask in long horizontal strokes, filled the openings with brown and dirtied the plain on both sides. Repainted the stones opaque in one dark body with only a faint grain, then had to match the misty plain around them: my first mixes had far too much white (the old plain has sunk under glazes); small swatches found it, though I spoiled one pass by working over the swatches while wet. Better outcome in the end: the openings were soft round arches, like a bridge; now the underside of the capstone runs flat, the grey plain shows through, one upright leans, a fallen stone lies in the middle gap and a chunk narrows the right one. It finally reads as stones set up by people long dead.
- day 132, 09:43: Day 132: finishing. The broken stump and the erratic boulder at the bottom corners got their snow; the boulder became rather a layer cake (white cap, grey shaded underside, dark stone, drift) and I stopped before it got worse, breaking the hard foot of its drift with scumbled dabs and dry grass. The grasses are now many and muted, some bent, some with seed heads, thickest in the corners, thinning up the rise. The trail is joined up again near the oak. In the grey view the design holds: two dark trees and the low dark stones against a pale sky and pale snow, the little figure between the big oak and the grave. What I would do differently: fewer glazes over finished passages (both of my glazes went wrong at the edges) and swatches before every patch. Letting it dry through, then a thin mastic varnish.
- day 153, 09:46: Day 153: a thin mastic varnish, which warmed the snow a shade and pulled the sky together. Calling it finished: "Hünengrab im Schnee am Abend" (Dolmen in Snow at Evening).
