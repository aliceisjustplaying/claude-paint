Across all ten studies, four recurring defects degrade the work, ordered by cost. Most damaging are razor-sharp vector edges and cutouts (the knife-edge horizon in pair3_Y, the mechanical waterline in pair4_X and the flat shadow stamp in pair2_X), which instantly destroy atmospheric recession and flatten the picture plane. Second are hairline and scratch artifacts (the steel-wool halo in pair5_Y, the comb-like grass needles in pair2_X and the spiky whiskers in pair1_Y), which betray algorithmic mark generation by defying the scale and fluid drag of actual bristle brushes. Third are dead, unmodulated silhouettes (the impenetrable foliage core in pair1_Y, the flat black ground in pair3_Y and the featureless trunks in pair5), where large dark masses lack internal values, bounce light and volume. Least damaging but still disruptive are procedural surface textures (the synthetic bump-map stipple in pair4_X and the noodle impasto in pair2_X), which apply mechanical grain across surfaces without responding to planar structure or lighting.
