- day 1, 09:00: Plan. A winter evening on the flat north-German land: a Hünengrab (dolmen) on a low snowy mound left of centre, an old stag-headed oak rising behind it with dead limbs above its living crown, the flat snowfield running off to a low misty horizon with a far village church and a line of woods; a thin crescent moon in a sky that goes from cool smalt-grey above to a pale yellow-rose glow at the horizon. One small figure seen from behind, standing in the snow looking at the stones. Crows. Ground: red-ochre knifed, then lead white brushed, pale and warm. Canvas 45 x 32 cm. Order after Friedrich: pencil drawing, thin lay-in, then thin layers and small particulars last.
- day 1, 12:49: Sky laid in four wet horizontal bands (cool smalt-grey above, neutral, rose-ochre glow at the horizon), a deeper smalt band added at the top, blended twice with the badger: smooth enough, a little mottled above like thin cloud, which I keep. Snow laid in three bands, blue-grey darkening toward the bottom, mound a shade cooler; a first broad pass left the left half thin so I restated it with the body hand and blended. Soft shadow of a drift across the foreground with a lit crest. The whole thing is pale and close in value now; the darks (oak, stones, figure) must carry the picture. Letting it dry before drawing into it.
- day 3, 15:31: Far woods: my first try at clumps read as haystacks; restated them as runs of small overlapping crowns of different heights, which reads as a wood seen through evening haze, and laid a thin lit strip of snow in front to seat them. Church tower with a steep spire and a snowy nave roof, small, in a gap between woods. The oak: drawn as a skeleton of limbs grown out by rule inside a crown envelope, then trunk and limbs filled in bark-dark, twigs by pointed round (about 4000 strokes). The first trunk read as a young tree; widened the bole and added two great elbowed low limbs. Problems I see: the crown is too even a bowl, the twigs run long and flowing like a lime rather than the short crooked spurs of an oak, and the dead limbs are far too pale, like peeled sticks, and do not rise above the living crown as a stag-head should. Letting it set before correcting.
- day 5, 06:46: Stag-head: repainted the dead limbs a darker silver-grey and carried them up above the living crown, broken at the tips; wrapped their feet in living bark so they grow out of the leader rather than float. Added about 1500 short crooked spurs along limbs and twigs, clustered at the tips as oak shoots are; the crown now reads oak rather than lime, though its outline is still a little too even. Added a few outward-reaching branches to break the bowl, then had to join them back to limbs because they floated. Dolmen blocked in: capstone on three uprights with the chamber dark between, two fallen stones; it reads like a table so far and needs modelling and snow. Spruces: first as fishbones, then filled as tiers of drooping plates; they read now. The figure: a man in a long coat and cap from behind, with a stick, standing below the mound looking up at the stones.
- day 6, 17:34: Stepping back (day 6). The oak carries the picture. The dolmen reads too much like a hut or a mushroom: the uprights stand clear with two dark door-like gaps. I will bury them to half height in a drift so the chamber is only a dark slit, and make the snow on the slab clean once the stone under it is dry (my first snow cap went into wet stone and came out dirty grey). Footprints: first as a chain of neat ovals (pills), wrong; overpainted as a trodden furrow, which reads, but as a pale streak; it wants its shadowed wall. Fence posts look like sticks on paper until snow buries their feet and caps them. Grass tufts are too few and too small. The mound front slope should be in shade, turned away from the glow, with only its crest lit. Waiting for the stones and posts to dry.
- day 13, 21:11: Accident: the mound-shade pass (a mask built from a curve that stopped at x=620) also laid a grey rectangle over the field from x 620 to 700, down to y 640. Lesson: every curve for above()/below() must span the whole canvas width. I will re-lay the whole field below the mound in fresh wet bands rather than patch a rectangle, and take the chance to deepen the near snow toward the bottom so the glow and the stones read more. Snow ridges laid along the upper sides of the level limbs of the oak; they are a touch too white, I will judge them later.
- day 15, 07:05: Re-laid the whole field below the mound in three fresh wet bands, blended; the rectangle is gone. Stippled the seam where mound meets field. Rebuilt the left flank of the mound as one clean slope with a faint lit rim. Snow on the capstone restated in clean white over the dry stone: lit crown, cooler front, an irregular overhanging lip with a broken shadow under it (my first lip was a regular scallop, like pastry). Spruces snowed: too much, their lower halves went ghostly; will put back shadowed undersides of the tiers once this sets.
- day 17, 08:27: Day 17. Footprints recomputed in perspective (eye 3.4 m, steps 0.7 m apart, closing up to a continuous line toward the man); they read as footprints now but as pale discs, stepping stones, rather than hollows. Grass tufts spread through the field were too many and too even, like a spotted rug; I veiled the middle-distance ones with a stipple of snow so they read as frosted tips poking through, and a few in front. Crescent moon, upper right, waxing, lit on its lower side toward the sun gone down. Five crows crossing toward the oak and two sitting on the dead limbs. Posts given snow caps, a snowy rail, and low drifts at their feet (first too peaked, like sand heaps, flattened). Overall the land is almost as light as the lower sky; at dusk the snow should sit in cool half-light under the glow. Planning a thin cool smalt glaze over the land, stronger toward the bottom, once all is dry.
- day 21, 08:40: Tried the cool smalt glaze on a test corner (bottom right): at medium 0.75 it went down as an opaque lavender block with swirls. Lifted it off while wet with a clean flat, wiping after every stroke, in crossing passes: it came away almost entirely. A second test at medium 0.92 and low load laid only a few starved streaks; lifted those too. I give up the idea of a single glaze over the land; the picture stays high in key, as his snow pieces often are.
- day 21, 09:49: Third glaze test: pale smalt alone with a trace of umber and white, medium 0.85, load 0.5, then blended: a gentle cool veil that darkens without turning to a coloured block. Lifted it again so the corner will not get it twice. Once the footprints and drifts are dry I will lay it over the land from the middle distance down, heavier toward the bottom edge, and blend it level. Footprints: painted the upper part of each a deeper blue-grey so they read as hollows (far wall in shade) rather than pebbles.
- day 45, 10:18: Laid the pale-smalt veil over the land in two dry passes, graded by load_at from almost nothing at the middle distance to a soft cool shade at the bottom edge, blended level. The snow now darkens toward us as the light goes, and the glow stays on the horizon and the mound. The burr on the trunk read as a button. My mid-bark mix was far too pale, lifting it left a light patch, and once dry I covered it with barkDark and carried thin bark lines through it. It now reads as plain trunk. I left the limb snow; at full size it reads as thin rime. Waited until dry, then a light varnish (0.3), which warmed and unified it. Finished. Title: "Hünengrab im Schnee am Abend" (Dolmen in Snow at Evening). What I would do differently: test every glaze small first, and mix bark tones darker than I think. The empty band of glowing sky between the crows and the far woods is the picture; I kept it empty.
