**"Contemplating the Young Moon"** — oil on linen, 950 mm, after four days at the easel.

An evening in the manner of Caspar David Friedrich: on a dark ridge crest, a man in a broad-brimmed hat with a walking stick and a hooded companion stand close together, backs to us, before a valley filling with mist. Below them a river catches the last light, a tiny church spire rises from the haze, and three blue ranges dissolve toward a snow peak still holding the afterglow. A gnarled stag-headed oak leans over the pair from the left, its dead leader bleached pale, while two comb spruces stand dark against the hills at right; high in the gray-violet sky hangs a young crescent moon with a single companion star. The whole was built Friedrich's way — warm red ground under a lead-white top, pencil underdrawing, thin stippled gradations, and the dark silhouettes laid crisp over set paint.
