# r14_p2: Evening over the flat country (a distant town after sunset)

Scratch: ~/tmp/r14-p2-ddd8e2ca

## Composition and why

A wide, low landscape, two thirds sky. The sun has just set behind a small
town on the far edge of a flat, marshy plain by a lagoon (the Pomeranian
coast country around Greifswald that Friedrich drew all his life). The
town is only a silhouette on the horizon: a tall Gothic tower with a spire,
a second, blunter church tower, a row of roofs and trees, a windmill off to
the left. A thin band of lagoon water under the glow holds the brightest
light in the land. The meadows step down in value toward us. In the right
foreground a low dark rise with dry grass carries two small figures seen
from behind, a man and a woman, standing and looking toward the town and
the glow. A thin waxing crescent hangs above the glow, its lit limb turned
toward where the sun went down.

What it draws on (from knowledge, not pictures): the Greifswald views and
meadows (a town seen as a silhouette of towers across flat land), the
evening and moon pictures (figures from behind, a crescent near the
afterglow), the Rückenfigur as the viewer's stand-in, the great empty sky
laid thin and stippled, the low horizon, symmetry avoided but the glow as
the still center. Palette: lead white, smalt, cobalt, ochres, red earth,
vermilion (a little in the glow), umber, bone black.

## Method, stage by stage

Canvas 1000 × 714 units (aspect 1.4), Style::friedrich (Dresden grounds, 1820
palette). Horizon at 468. Every look below judged on the 2400 px render,
whole and in crops.

1. **sky** (≈95 s of the 125 s render, most of it priming). A thin dead
   color first, a step darker and duller than the sky, crossed strokes;
   dried. Then the lay-in: long near-level arcs of the broad filbert, the
   color field carrying the whole sky (warm cream at the horizon under the
   glow, a greenish cream band, a breath of violet "purple light" high over
   the sun's place, smalt/cobalt gray-blue at the top) and the cloud streaks
   in the same wet paint; a second crossing pass; the badger top to bottom;
   then, still wet, the glow laid thin along the undersides of the heaviest
   low streaks with a small filbert, level; a coarse stipple into the wet
   whose every dip moves only 45% from what is
   there toward the sky wanted; dried; a fine stipple barely lighter than
   the field (+0.012 L), denser toward the glow.
2. **moon**: a young crescent, lit limb turned toward where the sun set
   (below left), horns up-right. First the ashen light: the whole disc in
   short strokes barely lighter than the sky under it (+0.014 L, judged over
   what is there). Then the crescent, a mask (disc minus offset disc) worked
   with short strokes of a small round along the limb, clipped.
2b. **birds**: six rooks far off, flying toward the town, each two wing
   strokes of a pointed round pressed at the body and lifted to the tips.
3. **far**: the far shore as a pale level strip (small round, level strokes)
   and upright hatching only where it rises into far woods; the town as one
   cool gray-violet backlit dark, a little lighter and warmer at its foot
   and a little farther into the air toward the town's ends,
   worked with a small round (upright strokes in the towers, level in the
   roofs), clipped to a silhouette built from a tabulated roofline (houses
   drawn at random as gable ends or broadsides, trees, two poplars, a Gothic
   west tower with corner pinnacles and a spire, nave with a ridge turret,
   a gate tower, a second church with a bell cap and lantern); a post mill
   on the far shore with four sails pulled out from the hub with a pointed
   round; belfry openings as four upright touches a step lighter.
4. **water**: the lagoon under the glow, level strokes of the sky mirrored
   (a step darker), the town's reflection as a soft dark; fused level with a
   small badger.
5. **land**: a thin brown dead color (clipped at the horizon), then the far
   meadows with a small filbert, the near ones with a larger, both level,
   the color field in value steps from a hazy mauve-gray at the horizon to a
   dark umber, with field bands widening toward us; then (after two hours
   on the clock) a grain of short near-level strokes a step off the paint
   under them, larger near.
6. **pools**: three flooded hollows, long level shapes laid out on the
   ground; the sky given back a step darker, the far shore's reflection as
   a dark band along their far side. Then the meadow carried back over their
   shores and the lagoon's near shore in short level strokes of its own
   color, so the water lies in the grass.
7. **grove**: three trees on the left, each crown several masses, worked in
   upright hatching; trunks; a low band of undergrowth joining them.
8. **rise**: the knoll in one dark (clipped at the crest), strokes along its
   slope, its top taking a little cool sky light; after 90 minutes, a heath
   hatching a step darker with some rust and gray-green.
9. **grass**: tufts flicked up with a pointed round, clumped along the
   skyline so the edge is made of grass, patches down the slope, bigger near,
   mostly dark blades, a few catching the sky.
10. **figures**: a man in a long coat (the `wanderer` gesture set, both feet
    down) and a woman in a high-waisted dress and shawl (`woman`), backs to
    us, heads breaking the horizon; rim light on the side toward the glow.
11. **feet**: a few blades over their feet.
12. **glaze**: a weak even umber film deepening toward the edges (Friedrich
    to Carus), wandering slowly. Then the finish: varnish, cracks with a
    narrow opening, raking light.

## FRICTION

1. **Helpers above `main` stale every checkpoint.** My geometry functions
   (town roofline, crest, pools) sit above `main`, so every edit to them made
   the sky checkpoint stale and the next run cost the full 95 s sky again.
   Workaround: wrap each helper in `// ckpt: from <stage>` … `// ckpt: end`
   and adopt the old checkpoints once with `--stale-ok --ckpt`. It works, but
   it is bookkeeping a painter shouldn't have to know; and a crop run needs
   its own set of checkpoints (`_full_crop`), so "resume a crop from my
   whole-canvas checkpoint" is impossible (`--crop … --resume grass` just
   fails with no checkpoint).
2. **No way to change the brush on a preset.** `Handling` has no `tool()`
   builder; to paint a `detail()` handling with a smaller round I had to
   write a little extension trait that sets the public field. Every painter
   will want this.
3. **`Palette::only` panics on a missing tube**, and the 1820 palette has no
   "smalt" (it's retained only in the early palette). Cost one 36 s run.
   A list of available tube names in the panic message would help (it names
   only the missing one).
4. **A glaze veil of pale paint turns milky white.** I laid an evening haze
   over the town's foot with `glaze(0.75)` of a pale warm color at load 0.55:
   the houses came out as white snow-covered cottages. Lead white scatters,
   so a "glaze" of it is a body scumble; the preset's name promised a veil.
   Removed it; the haze now lives in the town's own color field (lighter,
   warmer at its foot).
5. **Unclipped strokes overshoot a horizon.** A land dead color with crossing
   strokes (unclipped, `cross 0.4`) threw dark lumps above the horizon all
   along it, like a row of whales. `clip(true)` fixed it, at the cost of a
   stencil edge; at the horizon that is acceptable because the lagoon and the
   far shore strip cover it.
6. **A mask's zero contour draws a line.** My pool mask was
   `smoothstep(-0.3, 0.4, inside)` and outside the pools "inside" was exactly
   0 along a line, which painted needle-thin tails out of both ends of every
   pool. Obvious once found; took a zoomed crop to see.
7. **Water in a ribbon is a road.** A winding stream of even width reads as a
   path however it's colored (I tried pale, then darker with a bank
   reflection, then broken into stretches). Not an engine fault exactly, but
   nothing in the tools helps a painter make narrow water read as water;
   long level pools did it at once.
8. **Picket hedges.** Hedges drawn as rows of small upright pointed strokes
   read as fences or palisades; lumpy bands painted level read as floating
   dark leaves when broken. I dropped hedges.
10. **Dry brush lays slabs, not a broken scumble.** To make the flat meadows
    give back a little sky light I dragged a nearly empty filbert, level and
    light, over the dry meadow (load 0.18, pressure 0.25–0.4, no fill).
    Instead of catching only the tops of the grain, it laid flat opaque
    patches with clean edges, which read as snow or pale stones scattered
    over the field; even at +0.02 L and thinned they read as salt. I took the
    pass out. The bristle model's dry brush seems to break by stroke (patch
    on, patch off), not by the surface's relief within a stroke.
11. **A half tone aimed over a dark comes out pale.** Small touches of a
    warm gray a little lighter than a tree crown, aimed at `#554b45` over a
    crown of about `#35302b`, dried much lighter (near `#7a7068`) and turned
    the tree into a pale ghost. The aim seems to expect thin marks and
    overcompensates for the dark under them when the marks land thick.
    Removed; `by_masstone()` would probably have been the safer choice for
    small touches of a known paint.
12. **Thin blue over the warm ground turns olive, in soft blotches.** Once I
    made the upper sky a clearer blue, soft olive-green patches appeared all
    over it (visible whole, not only in crops). Switching the stipples off in
    a crop left them there, so they came from the lay-in: where the thinned
    blue ran thin, the orange-brown ground under it mixed optically to olive,
    and the badger spread each hole into a soft blotch. A leaner, more
    covering dead color (less medium, more coverage) and a leaner lay-in
    cleared it. To find it I had to add environment switches to my own
    program to turn passes off; there is no way to stop in the middle of a
    stage or to skip a pass from the command line.
9. **Figure helpers' hands.** The `woman` gesture paints hands in the `skin`
   paint; a normal flesh tone is the lightest thing on a backlit figure and
   read as two white dots. Painted them dark.

Final: `out/r14_p2_full.png`, 2400 × 1714, about 133 s whole. A whole render
and one resumed from "rise" came out byte-identical.

## Critique

What works: the picture is laid out as a Friedrich: two thirds sky, a low
level horizon, the glow as the still center with the great church's spire
standing in it, the land in value steps down to a dark knoll and two small
figures whose heads just break the horizon, looking where we look. The
town reads as a town at once (the spire carries it, the belfry openings make
the towers real). The young moon with its ashen light, placed off the glow's
axis with its lit limb toward the sunken sun, is right and quiet. The pools
read as standing water in flat land. The palette stays in Friedrich's range
(no green that isn't mixed, no pigment out of period).

What doesn't:
- The sky is still, at viewing distance, a smooth, correct gradient: the
  notebook's standing fault. Its grain is fine up close, but the big shape
  of it is a formula. The streaks sit in it but are soft and a little
  beaded along their length; they don't have the drawn, particular
  elegance of Friedrich's evening cloud bands.
- The town is a stenciled gray. Its edges are a mask's edges; a late pass
  put a little air toward its ends, but it is still more cut paper than
  distance.
- The meadow is a large brown field with little incident. Up close its
  grain is a mottle of short strokes rather than grass or earth; from
  across the room it's a dark carpet. That emptiness is Friedrich's, but his
  empty passages are finer and more deliberate than mine.
- The pools are all the same kind of shape, and the grove is three small
  dark lollipops.
- The figures are stock gestures from the helpers, correct but stiff; the
  birds are too even in size and spacing.
- The knoll's grass at close range is still a sprinkle in places.
