# Brief: compare six pairs of paintings

You are an experienced painting teacher. The images in this folder are six
pairs of oil paintings made with a simulated oil-paint engine (paintings
written as programs; no image model). In each pair, X and Y were painted by
exactly the same program, stroke for stroke; only the simulation of how wet
paint behaves differs between them (how paint laid into still-wet paint
mixes, drags, covers or is pushed aside). You don't know which is which;
don't guess. Look only at the images in this folder; don't open other files.

1. pair1: an evening sky over a dark horizon
2. pair2: a shore and its reflection in still water
3. pair3: a rock on grass in daylight
4. pair4: a broadleaf tree crown in summer leaf
5. pair5: a winter landscape with a boulder, a spruce wood and a stump
6. pair6: a summer valley seen from a hill with a tree, a stone and a hedge

Files: pairN_X.jpg, pairN_X_crop.jpg, pairN_Y.jpg, pairN_Y_crop.jpg: a
1000px view of the whole and one crop from a 3200px render (same window for
X and Y). Look at every image carefully, whole and crop, before writing.

The one question that matters: does it look good as a painting? Judge as a
painter would, with special attention to where one area of paint meets
another: edges (found, soft, lost), passages laid into or over other paint,
lights laid into darks, how objects meet their surroundings, and whether
the paint surface reads as paint or as something mechanical or digital.

For each pair write:
```
pairN: prefer X|Y|neither (strength: slight|clear|strong)
why: <one or two sentences, pointing to where in the image>
the loser's worst passage: <what, where>
the winner's worst passage: <what, where>
```
Then one closing paragraph: is there a consistent difference between the X
and Y "wet paint" simulations across the pairs? Describe it concretely,
noting that X and Y are shuffled independently per pair.

Write your answer to the file named in your task, then reply with the same
text. Be concrete and plain; no scores.
